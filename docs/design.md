# Game design

These decisions were made in design sessions on 2026-10-25 and 2026-10-26. Update this file when a decision changes, and give the date and reason.

## Vision
A simplified mobile extraction shooter: the risk/reward loop of Tarkov without its complexity. You gear up, go into a short raid on a small map, loot and fight AI, then either extract with what you found or die and lose it.

**Pillars**
1. **Risk vs. reward.** Whatever you bring into a raid can be lost. That tension is the game.
2. **Short and readable.** Raids are about 10–15 minutes of thorough exploration (Tarkov and Delta Force are around 45), the art is simple, and the systems are simple.
3. **Phone-first.** Every system must be usable with thumbs and run well on an iPhone.
4. **Easy to author.** Maps and items are built in in-game editors and stored as data.

## Scope
| In | Out (deliberately) | Deferred |

| Map editor, item editor (desktop) | Insurance | Water slowing movement |
| | Vibration | Temporary effects, such as painkillers (session 9) |

## Core loop and progression
- **Before a raid:** manage your stash, buy and sell with vendors, accept quests, and pick your loadout.
  - **Main menu (session 13):** the game opens on it after signing in: **Start raid**, **Stash and loadout** and **Settings**, with your name and money at the top, and **Vendors** (session 13b; quests come in 14). Your **loadout is simply the gear you're wearing**: the stash page shows the raid inventory (gear slots, pockets, rig, backpack, vault, attachment manager) beside the stash (pages of 10×30, below), with the same double-tap, drag and attachment rules. Nothing can be dropped on the ground there; anything the inventory would drop (a part taken off with no room in your grids) goes into the stash, and an action with no room in the stash either is refused. **The knife can't leave its slot.** **What's in your hands is your choice** (the user's call, 2026-10-27): the stash never takes a gun out for you, so going in with only the knife out (or only the knife at all) is fine; only if the slot in hand is emptied does the knife come out instead. The results screen's **Continue** returns to the menu.
  - **Stash pages (the user's call, 2026-10-27, Tarkov-like but for in-game money):** everyone starts with one 10×30 page and can buy up to three more, shown as numbered tabs, with a **+ Page** button that asks for a second tap. First-guess prices **100,000, 250,000 and 500,000**, kept in 13b against the item values: about one, two and a half and five starting kits' worth. A double-tap into the stash goes to the page shown, then the others; anything that would have been dropped goes to the page shown first. **Dragging an item onto a page's tab** puts it in that page wherever there's room (the tab lights green; red if it's already on that page or there's no room).
- **In the raid:** loot, fight AI, do quest objectives, and reach an extraction point.
- **Death:** you lose everything you brought or found, **except what's in your 2×3 vault** and **your knife**. Cash found in the raid is a physical item: lost on death unless it's in your vault. Your account balance is never at risk. (Since session 13b, cash you come out with goes into the balance.)
- **No insurance.** The safety net is starting equipment, weapons spawning in raids, the free knife, and the "go in with nothing, vault loot, sell it" strategy.
- **Knife:** always carried and never lost. Running is slightly faster with it out. (Session 9 gives it a gear slot. **It has an attack** (the user's call, 2026-10-26): Tarkov-style, with good damage, for raids where you bring nothing at all and for close quarters such as houses. First guesses: about 40 to the chest per hit with the usual zone multipliers (a head hit kills a scav), about 2 swings a second, 1.5 m reach, absorbed by armour like a bullet. It works when you can strike within an enemy's reaction time, by sneaking up crouched (silent) or round a corner.)
  - **Built in session 10b** with those guesses (in `content/items/knife.json`): 40 damage, a swing every 0.5 s, 1.5 m reach, a 30% bleed chance, drawn in 0.3 s. A swing lands at once (no wind-up) on the first body part within reach under the crosshair, or **within 10° of it** up, down, left or right, so it doesn't need pixel-perfect aim on a phone. Walls block it. Swinging is **silent**: enemies only notice being hit, which alerts them and makes them flinch like a shot. With **automatic fire** on, the knife **swings by itself** when an enemy is in reach under the crosshair (the user's call, 2026-10-26, matching auto-fire for guns). **"Slightly faster" is ×1.1** walking and sprinting with the knife out.

## Raids and maps
- Small maps, roughly 10–15 minutes to explore thoroughly.
- **Raid timer: 15 minutes by default**, set in the raid config. It's deliberately a little shorter than exploring the whole map, so you have to choose where to go and can't clear every corner.
- **Player spawns:** the editor places several possible spawn points, and one is picked at random each raid. Co-op players spawn together at the same point. Enemy spawns near player spawns should be avoided (a minimum distance).
- **A raid is a data config:** map + playable area + starting loadout rules + objectives + enemy spawns. Normal raids and future weekly challenges use the same format.
- Terrain is a **height grid** (smooth hills, Unturned-style). Water is a flat see-through surface with no collision; you walk on the ground under it.
- On slopes, buildings sit on a concrete base that fills the gap down to the ground.
- **Stairs and ladders** work for players. AI uses stairs and slopes, but never ladders.
- **Key rooms:** locked doors that open with key items.
- **Named zones** (for example "Science lab" or "Gas station") are drawn in the editor and used for map labels and loot weighting.
- **Loot:** containers (backpacks, desks, cabinets…) and loose item spawn points each reference a loot table ("household", "academic", "medical", "military", …). Everything is rolled once when the raid starts.
  - **Loose spawns (session 11b):** a loose spot holds **one item** from its table (the table's item count is for containers), and has a **chance** of holding anything at all (1 if not given), so loose loot isn't in the same places every raid. The small house's table spot is 0.7.
- **Quest items** spawn only for a player who has that quest accepted (in co-op, only for the quest holder).
- **Extraction (decided with the user, 2026-10-26, session 11):** Tarkov-like. Stand inside an extraction point's radius for **7 seconds**, with a countdown on screen; stepping out resets it, being shot doesn't. Every extraction point is open in every raid for now (a raid config could limit them later). **When the timer runs out you're "missing in action"**, which counts as dying: you keep only your vault and your knife.
  - Session 11 details: the countdown names the point ("Extracting at Pond dock · 4.2"). Extraction points are drawn as **faint green columns** so you can find them. **They stay now that the map tab exists** (the user's call, 2026-10-27): extracts are easy to find on a small screen, and removing them later is one line (Tarkov shows nothing). The timer is in the top left and turns red under 2 minutes.
  - **Results screen:** extracted (green), killed in action (red, with what killed you) or missing in action (orange); time in raid and kills; what you brought out, or what you kept (vault and knife) and lost. It comes up 1.5 s after a death, so you see it happen. Since session 12b it also says whether the raid is saved; since session 13 its **Continue** button goes back to the main menu.
- **Saving raids (session 12, decided with the user 2026-10-27):** the server owns the profile (money, stash, and the gear you'll take in, vault and knife included). **Starting a raid needs a connection**: the gear is then in the raid, and the profile can't be edited until the raid ends. **A raid that ends offline** is kept on the phone and sent before the next raid starts (session 12b; the results screen says "Not saved yet"); the result replaces your gear with what you kept (everything after extracting; the knife and vault after dying or going missing). **A raid that never reports** (the phone lost, or one not saved on the phone, below) counts as missing in action the next time the game opens (since session 13, so the stash can be edited; before, when the next raid started): you keep the knife and the vault as they went in.
  - **Resuming (the user's call, 2026-10-27):** iOS may unload a backgrounded home-screen app (reading a long text, say). So the raid is saved on the phone each time the game goes to the background, and the next start says "Your raid is still on" and **resumes it from that moment**; the raid clock stops while you're away (solo raids pause whenever the app is hidden, or with the settings button). Swiping the app closed resumes too, so closing it is no longer a loss. Only the phone that played it can resume it; anywhere else it counts as missing, and the next start tells you it happened. A result that turns up after that (say from another phone) is refused. **With no connection, the game shows "No connection" with Try again** (the user's call, 2026-10-27), rather than an unsaved practice raid.
- **Loot tables (session 11):** each table has a range of how many items a container gets, and items picked by weight, with a count range for stacks (cash, rounds). A container's items depend only on its table, not on the container's size; what doesn't fit is left out. **Enemies carry loot too:** an enemy type lists tables with weights and how many items, and an enemy spawn's loot weights multiply those (the yard boss doubles military). The first tables are household, medical and military; the numbers are first guesses.

## Movement (decided 2026-10-26, session 3)
- **Walk, sprint, crouch, jump and ladders.** Sprinting only works forwards, and it stands you up. You can't jump while crouched.
- **Crouching** lowers the capsule from 1.8 m to 1.2 m (eye 1.6 → 1.0 m) and slows you down. You can't stand up under something low.
- **Steps up to 0.45 m** are walked onto (kerbs, single stairs). A jump (0.6 m) plus that step lets you **mantle onto ledges about 1 m high**. Taller walls need stairs or a ladder. The editor's "unreachable spot" overlay must count jump-and-mantle as player movement.
- **Little air control:** you can speed up along the stick in the air, up to running speed, but never brake, so sprint jumps keep their momentum.
- **Ladders:** push towards the wall to climb up and away from it to climb down, whichever way you're looking. You grab one by walking into it, or by walking off its top edge. At the top you step over onto the surface behind it. Jump lets go.
- **Steep terrain** (over 45°) can't be walked up, but you can walk down it.
- Speeds: walk 3.5, sprint 6, crouch 1.8 and climb 2 m/s. All the tuning is in `MOVEMENT` in `packages/shared/src/sim/movement.ts`.

## Combat
- **Hitscan** (bullets travel instantly, as a raycast). No bullet travel time or drop.
- **Collision** is simple: boxes (rotated about the vertical axis only), the terrain height grid, and ramps for stairs. No physics engine.
- **Weapon attachments** use fixed slots, like Battlefield 4 or Black Ops 2: **optic, muzzle, magazine**, with underbarrel or laser possible later. Each gun defines its slots and where they sit on the model; each attachment says which slot type it fits.
  - **Fit (decided 2026-10-26, session 6b):** an attachment fits every gun with its slot, unless it lists the guns it fits. Optics and muzzles usually fit anything; magazines list their gun (a 45-round 5.56 mag fits only the rifle).
  - **Stat changes** multiply the gun's spread (hip and aimed), recoil, aim time, reload time and loudness; a magazine adds rounds. All the attachments' changes stack.
  - **Changing the magazine** puts rounds that no longer fit back with your spare ammo, and stops a reload in progress.
- **Magazines:** one magazine attachment per gun (for example, "extended mag" raises capacity). Reloading takes from the ammo you carry. No individual mags, no loading rounds.
- **Suppressors** reduce a shot's hearing radius (see AI). Session 6b: every gun has a `loudness` (how far away a shot is heard, in metres; rifle 180, pistol 120), and each shot carries it for AI hearing. The suppressor multiplies it by 0.3 and hides the muzzle flash. A muzzle brake is the opposite trade: 30% less recoil, 20% louder.
- **Laser:** a short see-through red line plus stat changes. No collision, and the AI doesn't notice it.
- **Firing (session 6):** weapons are `auto` (fire while held) or `semi` (once per pull), with a rate in rounds per minute. Shots stray randomly within a cone: wide from the hip, tight when aimed; aiming in takes the gun's aim time. Each shot kicks the view up (and a random bit sideways), and the view settles back by itself; the kick is the simulation's, so co-op sees the same. **Firing or aiming stops a sprint**, and firing ends sprint lock as aiming does. You can't fire on a ladder or while reloading. Pulling the trigger on an empty gun starts a reload. All weapon stats are in the item files (`content/items`).
- **Aiming:** magnified scopes use full-screen zoom (narrower field of view, a scope mask and a reticle), like other mobile shooters. Red dots (Coyote-style) have no zoom, just a reticle. Reticles and zoom levels are set in the item editor. Each gun model defines a sight line so the reticle lines up with the camera when aiming.
  - Session 6b: a scope's view **snaps to its zoom behind the mask at 90% aimed** (before that it zooms like iron sights), and the gun is hidden. Looking turns the view more slowly by the same factor, so it moves as fast on the screen. A red dot's reticle shows at 95% aimed, when its frame has come up to the eye. Behind a reticle the crosshair dot hides but still flashes for hit markers.
  - Reticles are drawn on the screen, not in 3D, so they stay sharp and readable at any distance. Their size is in % of the screen height, not angles: a real 2 MOA dot would be a fraction of a pixel on a phone.

## Damage, armour and health
- **Hit zones:** head, chest, left arm, right arm, left leg, right leg. Each has its own HP pool and damage multiplier. Session 6 values: HP 35, 85, 60, 60, 65, 65 (370 in all); shot multipliers 1.5, 1, 0.7, 0.7, 0.75, 0.75. Damage per shot is the ammo's. All tuning is in `HEALTH` in `packages/shared/src/sim/health.ts`.
- **Hit zones are a fixed capsule layout** (decided 2026-10-26, session 6), worked out from the character's position, yaw and crouch, not attached to animated bones. That keeps hits in the simulation, the same on a co-op server with no renderer. The drawn body (session 8b) is posed to match.
  - **Arms are held forward** (decided with the user, 2026-10-26, session 8b): every armed pose has both arms out in front, so the arm capsules run from the shoulders down to the elbows and in to the hands in front of the chest. From the front, arms now shield part of the chest (×0.7 instead of ×1). A sprinting or unarmed body doesn't quite match. The chest became two narrower capsules side by side, so it's wider than deep like the drawn torso and doesn't swallow shots at the forearms.
  - AI still aims at 0.6 of body height (about 1.08 m standing), just under the forearms, so some of its shots now land on arms. Retune if fights feel softer.
- **Armour:** helmets cover the head, chest armour covers the chest. Armour **absorbs a percentage** of damage (set per armour piece) and loses durability as it does; at 0 durability it absorbs nothing. Armour can be repaired with armour repair kits.
  - Session 6: it absorbs its **full percentage until it breaks** (no falloff as it wears), and loses durability equal to what it absorbed times the ammo's `armourDamage`. Simple and readable: the colour warns you it's close to breaking.
- **A limb at 0 HP is "broken"** (there's only one state):
  - Legs: slower movement.
  - Arms: more weapon sway and slower aiming.
  - **Damage to a broken limb spills over to the chest** at a reduced amount (about 70%, tunable).
  - **So does the rest of the hit that breaks it** (session 6): a 40 damage hit on a leg with 10 HP left breaks it and sends 30 × 70% to the chest. Without this, huge hits and falls to the legs could never kill.
  - Session 6 values: each broken leg multiplies speed by 0.75; each broken arm multiplies spread by 1.6 and aim time by 1.5.
- **Head or chest at 0 HP = death.**
- **Bleeding:** heavy hits can cause bleeding, which slowly drains HP until a bandage stops it.
  - Session 6: each ammo type has a **bleed chance** per hit that gets through armour. A bleeding zone loses 1 HP per second (through the same spillover rules), so bleeding out of a broken limb drains the chest.
- **Fall damage** (session 6, retuned 2026-10-26 because the user found it hurt too soon): landing faster than 12.5 m/s (a drop of about 4 m, so one-storey roofs are safe) hurts both legs by 15 per m/s over that, each. About 5 m bruises, about 7 m breaks both legs, and above about 11 m the spillover kills. A jump never hurts. (It was 10 m/s and 12 per m/s: hurt from about 2.5 m.)
- **Treatment items:**
  - Splint: fast and cheap. It removes the broken effect, but the limb stays at 0 HP and spillover still applies.
  - Surgery kit: slow and rare. It brings a broken limb back to 1 HP.
  - Medkit: has an HP pool and heals HP.
  - Bandage: single use, stops bleeding.
  - Items target limbs in a **fixed priority order: head, chest, left leg, right leg, left arm, right arm.** There's no body diagram.
  - Session 9: each use of a bandage, splint or surgery kit treats **one zone**, the first in that order that needs it (a splint can have several uses). A medkit heals down the order from its pool, **skipping broken limbs** (they need surgery first), and keeps what's left of its pool for next time. A splinted limb stays at 0 HP and still spills damage to the chest; surgery clears the splint.
- **Using meds: one heal button, not quick slots** (decided with the user, 2026-10-26, session 9, after Delta Force; it replaces the 2–4 quick-use slots). The button always shows the one treatment that fits your state, in this order: **bleeding → bandage; broken limb → splint, or surgery if you have no splint; lost HP → medkit.** Bleeding comes first because it's the only problem that gets worse by itself; a splint comes before surgery because surgery is slow and rare. A **long press** opens a small picker with every carried med that would help (for example surgery on purpose while safe), and a tap uses the default. With nothing wrong, the button is greyed out. Meds come from pockets, rig and backpack, **never the vault**. Tap to start; a progress ring shows the timer. You can walk slowly while healing; firing or sprinting cancels it (session 10). The use time, HP pool and effects are set per item in the item editor.
  - **Session 10b:** each tap is **one use** (one bandaged zone, one splint, one medkit use from its pool), taking the med's use time; then the button offers what's needed next. While using, you walk at most 1.5 m/s and the gun is lowered (no aiming). **Firing, sprinting, reloading, switching weapons, climbing a ladder, or tapping the heal button again** stops it, and nothing is used. **Automatic fire never fires while you're using a med**, so passing the crosshair over an enemy doesn't cancel a heal; only the fire button does. If the med moved in your inventory meanwhile, the same kind of med is used from wherever it is now.
  - **Painkillers are punted** (the user's call): there's no pain mechanic for them to fix, since the splint already removes broken-limb effects. The user wants **temporary effects** later, and painkillers would come with them.
- **HUD:** one overall health bar (sum of all limbs) plus status icons for broken limbs and bleeding. Armour icons change colour by durability: **yellow** when damaged, **orange** when low, **red** when broken.
  - Session 6: damaged is below 75% durability, low below 35%. The health bar, armour icons and status chips are at the top centre, the ammo count (with a reload bar) at the top right. The crosshair flashes red on a hit and grows on a kill; the screen edges flash red when you're hurt.
- **AI uses the same hit zones and limb system**, with only the leg effect (broken legs slow it down).

## AI
- **Awareness states:** unaware → suspicious (heard or glimpsed something) → alert (knows where you are) → lost track (searches, then goes back to patrolling).
- **Hearing:** each shot has a hearing radius; suppressors shrink it.
- **Unreachable player** (alert, but there's no path to you): take cover out of your line of sight, hold angles on the ways down, patrol nearby, and give up after a while. No grenades for now; if they're added later, one grenade "token" is shared across all AI in the area so many don't throw at once, and AI can't damage itself.
- **Bosses** are enemy types with stronger stats and loadouts.
- **Enemy spawn points** (set in the editor) define the enemy type, a loadout roll, and loot table weighting (for example, a lab boss drops more rare science and medical loot).
- **Navigation** is generated automatically from the map data (see the editor's navigation overlay).

**Session 7 decisions (2026-10-26).** All the tuning is in `AI` in `packages/shared/src/sim/ai.ts`; the numbers are first guesses to tune in play.
- **Patrols** (chosen by the user): a `patrol` node in a map or prefab is a named route, and an enemy spawn names one with `patrol`. A spawn without one **guards**: it stands at its spawn and looks a new way every few seconds, within 70° of the spawn's direction.
  - **The route is authored, not the shortest path** (asked for by the user): a point is either a **stop** (stand and look around for its own `wait`, 3 s by default) or a plain position the enemy **walks through** without slowing. Between consecutive points it takes the shortest way, so through-points shape the route (round the back of a house rather than across the yard).
  - **Areas, not spots:** a stop can have a `radius`, and each visit goes to a random walkable spot within it; an enemy spawn can have a `radius` too. The same patrol looks different from raid to raid.
  - **Way in, then repeat:** points before `repeatFrom` are walked once from the spawn; the rest repeat, in a loop or back and forth. For example: spawn in an area, walk a set route to area B, wait, walk on to area C, wait, then go back and forth between B and C.
  - After an alarm it picks the patrol up again at the nearest point.
- **Sight:** a 120° field of view out to 70 m, blocked by walls; you're seen if your head or the middle of your body is. Noticing builds up over time rather than being instant. Someone standing in the open within 8 m is noticed in about half a second, and it gets slower with distance (about 6 s at 70 m) and towards the edge of view. Crouching (×0.6) and standing still (×0.7) make you harder to notice, sprinting easier (×1.3), and an enemy already on edge notices faster (×1.6). Inside 2.5 m in view it's instant. A glimpse (30% noticed) makes it suspicious; full makes it alert.
- **Hearing shots, tiered** (chosen by the user, because the rifle is heard 180 m away on a 192 m map): within the shot's loudness, an enemy is suspicious and turns to look; within half of it, it walks over to investigate; within 15%, it's alert. Suppressors shrink every tier. A bullet passing or landing within 3 m, or a hit, makes it alert to the shooter wherever they are. A heard noise is placed with an error of up to 10% of its distance (at most 8 m).
- **Footsteps** (chosen by the user): sprinting is heard 15 m away and walking 6 m; crouch-walking and standing still are silent. Footsteps make an enemy suspicious, never alert on their own.
- **Awareness in practice:** suspicious → turns to look for 1.5 s, then walks over if it's worth checking (a glimpse, a nearby shot or footsteps), looks around there for 5 s, and goes back to its patrol. Alert → knows where you are while it can see you, and for 1 s after losing sight (it saw which way you went); then it fights as in session 8 below, and when it has lost you it's **searching**: it goes to where it last saw you, then checks 3 random spots within 12 m, and calms down. An unarmed enemy faces you, closes to 10 m and holds.
- Enemies' own shots: see session 8 below.
- **Bodies push each other apart** (player and enemies alike); the dead don't.

**Session 8 decisions (2026-10-26): combat.** Tuning is in `AI` and in the enemy type files; the numbers are first guesses to tune in play.
- **Enemy types are data** (`content/enemies/<id>.json`): a name, a default loadout, and a **skill**: reaction time, how far its aim wanders when it first sees you and once settled, how fast it settles, and how likely it is to hold an angle rather than push. Bosses are types with better skill and a better loadout, not more HP (the HUD and healing assume the same zone HP for everyone). The scav reacts in 0.7 s and settles to 0.7°; the yard boss reacts in 0.4 s, settles to 0.35° and holds angles more.
- **Loadouts** (`content/loadouts/<id>.json`) are rolled when an enemy spawns: the weapon, helmet and body armour are each picked by weight on their own, with a spare-rounds range, attachments that each go on by chance, and armour that can be worn (a condition range). An enemy spawn's `loadout` replaces its type's. Scavs carry a pistol or a rifle, sometimes a helmet or plate carrier, worn; the boss always has a rifle with a red dot or scope, helmet and plates.
- **Aim (chosen by the user: "settling aim error"):** an enemy waits its reaction time before its first shot, then its aim wanders around you (smoothly, not random per shot) by an error that shrinks the longer it watches you. A target moving fast and its own movement keep the error up, and being hit makes it flinch (+3°). Seeing you again **where it last saw you** takes only a 0.15 s reaction; seeing you **somewhere else** (2 m or more) after over a second takes its full reaction, with its aim off by about how far you went. So popping back out of the same cover is dangerous, and repositioning is how you win. It aims at your chest, or at your head when only that shows over cover, and pulls down half its recoil.
- **Firing:** automatics in bursts (3–6 rounds close, 2–3 beyond 15 m), semi-automatics in single shots, with pauses between that grow with distance. It aims down the sights beyond 12 m. **It never fires when another enemy is in the line of fire**; if its spread hits one anyway, that does damage but doesn't turn them on each other.
- **Fighting in the open:** it closes in until within 30% of its gun's range (at most 45 m), then sidesteps left and right at half pace. After 3–6 s in the open, when it's hit, or when its magazine is empty, it looks for **cover**: a reachable spot within 10 m where you can't see it crouched (with a 0.4 m margin either side), at least 4 m from you, not taken by another enemy. It reloads there. Over **low cover** it crouches and peeks by standing up; behind full cover it peeks by walking back to where it was fighting from. If you can see it in its cover, it gives up on that cover.
- **Losing sight (reachable):** it either **pushes** to where it last saw you or **holds the angle** (by its type's chance, and only if it can see that spot), then searches: after 4 s pushing or 8 s holding.
- **Out of reach** (you're somewhere it can't walk to, like a roof reached by ladder): while it sees you it fights as usual. Once it loses sight it finds the **way up** (the closest spot it can reach), moves to a spot within 10 m that's hidden from you and watches the way up, aimed, for 8 s, then moves to another spot within 8 m of it and holds again. **It gives up after 45 s without seeing you** and goes back to its patrol.
- **Allies' shots (chosen by the user: "join the fight"):** an enemy that hears another enemy firing joins in: within 15% of the shot's loudness it's alert to whoever its ally is fighting; within half it walks over to investigate there. Further away, or a shot from an enemy that isn't fighting anyone, it ignores. Suppressed enemies pull in fewer friends.
- **The dead stay down** (bodies for session 11's looting). The test page's `?respawn` brings them back for testing.
- **Looting bodies and loot weighting moved to session 11** (the user's call): they need items, inventory and loot tables (sessions 9–11).

- **Doors (decided 2026-10-26, for session 11, now 11c):** AI opens doors. A door is **open or closed in the simulation, with nothing in between**: opening one changes its collision at once, for players and AI alike, and the renderer animates the swing (about 0.2 s) for looks only, so nothing can get caught on a half-open door, and co-op only syncs one flag per door. A closed, unlocked door stays a link in the navigation grid, so paths go through it and an enemy opens it when it gets there. **Opening a door makes no noise** for AI, for now (the user's call). **Enemies never open locked doors**, even carrying the key (the user's call, 2026-10-26). But **unlocking is for the rest of the raid**: once a player unlocks a door, it's an ordinary door that anyone can open, enemies included. So a key room is safe only until you open it; after that, enemies can follow you in.
  - **Decided with the user in session 11c (2026-10-27):** an **open door is solid too**: it snaps to 90° from its hinge, on the side it opens towards, and stops bodies, shots and sight like a wall (no shooting or seeing past a door either way). Opening one into someone pushes them aside. **Keys have limited uses** (`uses` on the key item; left out, it never wears out), and are gone after the last; the key counts anywhere you carry it, the vault included, and the most worn one is used first. A locked door with its key carried unlocks and opens in one press. **Only players close doors**, and not on anyone standing in the doorway; enemies open doors in their way and leave them open. An unlocked door never locks again.
  - The use button says **open**, **close**, **unlock** or **locked** at a door under the crosshair within 2 m; a locked one names its key under the crosshair ("Locked · needs Shed key" or "Unlock with Shed key"). Looking through an open door's doorway counts as aiming at the door, so it can be closed with its slab off to the side, but anything else in reach comes first. A key's uses left show on its inventory tile (×n).

## Inventory and UI
- **A grid inventory with item sizes** (Tarkov-style), designed for touch:
  - Auto-placement puts an item in the first free space. Items can be rotated.
  - **Double-tap** moves an item between your inventory and the open container (or stash), fitting it automatically.
- Backpacks and rigs have their own grids. **No containers inside containers.**
- **Gear slots (session 9, decided with the user 2026-10-26):** two primary guns (any gun), a sidearm (pistols only), the knife, helmet, body armour, a rig and a backpack. What's in your hands is one of the two primaries, the sidearm or the knife.
- **Switching (session 10b, chosen by the user 2026-10-26): four slot buttons**, one per hand slot, where the quick slots were: tap one to take it out (keys 1–4 on desktop). Empty slots are dimmed and the one in hand is outlined. **Drawing takes the item's draw time** (rifle 0.8 s, pistol 0.4 s, knife 0.3 s), with the gun coming up from below; until it's drawn you can't fire, swing, aim or reload. Switching stops a reload and a med being used.
- **Pockets** (decided with the user): a 4×1 grid everyone always has, lost on death like the rest. A player with no rig or backpack can still carry a few rounds and a bandage, and enemies' spare rounds go there, so their bodies have something to loot.
- **The vault is part of the in-raid inventory** (2×3), kept on death. Players only.
- **Ammo is loose rounds in stacks** in your grids (60 per cell for the current rounds). Reloading takes the gun's rounds from pockets, then rig, then backpack, **never the vault**, so the vault stays a place to keep things. Rounds that no longer fit when a bigger magazine comes off go back into what you carry, and **are dropped on the ground if there's no room** (session 11b; until then such a change was refused).
- Session 9 details: auto-placement goes row by row from the top left, trying the item unrotated everywhere before rotated. A stack first tops up stacks of the same item, then takes new cells a full stack at a time. Dropping a stack on a stack of the same item merges as much as fits. **No nesting** means a rig or backpack can't go in pockets, the vault, or another rig or backpack, even empty; only the stash and loot containers (desks, crates, a body's gear) take them. A gun's size doesn't change with attachments.
- **The raid keeps running while the in-raid screen is open** (decided with the user, 2026-10-26, session 10): you stand still and can be shot, as in Tarkov, and co-op works the same way. The red hurt flash and the health bar show over it.
- **Looting (session 10):** the use button turns into **loot** when a container or dead body is within 2 m and you're facing it, and opens it beside your inventory. A **bag button** opens just your inventory. **Double-tap** sends your item to what's open, or theirs to you; with nothing open, it equips an item from your grids into an empty gear slot, or stows a gear item into what you carry. Taking a gun fills an empty gun slot first (a pistol tries the sidearm first), then goes into your grids. **Dropping on a gear slot swaps** with what's there, if that can go where the dragged item came from. **While dragging, a second finger turns the item** (R on desktop).
- **The in-raid screen has tabs:**
  - **Inventory:** includes looting containers and bodies, plus the attachment manager.
  - **Map:** a simple SVG generated from map data, showing building outlines, named zones and **your own position**.
    - Session 11b: looking straight down with −z up, everything that stands is drawn as its footprint in its own colour, highest on top (so buildings show as roofs, trees as dots), with water, named zones (dashed, named), extraction points (green circles, named) and you as a yellow arrow. **"Near me"** zooms to 60 m around you and "Whole map" back; labels stay the same size on screen, and a zone's name that would run into another's is left out until you zoom in. There's no pinch zoom (the game blocks pinching).
  - **Quests.**
- **Weapon attachments** can be managed both in the main-menu stash and during a raid.
- **The attachment manager (session 10c)** is in the bar along the bottom of the inventory: select a gun (yours, a body's or one in a container) and its optic, muzzle and magazine slots appear there. **Drop an attachment on a slot, or straight on the gun** wherever it lies (a grid or a gear slot, outlined green when it fits; added at the user's request, 2026-10-26) to fit it; **drag a fitted one off** to a spot in a grid, or **onto another gun** it fits (what that gun had in the slot goes onto the first gun if it fits there, a swap, else into what you carry; added at the user's request, 2026-10-26); **tap a slot** for a picker of the attachments you can reach that fit it (yours first, then the open body's or container's), plus "Take off"; **double-tap a fitted one** to take it off into what you carry. Fitting over an existing attachment swaps them: the old one goes where the new one was. Changing attachments is instant, like any inventory move, but stops a reload if it's the magazine.
- **Items on the ground (session 11b, the ground panel chosen with the user 2026-10-27):**
  - **Picking up:** look at an item within 2 m (the crosshair within about 20° of it) and the use button says **take**, the item is drawn lighter and its name shows under the crosshair. A tap takes it as a double-tap would: into an empty gear slot it fits (a gun into a gun slot), else into what you carry; a stack that only partly fits leaves the rest. With no room, "No room for …" shows under the crosshair for 1.5 s. Aiming at an item beats a container or body nearby.
  - **The ground panel:** with nothing open, the right column of the inventory is **On the ground**: everything within reach, as tiles. **Drag** one of yours there (or select it and press **Drop**) to drop it; drag a tile into a grid or onto a gear slot, or **double-tap** it, to take it. Dropping a ground item on a gear slot swaps them: what was in the slot goes on the ground in its place. So a full bag is sorted out without closing the screen.
  - **Dropping** puts an item 0.9 m in front of you (where you can see it past the gun), or at your feet if a wall is closer, with a little scatter, on the surface below (so off a ladder it falls to the ground).
  - **What has nowhere to go is dropped, not refused:** the rounds a smaller magazine hands back, an attachment swapped off a gun, and one taken off "into what you carry". Taking one off to a spot that doesn't fit is still refused.
  - Items on the ground are drawn with their models (session 18b): guns, knives and magazines lie on their side, worn gear on its back.
- The inventory UI's usability on a phone is an explicit design goal. Prototype it on a real phone before polishing.

## Quests and vendors
- **Quest types:**
  - **Investigate:** a marker visible through walls, with a live distance readout. Go to it and tap "Investigate".
    - Session 14b: a yellow diamond with the distance in metres, for each of your active quests' markers on the map not yet investigated; one off screen sits at the screen's edge in its direction. Within **2.5 m** the use button says **investigate** (beating a container or body, but not an item or door under the crosshair) and the objective's text shows under the crosshair; a tap ticks it ("Investigated · …") and the marker goes.
  - **Extract with an item:** a quest item that spawns only while the quest is accepted.
    - Session 14b (decided with the user 2026-10-27): the objective lists **its own spots**, quest markers on its map, and the item lies on the ground at **one picked at random each raid**. Spots are **not shown** on screen: the objective's text hints where ("by the car, or in the locked shed"), so finding it is the job. It counts if you **extract with it, or keep it in your vault** after dying (as in Tarkov). **The server takes every quest item** out of the gear you keep when the raid ends, wanted or not, so quest items never reach the stash. Only one quest may want a given quest item.
  - **In the raid** (14b): the raid screen's **Quests** tab lists your active quests with each objective's standing this raid ("Marked on screen", "Investigated", "Not found yet", "Got it: now get out", "Not on this map", hand-overs "from your stash"). The **results screen** lists the objectives the raid finished, and leaves quest items out of what you brought out.
  - **Hand over** (session 14): a normal item is handed over **from the stash** between raids, not by extracting with it (the user's call, 2026-10-27), so a gold watch already in the stash counts.
  - More types can be added as data.
- **Quests between raids (session 14, decided with the user 2026-10-27):**
  - Each vendor gives a **chain** of quests: a quest lists the ones that must be done first, and opens when they are. A quest with nothing before it is open from the start.
  - The **Quests** page (from the menu) has a tab per vendor with quests, as cards: what needs handing in first, then active, new and locked ones (dimmed, with what they come after); done ones in one line at the bottom. A vendor's tab shows a count of quests ready to hand in; the menu's button says how many are ready, active and new.
  - **Accept** takes one tap. **Hand over** takes as many of the item as the objective still needs from the stash pages and the bags in them, never from what you wear or carry, and only plain items (a bag with nothing in it, a gun with nothing fitted); a second tap confirms, since they're gone. Handing over can be done a bit at a time.
  - Once every objective is done, **Hand in** gives the rewards: money, and items into the stash (the page last shown first). With no room for them nothing happens and it says so, so a reward is never lost.
  - **Investigating counts even if you die** later in that raid (the user's call, 2026-10-27); a quest item you carried is lost like anything else. Both happen in raids (session 14b).
- **Vendors** buy and sell, always stock cheap basic gear, give quests, and sell armour repair kits.
- **Economy (session 13b, decided with the user 2026-10-27):**
  - **Every item has a value** (in its item file). Vendors sell new items for their value and **buy for 50% of it** (`VENDOR.sellShare`), scaled by condition (armour's durability, a med's uses or HP left, a repair kit's points, a key's uses); a gun sells with its attachments and loaded rounds. So selling always loses money and nothing can be bought and sold back for a profit. Items worth 0 (none yet) can't be sold, nor a rig or backpack with anything in it, nor the knife.
  - **Unlimited stock**: money is the only limit. Each vendor is a content file listing what it sells (rounds come 30 at a time). Two to start: the **Quartermaster** (pistol, assault rifle, 9 mm and 5.56 rounds, the 24-round pistol magazine, red dot, muzzle brake, steel helmet, chest rig, small backpack) and the **Medic** (bandage, splint, medkit, surgery kit, armour repair kit). The plate carrier, scope, suppressor, large backpack and 45-round magazine are only found in raids.
  - **Cash is money**: whatever cash a raid brings out (everything after extracting, the vault's after dying or going missing) goes into your balance **at its full value** when the server takes the result; the results screen says how much. It can't be taken back out as cash. Cash already in a stash from before can be sold at full value.
  - **Where:** the **Vendors** page (from the menu, or the stash's top bar) shows each vendor's offers as rows with their price; a tap, then a second tap ("Buy for 12,000?"), buys into the stash, the page shown first. **Selling is done in the stash**: select any item of yours (gear, carried, vault or stash) and press **Sell · price**, then confirm. Every vendor pays the same, so a sell screen per vendor would only add steps. **Several at once** (the user's request, same day): **Select to sell** (with nothing selected) or **Sell several** (beside Sell, starting with that item) makes taps pick and unpick items, outlined green, with the count and total in the bar; **Sell N** and a second tap sells them all in one request, or none if one can't go (a backpack can go with everything in it picked too). Drags do nothing meanwhile; **Cancel** leaves. A slower second tap on the selected item now lets go of it. The stash's top bar shows your money.
  - **First-guess values** (cash is 1): assault rifle 24,000, pistol 6,000; 5.56 rounds 40 each, 9 mm 20; plate carrier 30,000, steel helmet 12,000; 4× scope 18,000, suppressor 14,000, red dot 5,000, muzzle brake 4,000, 45-round mag 6,000, 24-round mag 2,500; medkit 4,000, surgery kit 9,000, splint 600, bandage 400; large backpack 20,000, chest rig 6,000, small backpack 5,000; gold watch 15,000, shed key 8,000; armour repair kit 12,000. The starting kit is worth about 94,000.
- **Armour repair kits (session 13b):** select a piece of armour of yours and press **Repair**: the first kit with points left mends it **back to full durability** (no wear on the most it can have), spending that many points; a spent kit is gone. **Between raids it's at once**, with a kit anywhere in your gear or stash. **In a raid it takes the kit's use time (8 s)**, with a kit you carry (pockets, rig or backpack, not the vault, as for meds), and works like a med: walking is slowed, and firing, reloading, sprinting or climbing stops it (nothing is spent); the heal button shows its progress and cancels it. **Dragging a kit onto armour** does the same as Repair with that kit (the armour lights green if it can). **While a med or repair is in use, a pill in the raid screen's top bar** shows its name, the seconds left and a bar filling up; **tapping it stops it** (the user's request, same day). A selected med you carry also has **Use** in the inventory bar (or "Nothing to treat"), the same as picking it from the heal button. A kit is priced so that **repairing never makes money**: a point costs more than it adds to the armour's sell price (a content test checks every kit against every armour).

## Controls and platform behaviour
- A standard twin-stick touch layout. **The layout can be adjusted in settings and is saved.**
- **Aim and fire behaviour** are chosen in settings (hold vs. toggle to aim, fire options). Default: hold, with automatic fire.
  - **"Automatic fire" means auto-fire on target** (clarified 2026-10-26, session 5): the gun fires by itself while the crosshair is on an enemy, like CoD Mobile's simple mode. The other option is "fire button only". The fire button works in both modes.
  - Session 6: "on an enemy" means the crosshair ray (with recoil) hits a living character's hit zone within the gun's range, with no wall in the way. With a semi-automatic gun, auto-fire pulls the trigger as fast as the gun allows.
- **Aim assist:** aim slows slightly when passing over an enemy. It's a setting, defaulting to low. Session 6: look speed × 0.8 (low), 0.6 (medium) or 0.45 (high) while the crosshair is on an enemy.
- **Touch layout (session 5):** the move stick floats where the left thumb lands on the left half; dragging on the right half looks. **The fire button also looks:** dragging the thumb on it turns the view, so you can track a target while shooting. Buttons: fire (it says "knife" with the knife out), aim, reload, use (interact), bag, jump, crouch (toggle), the four hand slots and heal (session 10b, replacing quick slots 1–4).
- **Sprint (decided 2026-10-26, session 5):** pushing the stick to its rim (forwards) sprints. Dragging on up into the "sprint" zone above the stick **locks sprinting**, so the thumb can let go; with a thumb on the stick while locked, left and right steer. **It unlocks** when you pull the stick back, tap the left half, aim, crouch, or jump off a ladder. Aiming stops sprinting, and sprinting turns crouch off.
- **Desktop works too** (the user's wish, 2026-10-26): every touch action has a keyboard and mouse equivalent, so the game can be played and tested at a desk. Currently: WASD, Shift, Space, C, F, Q, R, E use or loot, I or Tab the bag, 1–4 the hand slots, H heal and Shift+H its picker, Esc closes the raid screen, mouse drag to look; in the inventory, click, double-click and drag, with R to turn an item while dragging.
- **Landscape only:** a "rotate your phone (and turn off rotation lock)" overlay shows whenever the phone is in portrait, and the game pauses.
- **Safari workarounds:** unlock audio on the first tap, run in standalone mode from the home screen (no browser UI), and disable pinch and double-tap zoom with CSS and the viewport settings. **Zoom (2026-10-26):** iOS ignores `user-scalable=no` and a double tap still zoomed the game on the phone, so `app/no-zoom.ts` also cancels Safari's pinch gesture events and a second `touchend` within 350 ms. It blocks nothing while the page is zoomed in, so you can always pinch back out.

## Art direction
- Unturned-like: low-poly, solid flat colours, **no real lighting**.
- For readability, each face direction gets a slightly different shade (tops lighter, sides darker), plus **distance fog** (which also lets far-away objects go undrawn).
- Items and props are built from simple shapes (boxes, cylinders) in the editors.

## Trust model
A sandbox. The client is trusted. The server only applies sanity limits (for example, money can't jump from 20 to 9 trillion; item counts and values stay within reasonable bounds). No raid replay or loot validation. Revisit only if ranked challenges or friend trading get abused.
- **The stash is saved whole** (session 13): each change is applied on the phone and the profile sent a moment later, through the same limits as any edit between raids. If another device changed the profile meanwhile, or the server refuses the change, **the server's copy wins** and the stash reloads with a note saying so (unsaved changes on this phone are lost). With no connection the stash says "Not saved", and a raid can't start until it is.
- **Session 12's limits:** every item must exist, be within its stack, durability, rounds and uses, and fit where it is (its gear slot, or inside its grid with no overlaps and no containers in containers); pockets, vault and stash are their usual sizes; money is at most 1 billion. A raid's result may report at most the raid's time plus a minute, and up to 1,000 kills. **Edits between raids can't make items or change money:** there must be no more of any item than before (attachments on guns count), and only vendors (on the server, session 13b) change money. **Session 13b:** a stash edit can't mend armour by more durability than repair kits lost points (`overRepaired`). There's still no value limit on what a raid brings out: grid space bounds it (a raid can carry out a few hundred thousand in cash at most).
- **Accounts (session 12, the user's call 2026-10-27): username and password.** Passkeys and magic links were the alternatives (passkeys need HTTPS even in dev; magic links need an email sender and our own domain). No password reset without email: reset one by hand with `wrangler d1 execute`. Registering is open to anyone (no invite code, the user's call 2026-10-27).

## Characters (confirmed 2026-10-26)
- **No blocky cube people.** The base body is a rigged, animated **low-poly humanoid from a CC0 asset pack** (for example Quaternius), recoloured to flat colours to match the art style. No 3D modelling is needed from the user.
- **What you see on an enemy is what they carry:** helmets, armour, backpacks and weapons are the same models built in the item editor, attached to the body. You can read how dangerous an enemy is, and roughly what they'll drop, before you engage.
- **Factions and bosses** are told apart by uniform colours and gear, and bosses also by size or a distinctive item.
- **The player in first person** sees only arms and the gun. The full body is the same character system, which a co-op partner sees.
- **Animations** come from the pack: idle, walk, run, aim, shoot, reload, death. No ragdolls.
- **Session 8b (2026-10-26):** the pack is Quaternius's **Universal Animation Library (Standard)**, CC0 (chosen with the user). It's one mannequin with 45 clips, including pistol idle, aim, shoot and reload, but **no rifle clips**. So a gun is held by IK: the gun sits on a weapon bone in front of the right shoulder, from the low ready (tipped down) to aimed (shouldered) as the character aims, turned with where it looks, and both hands reach for it. While reloading or sprinting, and for distant bodies, the gun is carried in the right hand by the clip instead. A rifle reload uses the pistol reload clip, so the rifle points up for it. Enemy types pick their **outfit colours** (skin, top, bottom, hands, feet) in their files; a body's colours come from which bone moves each face most.
- Gear is drawn with its item models (session 18b; the placeholders of session 9 became the first models): helmet, body armour, rig, backpack, and the same gun as first person. Mount points are bones: helmet on the head, armour and rig on the chest, the backpack on the upper chest, and the gun on its own weapon bone.
- **First-person arms** are low-poly forearms and hands in the player's outfit, drawn with the gun in its pass. From the hip the gun hides most of them; better framing waits for real gun models, now made in the item editor (session 18b).

## Editors (admin only, desktop browser)
The editors run on the Mac in a desktop browser and save to JSON files in the repo. The game itself is played on iOS.

**Map editor**
- **Snapping:** adjustable grid snap (coarse for buildings, fine for props, or off). Rotation at any angle, with adjustable angle snapping and **"match the rotation of the selected object"**.
- **Placement:** objects **snap down onto the surface** below (a chair onto the floor, a lamp onto a table). Overlapping props get a **red warning** but are allowed; **buildings can't overlap other buildings**.
- **Prefabs** (cars, trees, trash cans, mailboxes, whole buildings) are placed by reference, so editing a prefab updates every copy. Individual copies can be recoloured.
  - **Session 16b (the user's calls, 2026-10-27):** a prefab is **made from a selection**, which is replaced by one copy of it standing where it was, and **edited by entering a placed copy** on a map (there's no prefab-only editor). A change to a prefab that would **break any map is refused at save**, not only the open one. A placed copy can be **unpacked** into a group of its parts, to make a one-off variant without changing every copy (it then isn't a building: no concrete base).
- **Editing safety:** a placed prefab or building is selected and deleted as **one object**, and you can enter it to edit its parts. Full **undo/redo**. **The ground layer is locked** against accidental deletion.
  - **Groups (session 15, the user's call 2026-10-27):** anything can be grouped, and groups nest; a group is moved, turned, copied and deleted as one, and you enter it to edit inside. **Grouping never changes ids**, so quests and patrols that name a marker keep working. The ground layer is the terrain: it can't be selected, so it can't be deleted.
- **Terrain:** raise and lower brushes, water level, and automatic concrete bases under buildings.
  - **Session 16 (the user's calls, 2026-10-27):** a **building** is a prefab that says so, with its footprint (`building.size`); the base fills under that rectangle, and buildings' footprints may not overlap. **An edit that would make buildings overlap is refused** at once (the building shows red while dragged); other things that cut into each other get a red warning and are allowed. **Things standing on the ground move with a brush**; buildings stay put and their base grows or shrinks. Brushes: raise, lower, flatten and smooth.
- **Gameplay markers:** random player spawns, enemy spawns (type, loadout roll, loot weighting), loot containers and spawn points (loot table), extraction points, quest markers, doors and keys, named zones.
- **Navigation overlay:** shows where AI can walk, and **highlights spots where a player can stand but AI can't reach them or shoot them**, so you can fix them while building.
  - **Session 17 (the user's call, 2026-10-28):** a hiding spot is one enemies can't walk to **and** can't see a standing player on from anywhere they can walk within their 70 m sight range. Roofs they can shoot onto aren't flagged. It's worked out when you ask (a button), since it's thousands of rays; walkable and cut-off areas show live. Doors count as unlocked (a key room opens up once unlocked).
- A **play-test** button.
  - **Session 17 (the user's call, 2026-10-28):** it plays the map **as it is in the editor**, unsaved changes included, in another tab, **offline**: the new-player kit, no server, nothing saved, and a raid saved on the phone left alone. It starts at a random player spawn, a chosen one, or where the camera is looking.

**Item editor**
- Stats for every item type: weapons (stats, ammo type, attachment slots and their mount points, sight line), ammo, attachments (stat changes; for optics, the reticle design and zoom level), medical items (use time, HP pool, what they fix), armour (absorption %, durability), containers (grid size), keys, quest items.
- A model builder that makes items from simple shapes.
- Grid size in the inventory for every item.
- **Session 18 (the user's calls, 2026-10-28):** the item editor is its own page (`/item-editor`), separate from the map editor. **Saving is refused if the edit breaks anything in content/** that names items (loot tables, loadouts, vendors, quests, door keys, starting kits), as the map editor refuses a broken map. Items can be **made** (blank or as a copy), **not deleted or renamed**. Models came out of this session into **18b**. A change that can make **players' saved items invalid** (a smaller size, stack, grid, magazine or durability, a slot taken away) **warns** rather than refusing.
- **Session 18b (the user's calls, 2026-10-28):** **every item has a model**, not only those drawn before (the rest start as a box the size of their grid), so the game and the editor have one path. **Inventory icons from the models are not part of 18b.** Models are made of boxes, cylinders and wedges; a gun's parts can be hidden by what's fitted (iron sights under an optic). Worn gear is modelled on the standing body, shown in the editor. The editor previews a gun in first person, from the hip and aimed, with the game's reticle, to check the sight line.
