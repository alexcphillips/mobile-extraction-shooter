# Game design

These decisions were made in design sessions on 2026-10-25 and 2026-10-26. Update this file when a decision changes, and give the date and reason.

## Vision
A simplified mobile extraction shooter: the risk/reward loop of Tarkov without its complexity. You gear up, go into a short raid on a small map, loot and fight AI, then either extract with what you found or die and lose it.

**Pillars**
1. **Risk vs. reward.** Whatever you bring into a raid can be lost. That tension is the game.
2. **Short and readable.** Raids are about 10–15 minutes of thorough exploration (Tarkov and Delta Force are around 45), the art is simple, and the systems are simple.
3. **Phone-first.** Every system must be usable with thumbs and run well on an iPhone.
4. **Easy to author.** Maps and items are built in in-game editors and stored as data.

## Core loop and progression
- **Before a raid:** manage your stash, buy and sell with vendors, accept quests, and pick your loadout.
  - **Main menu (session 13):** the game opens on it after signing in: **Start raid**, **Stash and loadout** and **Settings**, with your name and money at the top, and **Vendors** (session 13b; quests come in 14). Your **loadout is simply the gear you're wearing**: the stash page shows the raid inventory (gear slots, pockets, rig, backpack, vault, attachment manager) beside the stash (pages of 10×30, below), with the same double-tap, drag and attachment rules. Nothing can be dropped on the ground there; anything the inventory would drop (a part taken off with no room in your grids) goes into the stash, and an action with no room in the stash either is refused. **The knife can't leave its slot.** **What's in your hands is your choice** (the user's call, 2026-10-27): the stash never takes a gun out for you, so going in with only the knife out (or only the knife at all) is fine; only if the slot in hand is emptied does the knife come out instead. The results screen's **Continue** returns to the menu.
  - **Stash pages (the user's call, 2026-10-27, Tarkov-like but for in-game money):** everyone starts with one 10×30 page (briefly 9×30 on 2026-11-09, then back; a page saved at 9×30 is widened on load) and can buy up to three more, shown as numbered tabs, with a **+ Page** button that asks for a second tap. First-guess prices **100,000, 250,000 and 500,000**, kept in 13b against the item values: about one, two and a half and five starting kits' worth. A double-tap into the stash goes to the page shown, then the others; anything that would have been dropped goes to the page shown first. **Dragging an item onto a page's tab** puts it in that page wherever there's room (the tab lights green; red if it's already on that page or there's no room). **The tabs run down the right edge of the stash grid** (UX-3, 2026-11-08; they were in the panel's header), staying in view under the search box while the page scrolls; a search's count sits under the page number, and the buy button's confirm reaches left over the grid.
  - **The lobby (2026-11-09, the user's request to replace the grid of cards; Claude's layout, easy to change):** the main menu is a lobby. **Your character stands in the middle** on a plinth, in the gear you're wearing, with something in hand (the slot in hand, else the first gun you carry, else the knife). You can drag to turn it, and it drifts back after a moment. It has its own small WebGL canvas (`render/lobby-character.ts`), drawn at 30 fps and freed when you leave the lobby: one draw call for the body, three for the plinth. The mode's name is drawn huge and faint behind it, over a floor grid. **Top bar:** the mode button on the left (opens the mode drawer); on the right, Party (member count, invites as a red pill; opens the party drawer with the old party strip and the connection test), money, your name (hidden on a phone) and the settings gear. **Down the left, small framed buttons:** Stash, Vendors, Quests (new-quest pill), **Hangar** (shown locked with "Soon"; a tap says what it will be, [hangar.md](hangar.md)), Friends (request pill), and Admin on the dev server. The cards' sub-lines became tooltips. **Down the right:** the **loadout card** (each gear slot's picture on its rarity, empty slots dashed, and what it would all sell for: everything you're taking in, pockets, rig, backpack and vault included, the same total as the stash's Gear Value, the user's call on 2026-11-09; a tap opens the stash), then **Deploy** with the map and its length (the party's state under it in a party). **Deploy opens the map screen:** the maps as a numbered list on the left, the picked one **from above** in the middle (the map tab's drawing, `ui/map-overview.tsx`: buildings, named zones, extractions, solid green if always open and dashed amber if only some raids open them), and on the right its raid time, extractions ("3 + 1 of 2"), PMC squads, size, what you'd lose (at risk: the same total without the vault) and what you're taking in, then **Start raid** (or Ready? in someone else's party, where the leader's map is locked in). Zombies uses the same lobby in green: Friends and Admin only, a starting-kit card instead of the loadout, the character in the mode's kit, and **Play** opens its map screen with points, kit, the mystery box and Start game. Esc (○ on a pad) closes a drawer or the map screen. Notices are a toast along the bottom; tap to dismiss.
  - **Menu badges (UX-5, 2026-11-08):** new quests show as a bright red pill on the Quests card ("2 new"), as a phone app's notification; the card's line keeps ready and active ("New quests to take" when only new ones are waiting). The Friends card's line says **"N online"** (in the menu or a raid, as the friends page counts them), "No one online", or the add-friends hint with no friends yet, and **friend requests to you** get the same pill. The counts ride on the menu's heartbeat (`PartyResponse.friendCount`, `friendsOnline`, `friendRequests`, one query on the server), so there's no extra request; they're up to 10 s old. Ready quests keep the quests page's green vendor badge.
- **In the raid:** loot, fight AI, do quest objectives, and reach an extraction point.
- **Death:** you lose everything you brought or found, **except what's in your 2×2 vault** (2×3 until the user's call on 2026-11-06) and **your knife**. Cash found in the raid is a physical item: lost on death unless it's in your vault. Your account balance is never at risk. (Since session 13b, cash you come out with goes into the balance.) **Giving up** (the user's request, 2026-10-30): during a raid, Settings has a **Give up** button, which asks again, then kills you on the spot whatever your armour, health or admin cheats. It counts as killed in action ("Gave up" on the results screen).
- **No insurance.** The safety net is starting equipment, weapons spawning in raids, the free knife, and the "go in with nothing, vault loot, sell it" strategy.
- **Knife:** always carried and never lost. Running is slightly faster with it out. (Session 9 gives it a gear slot. **It has an attack** (the user's call, 2026-10-26): Tarkov-style, with good damage, for raids where you bring nothing at all and for close quarters such as houses. First guesses: about 40 to the chest per hit with the usual zone multipliers (a head hit kills a scav), about 2 swings a second, 1.5 m reach, absorbed by armour like a bullet. It works when you can strike within an enemy's reaction time, by sneaking up crouched (silent) or round a corner.)
  - **Built in session 10b** with those guesses (in `content/items/knife.json`): 40 damage, a swing every 0.5 s, 1.5 m reach, a 30% bleed chance, drawn in 0.3 s. A swing lands at once (no wind-up) on the first body part within reach under the crosshair, or **within 10° of it** up, down, left or right, so it doesn't need pixel-perfect aim on a phone. Walls block it. Swinging is **silent**: enemies only notice being hit, which alerts them and makes them flinch like a shot. With **automatic fire** on, the knife **swings by itself** when an enemy is in reach under the crosshair (the user's call, 2026-10-26, matching auto-fire for guns). **"Slightly faster" is ×1.1** walking and sprinting with the knife out.

## Raids and maps
- Small maps, roughly 10–15 minutes to explore thoroughly.
- **Raid timer: 15 minutes by default**, set in the raid config. It's deliberately a little shorter than exploring the whole map, so you have to choose where to go and can't clear every corner. **Northpoint is 20 minutes** (the user's call, 2026-11-07): it's about 195 × 190 m but has 70-plus rooms worth entering, and a full sweep was estimated at 18–25 min before any fighting.
- **Player spawns:** the editor places several possible spawn points, and one is picked at random each raid. Co-op players spawn together at the same point. Enemy spawns near player spawns should be avoided (a minimum distance). **Built 2026-10-30 (the user's call, after "enemies see you from everywhere"):** an enemy spawn within 60 m of a player spawn, or in sight of one (within 70 m with a clear line between standing eyes), is a warning in the map editor's panel, and a content test keeps every map in `content/` free of them. Player spawns go on a map's edges, Tarkov-style; enemies hold the points of interest in the middle.
- **A raid is a data config:** map + playable area + starting loadout rules + objectives + enemy spawns. Normal raids and future weekly challenges use the same format.
- Terrain is a **height grid** (smooth hills, Unturned-style). Water is a flat see-through surface with no collision; you walk on the ground under it.
- On slopes, buildings sit on a concrete base that fills the gap down to the ground.
- **Stairs and ladders** work for players. AI uses stairs and slopes, but never ladders.
- **Key rooms:** locked doors that open with key items.
- **Named zones** (for example "Science lab" or "Gas station") are drawn in the editor and used for map labels and loot weighting.
- **Loot:** containers (backpacks, desks, cabinets…) and loose item spawn points each reference a loot table ("household", "academic", "medical", "military", …). Everything is rolled once when the raid starts.
  - **Loose spawns (session 11b):** a loose spot holds **one item** from its table (the table's item count is for containers), and has a **chance** of holding anything at all (1 if not given), so loose loot isn't in the same places every raid. The small house's table spot is 0.7.
  - **One of several spots (2026-10-30, for the mall's keys):** loose spots that share a `oneOf` name are a set, and only one of them, picked at random, is used each raid (it then rolls its chance as usual). For an item that must be somewhere but not always in the same place, like a key, without making every spot roll it.
- **Quest items** spawn only for a player who has that quest accepted (in co-op, only for the quest holder).
- **Extraction (decided with the user, 2026-10-26, session 11):** Tarkov-like. Stand inside an extraction point's radius for **7 seconds**, with a countdown on screen; stepping out resets it, being shot doesn't. Every extraction point was open in every raid until 2026-11-07; now an extraction zone is either **always open** or **rotating** (see Extraction points are zones). **When the timer runs out you're "missing in action"**, which counts as dying: you keep only your vault and your knife.
  - Session 11 details: the countdown names the point ("Extracting at Pond dock · 4.2"). Extraction points are drawn as **faint green columns** so you can find them. **They stay now that the map tab exists** (the user's call, 2026-10-27): extracts are easy to find on a small screen, and removing them later is one line (Tarkov shows nothing). The timer is in the top left and turns red under 2 minutes.
  - **Extraction points are zones (map format 4, the user's call, 2026-11-07):** not circles any more. In the map editor, draw a zone with the zones tool (click its corners), then tick **Extraction point** in its inspector: its name is the point's name, and it gets a **floor height** (filled in from the surface under it, looking down from the floor shown with the cut, else from the top, so a roof's helipad finds the roof; editable, with a button to measure it again). Players leave by standing **inside its outline, from 0.5 m under that floor up to the zone's height** (its "counts up to" field, 2 m if left blank; the user's call, same day: a zone round a shipping container can count its inside, round it and its roof, but not the building's floor above it), for the usual 7 s. **In the raid it's a short light-green border** round the outline on its floor, 25 cm tall whatever the height, with no fill; the map tab draws its outline only. The editor shows the zone's top too, its corners joined, so the height can be seen. Maps from before (format 3) turn each old circle into an octagon zone of the same size when loaded; the three maps were converted.
  - **Always open or rotating (the user's call, 2026-11-07, as Tarkov's random extracts):** an extraction zone is always open unless ticked **Open in only some raids (rotating)**. The map says how many of its rotating ones each raid opens ("rotating extractions open each raid", e.g. 3 of 5; blank opens them all, 0 none), and the pick is **each player's own** (see below). A closed one doesn't exist for you that raid: no green border, not on the map tab, and you can't leave by it. The editor's zones list tags them "rotating extraction".
  - **Extractions depend on where you spawn (the user's call, 2026-11-07):** each **player spawn** says which extractions a player starting there **can't use** (in the editor, select the spawn: a checkbox per extraction, all on by default). So a spawn in the warehouse loading yard can have the yard's car extract switched off, while spawns across the map keep it. **Each player's extractions are their own**: the always-open ones their spawn allows, plus the rotating ones it allows out of their own random pick: the map's "N per raid" picked from **all** the map's rotating extractions, drawn from the raid and the spawn, *then* the spawn's rule applied. So a rotating extract stays a chance (3 of 5 is 60%) for every spawn that allows it, however many others that spawn rules out; picking among only the allowed ones (the first version, the same day) made it certain for a spawn with N or fewer left, which the user ruled out. The cost: a spawn that rules some rotating ones out gets fewer in some raids; one with no always-open extraction that a raid could leave with none is warned about, so a co-op party, which starts together at one spawn, shares them, and in PvP someone who spawned elsewhere can have extracts you don't. The green borders, the map tab and leaving all go by your own. The editor lists any spawn left with no extraction it could use (map settings, under the zones), and a content test fails on one.
  - **Results screen:** extracted (green), killed in action (red, with what killed you) or missing in action (orange); time in raid and kills; what you brought out, or what you kept (vault and knife) and lost. It comes up 1.5 s after a death, so you see it happen. **After a death it lists what hit you** (the user's request, 2026-10-30): one line per enemy and weapon ("Yard boss shot you 4 times for 142 (chest, head) · armour took 30"), falls, and HP lost to bleeding, with the killing blow's source in red. The client keeps this from the sim's damage events (bleeding is the HP lost that no hit explains) and saves it with the raid for resuming. **Raid gain** (the user's request, 2026-11-03): every outcome lists the found-in-raid items you keep, vault included, at vendor prices (cash at full value), with the sum, which is 0 if none survived. Found in raid is a saved mark on each item (`Item.found`, the user's call, 2026-11-03, because quests need it from the stash): **found and not-found stacks never merge**, and a `handOver` objective with `foundInRaid: true` takes only found items (opt-in; existing quests don't use it). In a raid the mark is the other way round: `markBrought` flags what you take in, so anything unmarked came from the raid, however it reached you, and `settleFound` turns that back into `found` on the gear sent to the server. Something you brought stays not found through dropping it and picking it up. **What comes off a gun, attachments and rounds, is never found**: guns don't record their parts' marks, and otherwise any bought round could be made found by passing it through a found gun. A found gun's parts do count towards its gain. Since session 12b it also says whether the raid is saved; since session 13 its **Continue** button goes back to the main menu.
- **Game modes (2026-11-04, the user's request):** a switch at the top of the main menu, **Extraction | Zombies**, picks which mode's menu shows (since 2026-11-09 it's the lobby's mode button, top left, which opens a drawer of mode cards); the pick is remembered on the phone (and with no zombies modes in content the switch is hidden). Extraction's menu is the one above (party, maps, Start raid, stash, vendors, quests, friends, money). Zombies' menu is green rather than amber and has no money, stash, vendors or party: a map pick (with more than one mode in `content/zombies`), **Start game**, cards for the starting kit and points and the mystery box's guns, how it works, and the Admin button in dev builds.
- **Picking the map (2026-10-30, the user's call):** with more than one raid in `content/raids`, the main menu shows a button per map above Start raid; the pick is remembered on the phone. Since 2026-11-09 the maps are on the lobby's map screen, behind Deploy, with an overview of each (see The lobby).

## Movement (decided 2026-10-26, session 3)
- **Walk, sprint, crouch, prone, jump, vault and ladders.** Sprinting only works forwards, and it stands you up. You can't jump while crouched or prone.
- **Crouching** lowers the capsule from 1.8 m to 1.2 m (eye 1.6 → 1.0 m) and slows you down. You can't stand up under something low.
- **Steps up to 0.45 m** are walked onto (kerbs, single stairs). A jump (0.6 m) plus that step lets you **mantle onto ledges about 1 m high**. **Jump, then vault** (the user's request, 2026-11-04): pressing or holding jump in the air vaults from where your feet are, so a jump reaches **ledges up to about 1.7 m**, not just 1 m; taller walls need stairs or a ladder. The editor's "unreachable spot" overlay must count jump-and-mantle as player movement.
- **Prone** (the user's request, 2026-10-30): **X toggles it** on a computer; on touch, **holding the crouch button** (0.4 s) does, and the button then reads "prone". A tap on it while prone gets up to a crouch; C, jump or sprinting gets up too (from prone, jump only gets up). The capsule is **0.6 m** (eye 0.4 m) and crawls at **0.8 m/s**; it's still an upright capsule, so you're as narrow as standing, and the hit zones squash with it as they do crouched. You can't stand or crouch up under something low, as with crouching. Only on the ground: in the air you're crouched. Enemies notice you at **×0.6 on top of crouching's ×0.6**, and crawling is silent. There's no prone animation yet: the body is drawn crouched. Enemies don't go prone.
- **Crouch: toggle or hold** (the user's call, 2026-11-08): a setting, **toggle by default**, for keys and the button alike, as aim and lean are. Toggle: C or a tap on the button crouches until pressed again (or sprinting), and from prone gets up to a crouch; on touch, holding the button still goes prone. Hold: crouched while C or the button is down. On touch, hold mode can't use a long press for prone, so **a quick double-tap goes prone** (each tap under 0.3 s, the second within 0.3 s), and a press from prone gets up (Claude's suggestion, the user's OK, 2026-11-08). On a PC this changes the default: C used to be held only. X still toggles prone either way.
- **Leaning** (the user's request, 2026-11-01): **Q and E** on a computer, and two **lean buttons** above fire on touch. **Hold or toggle is a setting** (the user's call, same day), like aim, for keys and buttons alike: **toggle by default** (a press leans, one on the same side straightens up, the other switches sides; sprinting straightens up), because a held button would need a third thumb while moving and firing. (First built as held on a computer and toggled on touch.) The body above the hips tips up to **about 29°** sideways from the hips, which moves a standing eye about **0.34 m** to the side and 9 cm down, over **0.15 s**. You can walk, aim and fire while leaning; **sprinting, going prone, jumping, ladders and vaulting straighten you up** (and on touch, sprinting turns the toggle off). A lean **stops short of walls** (15 cm from the eye), so it can't put your eye or gun through one. **It's in the sim, not just the camera** (Claude's call): your shots, knife, throws and looting start from the leaned eye; **your hit zones above the hips lean with you**, so leaning round a corner shows your head, shoulder and arm on that side but not your legs; and **enemies see and aim at what's showing** (the head and chest points they check move with the lean). **A lean is a little harder to notice** (the user's call, same day): ×0.75 at a full lean, less for a part one, on top of the other factors. A camera-only lean would have let you see without being seen, and your shots would have hit the wall. The view tilts **about 10°** (a third of the body's angle). Drawn by bending the spine in code (no clip: the Quaternius pack has none), with the gun canted with it. **Enemies don't lean yet.** Numbers are first guesses (`MOVEMENT.lean*`).
  - **Aim while leaning** (a setting, the user's request, 2026-11-07; off by default): leaning aims too, for as long as you lean, whether the lean is held or toggled, and like any aiming it stops a sprint. Letting the lean go stops aiming, unless aim is on by itself.
- **Vaulting through windows** (the user's request, 2026-10-30; built the same day): you can get through a standard-height window. A standard window is a gap in a wall with its **sill 0.7–1.1 m** above your feet, **at least 0.8 m tall and 0.7 m wide**, through a wall **at most 0.5 m thick**, with floor on the other side. **Jump while facing one, close to it** vaults: a short scripted move (about 0.5 s) over the sill to the floor beyond, with the camera dipping and no animation beyond that. The capsule isn't checked against the opening's height during the move (even crouched it wouldn't fit), only that the far side has room to stand or crouch. It works from crouch too, unlike jumping. Found from the collision geometry, not marked in the map, so any prefab with a gap that size is vaultable. The editor's unreachable-spot overlay must count vaults (the overlay isn't built yet). **Over small objects too** (the user's request, same day): the same rule vaults low walls, railings and crates, anything from the 0.45 m step height up to 1.1 m and **up to 1 m deep** (so a window's wall up to 1 m thick counts too), with the gap above it (0.8 m tall, 0.7 m wide) and room beyond. "Facing" is the look direction, "close" is within 0.5 m of the capsule, and you're slid sideways up to 0.3 m to line up with an opening. The far floor may be up to 1 m lower. You land crouched if there's only room to crouch. **Onto things (2026-11-04):** something flat and too deep to go over is vaulted onto, landing on top; in the air (jump, then vault) you land on top of anything with room for you, and from the ground you still go over what you can. No firing, aiming, knife or meds during the move (as on a ladder), and you can't vault from prone. **Open:** whether enemies vault too (a nav link through the window) or go round by the door; until they do, a window is a way to lose a chaser.
- **Glass (the user's request, 2026-11-02):** a window can have a **pane of breakable glass** in it, thinner than a wall (2 cm) and tinted any colour, drawn see-through. Whole, it's solid: you can't walk or vault through it and grenades bounce off it, but **you and enemies see (and hear) through it**. It **breaks for the rest of the raid** under a shot (the bullet goes on through, so you can shoot someone behind it), a knife, a blast in its radius, or a grenade on an impact fuse hitting it (a molotov smashes on it). Breaking it plays a **softened smash** (no harsh highs) and is **heard by enemies within 12 m** (the user's call: a room or two; less through walls), who go and look as they do for a blast. Glass an enemy breaks doesn't send the others to look.
- **Little air control:** you can speed up along the stick in the air, up to running speed, but never brake, so sprint jumps keep their momentum.
- **Ladders:** push towards the wall to climb up and away from it to climb down, whichever way you're looking. You grab one by walking into it, or by walking off its top edge. At the top you step over onto the surface behind it. Jump lets go.
- **Steep terrain** (over 45°) can't be walked up, but you can walk down it.
- Speeds: walk 3.5, sprint 6, crouch 1.8 and climb 2 m/s. All the tuning is in `MOVEMENT` in `packages/shared/src/sim/movement.ts`.

## Combat
- **Hitscan** (bullets travel instantly, as a raycast). No bullet travel time or drop.
- **Collision** is simple: boxes (rotated about the vertical axis only), the terrain height grid, and ramps for stairs. No physics engine.
- **Weapon attachments** use fixed slots, like Battlefield 4 or Black Ops 2: **optic, muzzle, magazine**, with underbarrel or laser possible later. Each gun defines its slots and where they sit on the model; each attachment says which slot type it fits.
  - **Fit (decided 2026-10-26, session 6b):** an attachment fits every gun with its slot, unless it lists the guns it fits. Optics and muzzles usually fit anything; magazines list their gun (the 45-round 7.62×39 mag fits only the AKM).
  - **Stat changes** multiply the gun's spread (hip and aimed), recoil, aim time, reload time and loudness; a magazine adds rounds. All the attachments' changes stack. Since 2026-11-04 recoil can also be changed up and sideways separately (`recoilUp`, `recoilSide`, on top of `recoil`), and **ergonomics** multiplies how fast recoil wears off (see Recoil below).
  - **Changing the magazine** puts rounds that no longer fit back with your spare ammo, and stops a reload in progress.
- **Magazines:** one magazine attachment per gun (for example, "extended mag" raises capacity). Reloading takes from the ammo you carry. No individual mags, no loading rounds.
- **Suppressors** reduce a shot's hearing radius (see AI). Session 6b: every gun has a `loudness` (how far away a shot is heard, in metres; rifle 180, pistol 120), and each shot carries it for AI hearing. The suppressor multiplies it by 0.3 and hides the muzzle flash. A muzzle brake is the opposite trade: 30% less kick up and 20% less sideways, 20% louder. The suppressor's weight costs 10% ergonomics (2026-11-04). A **compensator** (the user's call, 2026-11-04) only cuts kick sideways, by 20%, with no loudness cost: cheaper (2,500, uncommon), in the attachments and military loot and sold by the Quartermaster.
- **More guns (2026-10-30):** four more, each with one clear job. Numbers are first guesses, to tune in play.

- **Laser:** a short see-through red line plus stat changes. No collision, and the AI doesn't notice it.
- **Firing (session 6):** weapons are `auto` (fire while held) or `semi` (once per pull), with a rate in rounds per minute. Shots stray randomly within a cone: wide from the hip, tight when aimed; aiming in takes the gun's aim time. Each shot kicks the view up (and a random bit sideways), and the view settles back by itself; the kick is the simulation's, so co-op sees the same. **Firing or aiming stops a sprint**, and firing ends sprint lock as aiming does. You can't fire on a ladder or while reloading. Pulling the trigger on an empty gun starts a reload. All weapon stats are in the item files (`content/items`).
- **Aiming:** magnified scopes use full-screen zoom (narrower field of view, a scope mask and a reticle), like other mobile shooters. Red dots (Coyote-style) have no zoom, just a reticle. Reticles and zoom levels are set in the item editor. Each gun model defines a sight line so the reticle lines up with the camera when aiming.
  - Session 6b: a scope's view **snaps to its zoom behind the mask at 90% aimed** (before that it zooms like iron sights), and the gun is hidden. Looking turns the view more slowly by the same factor, so it moves as fast on the screen. A red dot's reticle shows at 95% aimed, when its frame has come up to the eye. **No crosshair at all (the user's call, 2026-11-05, as in Tarkov):** there's no dot from the hip either, and no hit-marker flash or sound: nothing says a shot landed or killed but the target itself (the hit and kill sounds went the same day). Hip fire, the knife, taking items and auto-fire still aim along the screen's centre, just unmarked.
  - **Sights move, and shots follow them (the user's call, 2026-11-04):** aimed, a player's gun sways (about 0.35° side to side and 0.25° up and down standing still, twice that walking, ×3 with a broken arm and ×5 with both) and lags behind turning (at most 1.5° and 1°, settling faster on guns with a short aim time). Recoil climbs the sights only a few degrees before the view takes over (see Recoil → View vs. gun). The reticle, the scope mask and iron sights all sit where the gun points, and bullets go there. The hip crosshair stays centred with no sway and full recoil on the view. Enemies have no sway. Tune on a real iPhone: thumb aiming is the risk.
  - **Iron sights (2026-11-09, the user's request after "the back block completely covers the front iron sight"):** aimed, the eye sits on a gun model's `sight` height, 0.3 m behind the gun's origin (0.34 m on a pistol), looking straight along −z, so the irons are built round that line: the **front post's tip is on it**, and the rear is a **notch whose ear tops are on it** or a **square aperture centred on it**. Sizes are set by the angle they cover from the eye, the same on every gun: post 0.7° wide, notch 2.1° wide and 1.7° deep with 1.2° ears, aperture hole 3.6° across in a 0.7° frame, the post tall enough to fill the notch or hole; front wings sit 1° below the tip so the post is the highest thing in the picture, and a hood (HK guns, bolt guns) clears it by 1.1°. Nothing nearer the eye may reach the line inside the notch or hole. Pistols have three white dots; the MP-133 a brass bead. Rear sights fold away under an optic; guns without real irons (AUG, AWM) get flip-ups that do too. The parts are named (`rear sight`, `rear sight ear`, `rear sight ring`, `front sight post`, `front bead`, …) in the group `iron sights`, and a content test holds them to `sight`.
  - **Recoil has to outrun recovery (the user's call, 2026-11-04):** recovery runs during a burst, so a gun only climbs if `up` is more than `recovery × 60 / rpm`; before this every automatic recovered more than it kicked and fired on one spot. New numbers (up / side / recovery): AKM 1.8 / 0.7 / 11, SMG 0.7 / 1 / 9, pistol 2.4 / 0.9 / 10, shotgun 6 / 1.8 / 9, sniper 5 / 1 / 8, crossbow 0.9 / 0.3 / 8, grenade launcher 8 / 2 / 8, flamethrower 0.3 / 0.4 / 2.5. **SMGs kick more sideways than up; assault rifles more up than sideways.** Admin spawner guns are unchanged. Enemies use the same numbers but cancel all of it (see AI). *Recovery's meaning changed with the rework below.*
  - **Recoil rework (the user's spec, 2026-11-04, after Delta Force and Tarkov):** no hand-made spray patterns; it's procedural.
    - **Kick:** each round pushes a target up by `up` and sideways by a random amount up to `side` either way. The **first round of a burst kicks 1.4× harder**, so taps need control too; letting go of the trigger for **0.25 s** starts a new burst.
    - **Spring:** the gun chases that target on a damped spring (damping 0.6, so it overshoots each kick a little), at the gun's **snap** (the spring's frequency per second; about 40 lands a kick in a tenth of a second). The target wears off at the gun's **recovery** (a rate per second; after 1/recovery s a third is left). So how hard a gun snaps and how fast it settles are tuned separately. *Until 2026-11-05* recovery ran in full during a burst, so sustained fire levelled off where kick and recovery balance (the AKM at about 7° by round 10); see the climb below. **Once the burst is over** (0.25 s off the trigger, when the next round would be a first round again) recovery is **4× faster**, so the gun comes back down in about half a second instead of hanging (the user's call, same day); quick semi-automatic taps don't count as letting go, so their climb is unchanged.
    - **It keeps climbing, as in Tarkov (the user's call, 2026-11-05, after "I just pull down a little and it stops going up"):** the levelling off came from recovery during the burst, not from the cap. Now, while a burst lasts, the climb wears off at only **10% of the gun's recovery** (`RECOIL.inBurst`; sideways still recovers in full, so the shake doesn't wander), and from the **sixth round** each kick is **half as hard** (`steadyAfter`, `steadyKick`): a hard early kick, then a steady climb that never stops, which the player pulls down. Uncontrolled from standing, a magazine climbs about 21° on the AKM (9° after 5 rounds), 16° on the AUG and 9° on the SMG. The cap went from 20° to 35° so a magazine never reaches it. Letting go still brings it back down in about half a second. First guesses; tune on an iPhone.
    - **Stance and movement** scale every kick: standing 1, crouched 0.75, prone 0.5, and ×1.35 moving faster than 0.5 m/s (crawling counts).
    - **Thumbs kick 0.6× (the user's call, 2026-11-08, after "quite hard to aim"):** on a touchscreen or a controller every kick, up and sideways, is ×0.6 (`RECOIL.thumb`); a mouse keeps the full numbers. The guns' numbers came from Tarkov, a mouse game: aimed with iron sights or a red dot, an AKM magazine needed about 115 px of thumb drag to hold down (more through a scope, which is still a separate question), and now about 70. The shape (a hard early kick, then a climb that never stops) and how the guns compare are unchanged. It's a per-player `thumb` flag on the input, so a co-op host applies each player's own. Enemies are unaffected.
    - **View vs. gun:** from the hip all of recoil is on the view, so the centred hip crosshair stays true. Aimed, the gun and its reticle take about 70% of the first few degrees, then only **10%** of the climb, and the view takes the rest (`gunRecoil`, `RECOIL.aimedShare`, `aimedMax`, `aimedTail`). Decided 2026-11-05 (the user's call, for Tarkov's feel without modelling convergence): a flat 70% put the sights halfway to the top of the screen after an AKM magazine, two-thirds on the SCAR-H and off screen through a 4× scope. A first version stopped the gun at a hard 5°, but then the sights stopped moving at all and recoil seemed to stop with them (the user, on SMGs), so the 10% tail stays: heavier guns end further up, and every round still shows. A magazine now leaves the sights about 20% of the way to the top of the screen on the AKM, and the view climbs about 18°. The result is divided by the scope's zoom, so it's the same distance on the screen. This only changes what's drawn: shots go where the sim says, as they did.
    - **Numbers (up / side / snap / recovery):** AKM 1.8 / 0.7 / 40 / 3, SMG 0.7 / 1 / 45 / 3, pistol 2.4 / 0.9 / 45 / 6, shotgun 6 / 1.8 / 35 / 4, sniper 5 / 1 / 30 / 3, crossbow 0.9 / 0.3 / 30 / 4, grenade launcher 8 / 2 / 25 / 3, flamethrower 0.3 / 0.4 / 20 / 2. First guesses; tune on an iPhone.
    - **Attachments** use the slots there are (optic, muzzle, magazine); grips and stocks would need new slots and mounts on every gun model, so they wait for their own session. The mods for them (`recoilUp`, `recoilSide`, `ergonomics`) already work on any attachment.
    - **Kick angle and steadying (the user's call, same day, after Tarkov's `RecoilAngle` and `RecoilStableIndexShot`):** a gun's optional `angle` (degrees off straight up, + right, at most 45) makes every burst drift that way, on top of the random sideways noise, so its pull can be learnt. Optional `stableAfter` makes the gun stop climbing (and drifting) past that round of a burst: later rounds only top it back up to where that round left it, so it still bounces and shakes. The AKM drifts 8° right and the SMG 12° left; neither steadies early (they level off by themselves around round 10).
    - **Tuning a new gun's recoil:** start from the template (up 1, side 0.4, snap 40, recovery 4). `up` sets how hard it climbs; a magazine's climb is roughly `up × (4 + 0.5 × (rounds − 5))` less a little recovery (`recovery` mostly sets how fast it comes back after the burst), so a fast-firing gun climbs faster for the same `up`. `side` is shake, `angle` is drift. `snap` 30 feels heavy and 45 sharp. `recovery` 2–3 suits automatics, 4–6 semi-automatics. `stableAfter` caps the climb at a chosen round when the balance above lets it go too high.
    - **Softer early kicks, steadier late climb (the user's call, 2026-11-05, after "early shots are too much across the board" and "with smgs I just pull down a little and recoil stops"):** a burst now kicks 1× on its first round and 0.75× on the next four (1.4× and 1× before), then 0.5× as before, and the climb wears off at **5%** of recovery during a burst (10% before, which ate nearly half an SMG's late kicks). The first five rounds climb about 25% less, and the late climb is 25–55% faster. The per-gun climb figures quoted elsewhere in this file are from before this. A magazine now climbs about 23.5° on the AKM (20°), 29° on the SCAR-H (28°), 11.5° on the MP5 (10°) and 9.5° on the K10 (9°). `firstShot` still kicks harder than a later round, but no harder than the gun's `up`.
    - **SMGs and the AS VAL up a little (the user's call, 2026-11-05, after playing the softer curve):** the K10 most, as it should be harder to control, up 0.75 → 1 and side 0.7 → 0.8. The MP5, MPX, MP7 and UMP 45 go up 10% (0.92, 0.84, 0.82, 1.18). The AS VAL goes up 1.19 → 1.4, vertical only. These replace the numbers in their entries, which stay as the scaling from Tarkov's stats.
    - **The sights show exactly where the next round goes**, even mid-burst: they're drawn with the sim's latest recoil rather than eased between ticks (a bug fix, same day: rounds landed up to 0.4° under the reticle at the AKM's peak). They may step slightly at 30 Hz in a burst; the view stays smooth.
    - Enemies still cancel all of their recoil exactly: the spring settles after the tick's shots, so the recoil an enemy aims against is the recoil it fires with.
  - Reticles are drawn on the screen, not in 3D, so they stay sharp and readable at any distance. Their size is in % of the screen height, not angles: a real 2 MOA dot would be a fraction of a pixel on a phone.

## Damage, armour and health
- **Hit zones:** head, chest, left arm, right arm, left leg, right leg. Each has its own HP pool and damage multiplier. Session 6 values: HP 35, 85, 60, 60, 65, 65 (370 in all); shot multipliers 1, 1, 0.7, 0.7, 0.75, 0.75. Damage per shot is the ammo's. **The head is ×1** (the user's call, 2026-11-08; it was ×1.5): every bullet of 35 damage or more still one-shots an unarmoured head, but a single buckshot pellet (33) no longer kills through it, 4.6 AP and Igolnik need two head hits, and a round level with a helmet gets half its damage through rather than 75%, so a matching round no longer kills through a helmet in one hit (AKM 52 → 26). A stomach zone (70 HP, non-lethal) and a 100 HP chest were weighed the same day and skipped for now: nothing felt too fast yet; the stomach split, with the chest kept at 85 and the vest covering both, is the fix if unarmoured fights later feel over too soon. All tuning is in `HEALTH` in `packages/shared/src/sim/health.ts`.
- **Hit zones are a fixed capsule layout** (decided 2026-10-26, session 6), worked out from the character's position, yaw and crouch, not attached to animated bones. That keeps hits in the simulation, the same on a co-op server with no renderer. The drawn body (session 8b) is posed to match.
- **The head zone follows the pose (the user's report, 2026-11-04):** shots could hit the drawn head and miss its capsule, because the animation clips carry the head well forward of the layout (measured: 3.5 cm standing, 14 cm walking, 20 cm jogging, 38 cm sprinting, plus crouching and looking down). A sphere wouldn't help: the position was wrong, not the shape. The head capsule now moves with speed and direction, crouch and pitch, in the sim, so a co-op host needs no skeleton. The chest and limbs are still fixed.
  - **Arms are held forward** (decided with the user, 2026-10-26, session 8b): every armed pose has both arms out in front, so the arm capsules run from the shoulders down to the elbows and in to the hands in front of the chest. From the front, arms now shield part of the chest (×0.7 instead of ×1). A sprinting or unarmed body doesn't quite match. The chest became two narrower capsules side by side, so it's wider than deep like the drawn torso and doesn't swallow shots at the forearms.
  - AI still aims at 0.6 of body height (about 1.08 m standing), just under the forearms, so some of its shots now land on arms. Retune if fights feel softer.
- **Armour:** helmets cover the head, chest armour covers the chest. Armour loses durability as it's hit; at 0 durability it stops nothing. Armour can be repaired with armour repair kits.

- **Ammo and armour: Tarkov's numbers on fixed levels (the user's call, 2026-11-05; levels since 2026-10-30).** Rounds and armour take their numbers from Tarkov (damage, penetration as a level, armour wear, blunt damage); the hit itself is decided by a **fixed level comparison** (from Delta Force, chosen 2026-10-30 over Tarkov's random penetration chance), and **rarity follows level**. Both are this game's own and stay. In the docs, "levels" means the comparison and "Tarkov" where the numbers come from.
  - **Levels 1–6** for every round and armour piece, coloured grey, green, blue, purple, gold and red (a small level tag in item details and the ammo readout; item rarity colours the tile, so tiles have no level tag, the user's call, 2026-11-09).
  - **A hit on armour with durability left** compares levels: **level with it: half** gets through; **one level above: 75%; two or more above: all** (the user's call, 2026-11-04). **Below it: nothing gets through** (the user's call, 2026-11-08: the "absolute gate"), and it never starts bleeding. From 2026-11-05 a round below the armour's level let blunt damage through (25% through a helmet, 15% through body armour, after Tarkov), but that let cheap rounds kill through the best armour (PMCs killed Jug with level 2–3 headshots through his level 6 mask) and favoured high-damage, low-level rounds.   - **Wear:** every hit takes the round's `armourDamage` in durability, ×1 against armour at or below the round's level, ×0.8 one level above, ×0.6 two, ×0.4 three or more. So low rounds can still strip armour, slowly.
  - **From Tarkov to levels (2026-11-05):**
    - **Level = Tarkov penetration ÷ 10, rounded down** (1–6; Tarkov's armour classes are about 10 apart). A round within one of a boundary may move across it to fill an empty level (TCW SP 30 → 2, T-45M1 30 → 2, PBP 39 → 4).
    - **Damage = Tarkov damage × 0.85**, anchored on the rounds that already matched (PS 61 → 52, M855 54 → 46). Lower-penetration rounds do more damage, often much more (Hydra-Shok-style JHP 85 against .45 AP 56).
    - **Armour damage ≈ 0.9 × Tarkov penetration × Tarkov armour-damage %** (Tarkov's durability lost per hit; M80 43 × 67% × 0.9 = 26).
    - **Guns don't change damage** (Tarkov's barrels change velocity slightly); a gun's power is its round's. Rounds the game invented (incendiaries, bolts, dragon's breath) keep their numbers, scaled with their calibre where it moved.
    - **Low-level, high-damage rounds** (Ultra Nosler, HP, JHP, HP BT) kill unarmoured targets fast and do nothing through better armour: for scavs, limbs and heads without helmets. The knife counts as level 1, so armour stops it outright (it wore it down only, since 2026-11-08).

## AI
- **Awareness states:** unaware → suspicious (heard or glimpsed something) → alert (knows where you are) → lost track (searches, then goes back to patrolling).
- **Hearing:** each shot has a hearing radius; suppressors shrink it.
- **Unreachable player** (alert, but there's no path to you): take cover out of your line of sight, hold angles on the ways down, patrol nearby, and give up after a while. Since AI-6 (2026-11-08) PMCs carrying frags throw them, even onto somewhere they can't reach (see PMCs → Built in AI-6); one grenade "token" is shared across all AI in the area so many don't throw at once, and AI can't damage itself or its allies (as for every ally hit).
- **Bosses** are enemy types with stronger stats and loadouts.
- **Enemy spawn points** (set in the editor) define the enemy type, a loadout roll, and loot table weighting (for example, a lab boss drops more rare science and medical loot).
  - **Not the same every raid (the user's call, 2026-11-01, after "they spawn 100% where their spot is and it's predictable"):** an enemy spawn has a **chance** (0 to 1, 1 if left out) of putting its enemy in the raid at all, and a **one of** name: of the enemy spawns sharing it, only one picked at random is used (then rolling its chance), as for loose loot. **One of is the main tool**: where enemies stand changes but not how many, so difficulty stays steady; chance is for rare ones like bosses, since a chance on every spawn makes the count swing (three at 0.6 is no one about 1 raid in 16). Rolled once at raid start with the raid's RNG (`rollEnemySpawns`, `sim/spawn-roll.ts`); a resumed raid or a co-op guest takes the enemies already in the state. Names are map-wide and kept apart from loot spawns' names. The test map's yard scav is one of `yard-scav` (its shed patrol, or guarding by the small house) and the yard boss is there 60% of the time. Later, if needed: pick N of a group, and enemies arriving during the raid.
- **Navigation** is generated automatically from the map data (see the editor's navigation overlay).

**Session 7 decisions (2026-10-26).** All the tuning is in `AI` in `packages/shared/src/sim/ai.ts`; the numbers are first guesses to tune in play.
- **Patrols** (chosen by the user): a `patrol` node in a map or prefab is a named route, and an enemy spawn names one with `patrol`. A spawn without one **guards**: it stands at its spawn and looks a new way every few seconds, within 70° of the spawn's direction.
  - **The route is authored, not the shortest path** (asked for by the user): a point is either a **stop** (stand and look around for its own `wait`, 3 s by default) or a plain position the enemy **walks through** without slowing. Between consecutive points it takes the shortest way, so through-points shape the route (round the back of a house rather than across the yard).
  - **Areas, not spots:** a stop can have a `radius`, and each visit goes to a random walkable spot within it; an enemy spawn can have a `radius` too. The same patrol looks different from raid to raid.
  - **Way in, then repeat:** points before `repeatFrom` are walked once from the spawn; the rest repeat, in a loop or back and forth. For example: spawn in an area, walk a set route to area B, wait, walk on to area C, wait, then go back and forth between B and C.
  - After an alarm it picks the patrol up again at the nearest point.
- **Sight:** a 120° field of view out to 70 m, blocked by walls; you're seen if your head or the middle of your body is. Noticing builds up over time rather than being instant. Someone standing in the open within 8 m is noticed in about half a second, and it gets slower with distance and towards the edge of view. **Retuned 2026-11-01** (the user's report: an enemy in the open on the test map noticed them from across the map while they walked around; measured there, enemies were suspicious at 55–65 m within 2–3 s and alert soon after, crouched or not): beyond 8 m the rate falls as (8 / distance)^1.6, so in plain view straight ahead, walking, full notice takes **about 3 s at 25 m, 9 s at 50 m and 16 s at 70 m** (a glimpse, 30% of that). It was a square-root falloff to 8% at 70 m (about 6 s there, but only about 3 s at 60 m). Walking straight at an enemy across open ground now makes it suspicious at about 50 m and alert at about 40 m. Crouching (×0.6) and standing still (×0.7) make you harder to notice, sprinting easier (×1.3), and an enemy already on edge notices faster (×1.6). Inside 2.5 m in view it's instant. A glimpse (30% noticed) makes it suspicious; full makes it alert.
- **Hearing shots, tiered** (chosen by the user, because the rifle is heard 180 m away on a 192 m map): within the shot's loudness, an enemy is suspicious and turns to look; within half of it, it walks over to investigate; within 15%, it's alert. Suppressors shrink every tier. A bullet passing or landing within 3 m, or a hit, makes it alert to the shooter wherever they are. **Walls muffle (2026-10-30):** with walls or ground between the shot and the listener, the shot counts as half as loud for every tier (one ray per listener in earshot). A heard noise is placed with an error of up to 10% of its distance (at most 8 m).
- **Gunfire and blasts draw few, and carefully (the user's call, 2026-11-01**, after one shot pulled every enemy on the map to it, single file through a door the player held at head height):
  - **At most 2 go to check the same one**, the nearest first: a nearer enemy takes the place of the furthest one going, which stays where it is and looks around. The rest turn to look and stay at their post. "The same one" is anything placed within 20 m of a spot already being checked. This counts players' shots, allies' shots ("join the fight" below) and grenades, so a grenade is still heard across the map but sends only two. Footsteps and glimpses aren't capped. Being alarmed (a shot within 15%, a near miss, a hit) isn't capped either.
  - **They stop short and watch:** on the way, the first spot within **12 m** of the noise that can see where it was (1 m above it) is where it stops. It **watches it aimed for 6 s**, then **creeps in** at about half its investigating pace, still aimed, and looks around there as before. So an enemy coming to a shot behind a door watches the doorway from outside, rather than walking through it. Footsteps and glimpses are still walked straight up to.
  - Hearing radii didn't change (Claude offered shrinking the investigate tier to 30%; not taken).
- **Footsteps** (chosen by the user): sprinting is heard 15 m away and walking 6 m; crouch-walking and standing still are silent. Footsteps make an enemy suspicious, never alert on their own.
- **Awareness in practice:** suspicious → turns to look for 1.5 s, then walks over if it's worth checking (a glimpse, a nearby shot or footsteps), looks around there for 5 s, and goes back to its patrol. Alert → knows where you are while it can see you, and for 1 s after losing sight (it saw which way you went); then it fights as in session 8 below, and when it has lost you it's **searching**: it goes to where it last saw you, then checks 3 random spots within 12 m, and calms down. An unarmed enemy faces you, closes to 10 m and holds.
- Enemies' own shots: see session 8 below.
- **Bodies push each other apart** (player and enemies alike); the dead don't.

**Session 8 decisions (2026-10-26): combat.** Tuning is in `AI` and in the enemy type files; the numbers are first guesses to tune in play.
- **Enemy types are data** (`content/enemies/<id>.json`): a name, a default loadout, and a **skill**: reaction time, how far its aim wanders when it first sees you and once settled, how fast it settles, and how likely it is to hold an angle rather than push. Bosses are types with better skill and a better loadout, not more HP (the HUD and healing assume the same zone HP for everyone). The scav reacts in 0.7 s and settles to 0.7°; the yard boss reacts in 0.4 s, settles to 0.35° and holds angles more.
- **Loadouts** (`content/loadouts/<id>.json`) are rolled when an enemy spawns: the weapon, helmet and body armour are each picked by weight on their own, with a spare-rounds range, attachments that each go on by chance, and armour that can be worn (a condition range). An enemy spawn's `loadout` replaces its type's. Scavs carry a pistol or a rifle, sometimes a helmet or plate carrier, worn; the boss always has a rifle with a red dot or scope, helmet and plates.
- **Aim (chosen by the user: "settling aim error"):** an enemy waits its reaction time before its first shot, then its aim wanders around you (smoothly, not random per shot) by an error that shrinks the longer it watches you. A target moving fast and its own movement keep the error up, and being hit makes it flinch (+3°). Seeing you again **where it last saw you** takes only a 0.15 s reaction; seeing you **somewhere else** (2 m or more) after over a second takes its full reaction, with its aim off by about how far you went. So popping back out of the same cover is dangerous, and repositioning is how you win. It aims at your chest, or at your head when only that shows over cover, and pulls against all its recoil, up and sideways (2026-11-04, the user's call: harder gun recoil must not cost enemies accuracy).
- **Firing:** automatics in bursts (3–6 rounds close, 2–3 beyond 15 m), semi-automatics in single shots, with pauses between that grow with distance. It aims down the sights beyond 12 m. **It never fires when another enemy is in the line of fire**; if its spread hits one anyway, that does damage but doesn't turn them on each other.
- **Fighting in the open:** it closes in until within 30% of its gun's range (at most 45 m), then sidesteps left and right at half pace. After 3–6 s in the open, when it's hit, or when its magazine is empty, it looks for **cover**: a reachable spot within 10 m where you can't see it crouched (with a 0.4 m margin either side), at least 4 m from you, not taken by another enemy. It reloads there. Over **low cover** it crouches and peeks by standing up; behind full cover it peeks by walking back to where it was fighting from. If you can see it in its cover, it gives up on that cover.
- **Losing sight (reachable):** it either **pushes** to where it last saw you or **holds the angle** (by its type's chance, and only if it can see that spot), then searches: after 4 s pushing or 8 s holding.
- **Out of reach** (you're somewhere it can't walk to, like a roof reached by ladder): while it sees you it fights as usual. Once it loses sight it finds the **way up** (the closest spot it can reach), moves to a spot within 10 m that's hidden from you and watches the way up, aimed, for 8 s, then moves to another spot within 8 m of it and holds again. **It gives up after 45 s without seeing you** and goes back to its patrol.
- **Allies' shots (chosen by the user: "join the fight"):** an enemy that hears another enemy firing joins in: within 15% of the shot's loudness it's alert to whoever its ally is fighting (they call it out); within half it walks over to **where the shots came from** (the ally), not to the ally's target, and finds you from there by sight like anyone else (changed 2026-10-30: before, every enemy within half a rifle's loudness, 90 m, walked straight to you). Walls muffle allies' shots too. Further away, or a shot from an enemy that isn't fighting anyone, it ignores. Suppressed enemies pull in fewer friends.
- **The dead stay down** (bodies for session 11's looting). The test page's `?respawn` brings them back for testing.
- **Looting bodies and loot weighting moved to session 11** (the user's call): they need items, inventory and loot tables (sessions 9–11).

- **Doors (decided 2026-10-26, for session 11, now 11c):** AI opens doors. A door is **open or closed in the simulation, with nothing in between**: opening one changes its collision at once, for players and AI alike, and the renderer animates the swing (about 0.2 s) for looks only, so nothing can get caught on a half-open door, and co-op only syncs one flag per door. A closed, unlocked door stays a link in the navigation grid, so paths go through it and an enemy opens it when it gets there. **Opening a door makes no noise** for AI, for now (the user's call). **Enemies never open locked doors**, even carrying the key (the user's call, 2026-10-26). But **unlocking is for the rest of the raid**: once a player unlocks a door, it's an ordinary door that anyone can open, enemies included. So a key room is safe only until you open it; after that, enemies can follow you in.
  - **Decided with the user in session 11c (2026-10-27):** an **open door is solid too**: it snaps to 90° from its hinge, on the side it opens towards, and stops bodies, shots and sight like a wall (no shooting or seeing past a door either way). Opening one into someone pushes them aside. **Keys have limited uses** (`uses` on the key item; left out, it never wears out), and are gone after the last; the key counts anywhere you carry it, the vault included, and the most worn one is used first. A locked door with its key carried unlocks and opens in one press. **Only players close doors**, and not on anyone standing in the doorway; enemies open doors in their way and leave them open. An unlocked door never locks again.
  - **Doors swing away from whoever opens them** (the user's request, 2026-10-30): from outside a house inwards, from inside outwards, for enemies as well. So an open door is always on the far side of its doorway from where it was opened, and closing it is done from that side too (it stays where it swung until closed). A door that starts open stands on the side its map yaw gives.
  - The use button says **open**, **close**, **unlock** or **locked** at a door under the crosshair within 2 m; a locked one names its key under the crosshair ("Locked · needs Shed key" or "Unlock with Shed key"). Looking through an open door's doorway counts as aiming at the door, so it can be closed with its slab off to the side, but anything else in reach comes first. A key's uses left show on its inventory tile (×n).

The user's notes on Tarkov's AI are the inspiration; names, uniforms, voices and looks are our own. Built in the AI rework sessions in roadmap.md, bosses last.
- **No mercy for phone players** (the user's call: "I do it… this isn't much harder than Delta Force"). Tune for the design, not for touch controls. **Northpoint is a hard map**, not a starter map.
- **Factions:** every enemy type has a `faction`: `scav`, `pmc` or `boss`. Scavs and bosses (with their followers) are allies; PMCs are hostile to everyone; everyone is hostile to players. They fight whoever they see but **don't go looking for each other**: on gunfire each heads for it on its own, and if they meet on the way they fight. An ally's shots join it in the fight (as now); another faction's shots are a noise to check, which is how a scav fight draws PMCs in as a third party.
- **Hearing scale per map** (the user's call, to try): a map's `hearingScale` multiplies every noise's loudness there. Guns are heard 110–190 m, about all of Northpoint (some 195 × 190 m, crossed in about 35 s sprinting), so without it every fight is everyone's. Northpoint starts at 0.6. If that isn't enough, muffling that counts each wall and floor in the way comes next.
- **Built in AI-1 (2026-11-07), with these calls of Claude's (open to change):**
  - **Footsteps aren't scaled** by the hearing scale, and **enemies hear only players' footsteps**. Footsteps are already short (15 m sprinting, 6 m walking), scaling them would make a hard map easier up close, and hearing each other's steps would have them tracking each other down, which the design rules out. Shots, blasts and breaking glass are scaled.
  - **Two PMCs are hostile to each other** unless they're in the same squad (AI-3); other squads stay hostile, like other players would be.
  - **A hostile enemy's shot is treated exactly like a player's:** a near miss or a shot within 15% of its loudness alarms the listener to the shooter; further out it's a noise to check, capped at 2 responders. The responder cap counts only the listener's allies, so a PMC going to check doesn't stop scavs coming too.
  - Scavs and bosses still never hurt each other; a hostile's rounds, blasts and fire land like a player's.

**Scavs: the alarm bells.**
- Local thugs who **guard or patrol fixed areas** (as now). Erratic and uncoordinated: sudden stops, crouches and short sprints at random. A narrower field of view than PMCs (about 100°; everyone has 120° now).
- **Civilian clothes:** an enemy type can list several outfits, one picked per spawn: bright jackets, tracksuits, mismatched hoodies.
- **Gear:** no armour or soft armour, simple rigs, small bags, unmodded guns, low-penetration rounds (levels 1–3, as `scav-kit`). **Loot:** mostly household, electronics and basic computer parts; medical rare, military very rare.
- **Noisy:** never suppressed, and loud in a fight, so a scav fight tells the map where people are.
- **Waves:** about a third of the scav slots fill at raid start; the rest arrive in waves every few minutes, into their own sectors (the same `oneOf` slots).
- **Replenishing:** a dead scav frees its slot, filled again in a later wave. A new scav never appears in sight of any player or within a minimum distance of one (the `exposedSpawns` tests, checked when it spawns), and a slot's spot away from players is preferred. Refills slow late in the raid and stop at about twice the slot count per raid, so a corner can't be farmed forever. A resumed raid keeps its queue of waves.
- **Gunfire:** one noise still draws at most 2 scavs (19zj, the user's fix for single-file streams through a held door). Firing that keeps on in the same area draws one more about every 30 s, the nearest free scav, often from the next sector: a trickle, not a stampede.

- **Built in AI-2 (2026-11-07), with these calls of Claude's (open to change; the numbers are first guesses, in `SCAV_WAVES` and `AI` in the sim):**
  - **A slot is a sector:** a `oneOf` group of scav spawns, or a scav spawn on its own. Its `chance` is rolled once at raid start, for whether the slot is used at all. Every time it fills, it picks any spot in the sector that's clear of players, so a refill can come in somewhere else in the sector. Only scavs come in waves; bosses (and PMCs later) are all in at the start.
  - **Waves:** a third of the slots (rounded up), picked at random, at raid start; the rest in waves of that size every 3 minutes (each wave up to 30 s early or late). A map can set its own share (`scavStartShare`). **Northpoint starts half** (the user's call, since it's a hard map): of its 12 slots, 6 at the start and 6 at about 3 minutes.
  - **Refills:** a freed slot waits at least 2 minutes, ×(1 + 2 × the share of the raid gone) (about 6 minutes at the end), then comes with the next wave. None after the timer, and **24 spawns at most on Northpoint** (twice the slots, the start's included).
  - **Clear of players:** never in a living player's sight (out to the 70 m sight range) nor within 30 m. A spot 60 m or more from everyone is picked first. With no clear spot, the slot tries again every 10 s. The first scavs keep to these rules too where their sector allows, and the map's spawn check covers the rest.
  - **The trickle:** gunfire within 20 m of the last shot counts as the same fight, and so does anyone's, scavs' own included. Every 30 s it keeps on, it draws the nearest free scav (not fighting and not already going to check something) that's within the loudest shot's loudness after the map's scale. That scav checks it with care, as for any gunfire. After 15 s without a shot the fight is forgotten.
  - **Erratic:** an enemy type's `erratic` (scavs 1). About every 6 s (between 3 and 9) while walking a patrol, investigating, searching or loitering: a stop of 0.6–1.8 s (35%), a crouch-walk of 1.2–3 s (35%) or a sprint of 0.8–2 s (30%). While fighting, only crouches, as often. Never while running from a grenade or out of fire.
  - **Field of view:** an enemy type's `fov`; scavs 100°, everyone else 120°.
  - **Low-quality gear (the user's call):** `scav-kit` guns are unmodded (no attachments at all). Rounds are level 1, sometimes level 2 (3 to 1), and shotguns get buckshot only. Armour is none, or level 1, rarely level 2 (bump or riot helmet, soft or police vest), worn to 30–80%.
  - **Civilian outfits:** 8 of them (a red jacket and jeans, navy and teal tracksuits, a mustard hoodie, a purple hoodie, an orange jacket, a grey hoodie, the old olive one), with a range of skin tones.
  - **Loot:** household 4, electronics 3, basic computer parts 1.5, medical 0.4, military 0.15 (a military item about 1 roll in 60).

**PMCs: the roaming predators.**
- Other raiders on the same job as you, behaving like players. **3–6 per raid, all at the start, in squads of 2–4**, at player spawns nobody is using (the sight rule applies). **They don't replenish.**
- **Look:** a tactical uniform per faction. No knee pads or armbands (the user's call).
- **Gear:** armour level 3–5 (5 rare), worn 60–100%; tactical rigs, mid to large backpacks; modded guns (suppressors, optics, grips). **Rounds are capped:** mostly levels 2–3, level 4 sometimes, rarely higher. Deadly to the level 3 starting kit, so gearing up in level 4–5 armour pays off.
- **Aim:** between the scav and the boss (about 0.45 s reaction, 0.4° settled), first guesses.
- **Routes:** maps get `poi` markers (loot, choke or extraction, with a weight). A squad walks a chain of them along loot lanes and ends at an extraction, where it **leaves the raid with what it carries**.
- **Looting:** at a loot spot they search containers (which then show as searched) and take items into their rig and backpack, which is their limit; they walk to bodies they've killed and search those the same way. They never change their own gear. **What they take is on their body:** follow them and kill them for it.
- **Seeing you:** a callout (a voice line) when one spots you, then fire after its reaction time; break line of sight in that moment and it may not shoot. **The whole squad knows where you are at once.**
- **Hearing gunfire**, as a share of the shot's loudness after the map's scale: within 20% alert; within 70% it stops looting and advances; out to 100% it turns and heads for the next point of interest that way. A squad decides as one and doesn't count towards the scavs' 2-responder limit. Squads converging on a long fight is intended; suppressors are the counter. With no sound or sight of you for 15–20 s, a hunt ends and the squad goes back to its route at the nearest point of interest.
- **Fighting:** less time in cover than scavs, and they sprint to it. **Hearing you heal or reload** (by its sound within about 10 m, not by knowing) makes them push round your corner, so a fake heal can bait them. **Squad roles:** one or two hold and fire at your cover (your last known position), the rest flank to a spot that sees it from at least 45° off. **Frag grenades** into hard cover or your last known position, one at a time per area from a shared token. They already run from grenades and don't shoot while running, so a grenade back breaks their hold.
- **Other factions:** they fight scavs and bosses on contact, and push into a boss's ground if their route takes them there.
- **Built in AI-3 (2026-11-07), with these calls of Claude's (open to change; numbers in `SQUAD` and `PMC_SQUADS` in the sim):**
  - **How many:** a map's `pmcs` setting, fewest and most (an editor field under the scav share). **Northpoint has 3–6.** Split at random into squads of 2–4 (never a lone PMC unless the map asks for exactly 1).
  - **Where:** each squad at its own player spawn that's out of every player's sight and at least 30 m from them, preferring 60 m+, and at least 50 m from and out of sight of the other squads. With too few such spawns, fewer squads; on Northpoint (9 spawns) there have always been enough. The leader stands on the spawn, the rest in their places behind it.
  - **On a map without points of interest, a squad guards its spawn** like a scav guard: the leader looks about, the rest stand in formation each watching its own side. With them, it walks a route (AI-4, below).
  - **Formation:** the first two followers 3 m back and 2.5 m to either side, watching 60° out to their side; the third 6 m back in the middle, watching behind. When the leader turns back on itself, they keep to their own side of the way rather than cross over. They hurry the further off their place they are.
  - **The squad moves as one:** a follower that hears something tells its leader, who goes to check it (with the squad following), unless it's already fighting or checking something. A leader that dies hands over to the next in line, who carries on from where it stands.
  - **"The whole squad knows":** the moment one member is alert to someone (sees them, or was just hit by them), every member is, and those fighting them out of sight are told where they are for as long as a mate can see them. Each still has its own reaction time before firing once it sees you. On Northpoint, a squad of 3–4 that saw you at 10–25 m was alert on one tick and all of them fired.
  - **Kit (`pmc-kit`):** M416, AK-74M, the 7.62×39 rifle, MPX, MP7, SCAR-H, AS VAL and Saiga-12, each with optics, a muzzle device and a bigger magazine by chance (suppressed about half the time). Rounds about 3 parts level 2, 4 parts level 3, 1.5 level 4 and 0.3 level 5. Helmets none, level 3 or 4 (level 5 rare); body armour level 3 or 4 (level 5 rare), all worn to 60–100%. A chest rig and a small, medium or large backpack. Loot: medical, ammo, some military, a little household and electronics.
  - **Look:** one ranger-green uniform, black gloves and brown boots, over five skin tones. Named "PMC" in the damage log for now (open question below).
  - **Aim:** reaction 0.45 s, first error 5°, settled 0.4°, settling in 1.2 s, holds an angle half the time; the full 120° view and not erratic.
- **Built in AI-4 (2026-11-07), with these calls of Claude's (open to change; numbers in `ROUTES` in `sim/routes.ts`):**
  - **Points of interest:** a map's `poi` markers, each a **loot** spot (its containers within its radius, 8 m by default, are searched) or a **choke** (stood at 4–8 s, looking about), with a weight. Placed in the editor's marker tool. **Extractions are the map's own extraction zones**, not `poi`s: one set of data, so PMCs follow whatever the user does with extractions.
  - **The route is made up as it goes, not planned at the start:** from each stop the next is picked by weight among those it hasn't been to, favouring a walk of 15–70 m over the one next door or the far end of the map. That's what lets far gunfire turn it, and a fight put it back on at the nearest stop it hasn't done.
  - **When it leaves:** from a time picked at raid start, between 35% and 80% of the raid, it heads out after the stop it's at; with 3 minutes left it heads out at once. So PMCs are about for most of a raid but thin out late, and it differs per squad. It goes for an always-open extraction it can **walk into now**: one behind a locked door or an unopened switch shutter doesn't count (they don't use switches). With none, it stays in the raid and roams on. Each member leaves after standing in the zone the same 7 s as a player, with what it carries; a follower far behind isn't waited for.
  - **Looting:** the leader and followers share out a loot stop's containers, nearest first. Searching takes 0.6 s an item (at least 1.5 s); then the container shows as searched, as a player's search would, and it takes what fits into its rig and backpack, most valuable for its size first. It leaves a container the squad has searched, or one already searched and empty. The leader walks to bodies the squad killed within 40 m and searches their pockets and bags the same way. **Worn gear and guns on a body aren't taken** (it never changes its own gear, and taking yours off your body is left for later). A loot stop is left after 75 s at most.
  - **Hearing gunfire:** within 20% of a shot's loudness, alert; within 70%, it stops looting and goes to check it with care, and isn't held to the scavs' 2-responder cap (its followers tell the leader, so the squad goes as one); out to 100%, the leader turns its route to the nearest stop that way (within 60° of it and nearer the shots), without stopping. Blasts and glass are heard as before.
  - **The hunt:** having lost you, a squad member searches on until 15–20 s after it last saw you, then goes back to its route (or its place behind the leader).
  - **Northpoint (2026-11-07, Claude's first pass, for the user to reshape):** 28 points of interest in the group "PMC points of interest": 15 loot spots where the containers are now (yard crates ×2 weight, gun store ×2, front offices ×1.5, the admin office, warehouse office, security office, staff lockers, the upper east lockers, laser tag's crate, music store, vault offices, upper tech store, jewellery, key shop, and the staff fridge at ×0.5) and 13 chokes at the scavs' areas and crossings (atrium, food court, department store aisles, furniture showroom and market, warehouse racks, dock hall, service corridor, south stores, both ends of the upper ring, the upper west run, the yard containers). All on the AI's main nav area. Loot isn't placed yet, so the loot spots will move with it.
- **Built in AI-5 (2026-11-08), with these calls of Claude's (open to change; numbers in `TACTICS` in `sim/ai.ts`, and the PMC's skill):**
  - **The callout:** a squad member that spots someone by sight shouts once (a generated two-syllable bark, the type's `voice.callout`); a squad calls out once even when several see you together, and not again within 4 s. Not when it's only shot at or told by a mate. It's a warning, not a delay: it fires after its reaction time as before. PMCs call out on scavs too, so you hear a PMC fight start.
  - **Cover:** a PMC stays in cover 0.6–1.4 s before peeking (scavs 1.5–3 s), and sprints to cover more than 2 m away, turning to face you for the last of it. Data on the enemy type (`coverTime`, `sprintToCover`), so Jug can use none.
  - **Hearing you reload or heal:** within 10 m of you, walls or not (as footsteps are), by your reload or med starting, not by knowing. Fighting you out of sight (or searching for you), it knows where you are and pushes at you for 5 s, sprinting until 4 m off and coming round the corner aimed, and doesn't take cover meanwhile. Not fighting you, it goes to check, like footsteps. A repair kit counts as a heal. Players only, like footsteps.
  - **Squad roles:** once the whole squad has lost sight of its target for 1 s, each takes a part, once per time it loses it. By place, the last half (rounded down) flank and the rest hold: 2 is one and one, 3 is two holding and one flanking, 4 is two and two. A lone survivor fights on as anyone would.
  - **Holding:** it stays where it is and fires short bursts (2–4 rounds, 0.8–1.6 s apart) at where it lost you, for 6–10 s, but only where its rounds would hit within 2.5 m of that spot (your cover), not a wall in front of it. A mate who can see you keeps its aim on you. Then it searches.
  - **Flanking:** it goes to a spot 6–18 m from where it lost you that sees that spot standing (and 0.4 m either side), at least 45° off the line from the holders, the nearest of 16 tried. It sprints while more than 8 m off, then comes up aimed, and watches for 6 s before searching; 20 s at most to get there. With no such spot it pushes in as before.

- **PMCs reworked after AI-8 (2026-11-08, the user's calls; numbers in `TACTICS` and the PMC's skill):**
  - **Scavs only when they meet them (the user's call):** a PMC squad fights any scav or boss it sees, that shoots near it, hits it, or that a squad mate is fighting, but **no longer goes to check other AI's gunfire, blasts, glass or kicked doors**. Players' noises draw them as before. Scavs and bosses stay allies of each other (Jug and the yard boss are scav bosses, as in Tarkov), as they have been since AI-1.
  - **Longer bursts (the user's call):** PMCs fire 4–8 rounds 0.15–0.35 s apart (the pause still grows with distance), from the enemy skill's new optional `burst` and `burstPause`; scavs keep 2–6 rounds 0.35–0.8 s apart. Holding at your cover, every enemy now fires 4–8 rounds 0.3–0.7 s apart (it was 2–4 rounds, 0.8–1.6 s).
  - **Holders move to where their rounds land (an AI-5 loose end):** a holder whose rounds can't get within 2.5 m of where it lost you for 1 s moves, aimed, to the nearest spot within 8 m (at least 6 m from you, not another's spot) from which they can, then fires from there.
  - **Flankers go round (an AI-5 loose end):** a flanker's path keeps 4.5 m from where it lost you where it can (each metre inside costs 2 more), so it doesn't walk past you on the way to its spot.
  - **Rival squads keep apart early (the user's call: PMC against PMC is fine, but not all in the first minutes):** for the first 6 minutes after the squads start, a squad picking its next stop takes one within 35 m of another squad's members, or of the stop that squad is heading for, only 3% as often. Afterwards routes are as before, so squads still meet later in the raid. Two squads that see each other across open ground (Northpoint's ground floor gives 60 m+ sightlines) still fight early: routes don't avoid each other's sight.

## Inventory and UI
- **A grid inventory with item sizes** (Tarkov-style), designed for touch:
  - Auto-placement puts an item in the first free space. Items can be rotated.
  - **Double-tap** moves an item between your inventory and the open container (or stash), fitting it automatically.
  - **Touch: scroll, or hold to lift (UX-1, 2026-11-08):** a finger on an item that moves within **200 ms** scrolls the panel instead; one held still for 200 ms **lifts** the item (it pops up under the finger, with a shadow) and then drags it. A mouse still drags as soon as it moves. 200 ms is under the double-tap's 350 ms, so both taps of a double-tap stay taps. A lifted item let go without moving is a tap (its details open), not a drop on itself. A finger moving sideways before the lift lets go too (nothing scrolls sideways). Android's long-press menu is blocked while a finger holds an item.
- Backpacks and rigs have their own grids. **No containers inside containers.**

## Tutorial (planned 2026-11-09 with the user)
A short scripted first run, about 60–90 s, that starts on the first launch for a new player. It's not a raid: it's **offline, before signing in**, with a fixed kit (the starter M4 setup), and **nothing is kept**. Finishing (or skipping) it leads to creating an account, then the menu with the real starting kits. It can be skipped from its first prompt and replayed from settings. It's a scripted corridor, so the tactical rules in [level-design.md](level-design.md) don't apply, but its movement numbers do.

The interior of a house, in order:
1. **Wake up, move and look.** A prompt that fades once you've moved a few metres.
2. **Fallen debris to jump or vault over** (inside the vault rules: 0.45–1.1 m high, 0.8 m clear above the top).
3. **A furniture barricade with a gap at the bottom middle to crouch through** (under 1.8 m and at least 1.2 m tall), **with barbed wire around the gap**: crawling through gives you a light bleed.
4. **A bandage just past it, with something bright pointing at it** (a marker or arrow). Pick it up and the heal button is the next prompt; the step ends when the bleed stops.
5. **A scav standing idle, facing away.** He's deaf and doesn't notice you. Shoot him with the M4. **If you hit him without killing him, he turns and fires back, but his shots do 0 damage.** You still get the full feedback of being shot (below), so you learn what it feels like with nothing at stake. A reload prompt after he dies.
6. **Loot his body.** His **pocket has the front door key**. The prompt walks you through opening the body and moving the key into your gear.
7. **The locked front door.** The key opens it, and you step out into an **extraction zone**. The countdown and the normal results screen teach "extract" as the goal.

**Being shot (Tarkov-like, the user's call; built in TU-1, 2026-11-09):** in every raid, any hit by a round, a knife or a blast, even one doing 0 damage, sets off all of these: the red screen edge flash, **aim punch**, a **direction marker**, the **hit sound** (the thud of the round or blade, and a grunt, one of each per quarter second) and controller rumble. A fall only does when it hurts; burning doesn't. **Aim punch throws where your shots go**, not only the view: 1.5° plus 0.05° per HP the hit was worth (lost plus stopped by armour), at most 5° however many hits land at once, mostly upwards, then it springs back at your gun's snap. The view also rolls a little sideways and settles. Only players are punched; enemies' aim is unchanged. **The direction marker** is a red arc on a ring around the crosshair pointing to where the hit came from (the shooter, the swinger, the blast); it turns with you, holds half a second and fades over a second, and hits from about the same way share one arc.

Prompts name the input in use: touch button outlines, controller glyphs, or keys on desktop.

**On screen (TU-3, 2026-11-09; Claude's calls, easy to change):** a first launch on a device that hasn't seen the tutorial, with nobody signed in (or no server), goes straight into it; signing in marks it seen, so a player with an account isn't sent in. The prompt is a card at the top left under the timer, with the step count, worded for the input in use and changing with it; with the bag open it sits in the tab bar. The step's touch buttons pulse gold, and a gold chevron with its distance points at the step's spot (the bandage, the front door). On the first card, touch has a **Skip tutorial** button; a mouse or pad is told to skip from settings, where Skip tutorial stays for the whole tutorial. Finishing (the results screen's Continue) or skipping both go to **Create an account** (or, signed in, the menu). Killed in it, the results also offer Try again. **Settings → Play the tutorial again** from the main menu replays it.

**The house as built (TU-2, 2026-11-09; Claude's calls, easy to change):** one straight run along a 6 m wide, 32 m long house with a 2.8 m ceiling, `content/maps/tutorial.json`. You wake by the bed in the bedroom; through a doorway, a ceiling joist and plaster have come down across the hall (0.77 m high, 0.8 m deep: a vault); then wardrobes, a bookcase, an upturned table and a mattress fill the hall to the ceiling, leaving a 1.2 × 1.4 m gap at the bottom middle with a coil of barbed wire in it (and coils in front of the furniture). The bandage lies just past it. Through the next doorway, the living room: the scav stands with his back to you watching the TV, about 6 m in. The front door is in the living room's outside wall, and the fenced (hedged) front yard outside it is the only extraction. **The kit** is the starter's (M416 with a red dot, M9A3, helmet, vest, chest rig, backpack) with 90 rifle and 30 pistol rounds and **no meds**: the bandage you pick up is the one you use. **The raid lasts 30 minutes** so time is never short. **The scav** has an M9A3 and no armour, so a few rounds drop him, and his pocket holds only the **front door key** (no random loot). He's **deaf** and **passive** (he notices nothing, by sight or sound, until he's hit or set alight), **harmless** (his hits do nothing but are felt in full: flash, punch, marker, sound), and put in **once** (killed, nothing replaces him). **Barbed wire** works in any map: touching it (standing, crouched or prone) starts one leg bleeding, once each time you go in. **The steps:** move 3 m; climb over the debris; crouch through the gap (ends past the barricade); pick up the bandage (marked); heal until nothing bleeds; kill the scav; reload; search his body; take the key; unlock the front door (marked); extract. The tutorial's raid is **offline**: not in the raid list, and the server won't start it.

## Controls and platform behaviour
- A standard twin-stick touch layout. **The layout can be adjusted in settings and is saved.**
- **Button layouts (UX-4, 2026-11-08):** settings → Buttons keeps **3 saved layouts**, one equipped (used in raids); "Edit layout N" opens the editor on the equipped one. Each layout has its own places, sizes and **hidden buttons**: the editor's Hide/Show takes a button off the HUD (it shows faded while editing, so it can come back). **Fire, use and bag can't be hidden**, since on touch nothing else fires, loots or opens the bag. Put out and detonate can be hidden, though only they do what they do (the user's call, 2026-11-08). **Share codes:** each layout has a code (`MES1-` then base64url, from about 8 characters for the default layout to about 140 for every button moved) to copy and paste into a chat; "Paste a code" loads one into the equipped layout, asking first if that layout isn't the default. No server: positions are fractions of the screen, so a code fits any phone. A code has a check byte, so a mistyped or cut-off one is refused, and a code from a newer build skips the buttons this one doesn't know. The format is `hud/layout-code.ts`; control numbers there are append-only. **Settings:** `layouts` (3 slots of `placements` and `hidden`) and `equippedLayout` replace `layout`; an old `layout` becomes slot 1 when loaded, with no format bump, so every other field still falls back on its own. Layouts are named Layout 1–3, with no custom names (the user's call, 2026-11-08).
- **Aim and fire behaviour** are chosen in settings (hold vs. toggle to aim, fire options). Default: hold, with automatic fire.
  - **"Automatic fire" means auto-fire on target** (clarified 2026-10-26, session 5): the gun fires by itself while the crosshair is on an enemy, like CoD Mobile's simple mode. The other option is "fire button only". The fire button works in both modes.
  - Session 6: "on an enemy" means the crosshair ray (with recoil) hits a living character's hit zone within the gun's range, with no wall in the way. With a semi-automatic gun, auto-fire pulls the trigger as fast as the gun allows.
- **Aim assist:** aim slows slightly when passing over an enemy. It's a setting, defaulting to low. Session 6: look speed × 0.8 (low), 0.6 (medium) or 0.45 (high) while the crosshair is on an enemy. **It never slows pulling down against recoil (the user's call, 2026-11-08):** during a burst (until the trigger has been let go 0.25 s, so auto-fire and semi taps count) it slows only turning sideways; up and down stay at full speed. Before, low assist made an aimed AKM magazine need about 145 px of drag instead of 115.
- **Touch layout (session 5):** the move stick floats where the left thumb lands on the left half; dragging on the right half looks. **The fire button also looks:** dragging the thumb on it turns the view, so you can track a target while shooting. ## Art direction
- Unturned-like: low-poly, solid flat colours, **no real lighting**.
- For readability, each face direction gets a slightly different shade (tops lighter, sides darker), plus **distance fog** (which also lets far-away objects go undrawn).
- Items and props are built from simple shapes (boxes, cylinders) in the editors.
- **Buildings follow `admin-building`** (the user's call, 2026-11-03: their buildings will copy it). A prefab with a `building` footprint and named colour slots (walls, partition, floor, roof, trim, door, glass, stairs, metal); one group per floor (Ground floor, Upper floor, Roof, Fire escape); outer walls 0.2 m and partitions 0.15 m, as `wall` nodes with their openings and a `door` in each doorway; storeys 3.2 m (a 0.2 m slab, then walls 3.0 m on the ground floor and 2.8 m above); windows 0.9 m sill to 2.0 m, glazed, so they're vaultable once broken; doorways 1.2 × 2.1 m; **rooms about 1.25× real size** (the user's call, 2026-11-03, after walking it: breathing room for the camera and thumbsticks), so the admin building is 15 × 10 m with a 3.5 m hall; inside stairs through a hole in the floor above, railed; **two ways up** (inside stairs and a fire escape) so the upper floor isn't a dead end. Built empty: furniture and loot are placed afterwards.

## Sound (session 19, decided with the user 2026-10-30)
- **Generated, not recorded:** every sound is a recipe (layers of noise or tones, each with an envelope and an optional filter) that the game renders itself before first playing it. Almost nothing to download, and in keeping with the flat art. Guns and impacts will sound simpler than recordings; that's accepted.
- **Recipes are content** (`content/sounds/`), tuned on the `/sounds` page. Guns, the knife, meds and repair kits **name their own sounds** in their item files, so a new gun can sound different without code; the game names the rest (footsteps, doors, the HUD).
- **What you hear is what enemies hear:** a shot is heard as far as its loudness, the same distance AI hears it from, so a suppressor quietens it for you too (a shot under 75% of its gun's loudness plays the gun's suppressed sound).
- **Where it comes from:** quieter and duller with distance, left or right by direction, a little duller from behind, and muffled through walls (one ray from your head to the sound when it starts). Your own sounds play centred, and your footsteps quieter than others'.
- **Footsteps** tell you who's near: crouching carries about 8 m, walking 20 m, sprinting 35 m. Also landing, ladders, doors (opening, closing, rattling locked, unlocking), reloads, meds and repairs starting, a body falling, an enemy's bullet passing close, being hurt and killed, the extraction countdown (a beep a second), extracting, investigating, the timer going under two minutes, inventory moves and refusals, menu taps, and money changing hands.
- A **volume** setting (Settings → Sound). At most 16 sounds play at once; past that the quietest gives way.

## Trust model
A sandbox. The client is trusted. The server only applies sanity limits (for example, money can't jump from 20 to 9 trillion; item counts and values stay within reasonable bounds). No raid replay or loot validation. Revisit only if ranked challenges or friend trading get abused.
- **The stash is saved whole** (session 13): each change is applied on the phone and the profile sent a moment later, through the same limits as any edit between raids. If another device changed the profile meanwhile, or the server refuses the change, **the server's copy wins** and the stash reloads with a note saying so (unsaved changes on this phone are lost). With no connection a raid can't start until it is saved (the stash page's Saved / Saving… / Not saved label was taken out on 2026-11-09, the user's request; the refusal note when starting a raid still says why).
- **Session 12's limits:** every item must exist, be within its stack, durability, rounds and uses, and fit where it is (its gear slot, or inside its grid with no overlaps and no containers in containers); pockets, vault and stash are their usual sizes; money is at most 1 billion. A raid's result may report at most the raid's time plus a minute, and up to 1,000 kills. **Edits between raids can't make items or change money:** there must be no more of any item than before (attachments on guns count), and only vendors (on the server, session 13b) change money. **Session 13b:** a stash edit can't mend armour by more durability than repair kits lost points (`overRepaired`). There's still no value limit on what a raid brings out: grid space bounds it (a raid can carry out a few hundred thousand in cash at most).
- **Accounts (session 12, the user's call 2026-10-27): username and password.** Passkeys and magic links were the alternatives (passkeys need HTTPS even in dev; magic links need an email sender and our own domain). No password reset without email: reset one by hand with `wrangler d1 execute`. Registering is open to anyone (no invite code, the user's call 2026-10-27).

## Characters (confirmed 2026-10-26)
- **No blocky cube people.** The base body is a rigged, animated **low-poly humanoid from a CC0 asset pack** (for example Quaternius), recoloured to flat colours to match the art style. No 3D modelling is needed from the user.
- **What you see on an enemy is what they carry:** helmets, armour, backpacks and weapons are the same models built in the item editor, attached to the body. You can read how dangerous an enemy is, and roughly what they'll drop, before you engage.
- **Factions and bosses** are told apart by uniform colours and gear, and bosses also by size or a distinctive item.
- **The player in first person** sees only arms and the gun. The full body is the same character system, which a co-op partner sees.
- **Animations** come from the pack: idle, walk, run, aim, shoot, reload, death. No ragdolls.
- **Session 8b (2026-10-26):** the pack is Quaternius's **Universal Animation Library (Standard)**, CC0 (chosen with the user). It's one mannequin with 45 clips, including pistol idle, aim, shoot and reload, but **no rifle clips**. So a gun is held by IK: the gun sits on a weapon bone in front of the right shoulder, from the low ready (tipped down) to aimed (shouldered) as the character aims, turned with where it looks, and both hands reach for it. While reloading or sprinting, and for distant bodies, the gun is carried in the right hand by the clip instead. A rifle reload uses the pistol reload clip, so the rifle points up for it. Enemy types pick their **outfit colours** (skin, top, bottom, hands, feet) in their files; a body's colours come from which bone moves each face most.
- Gear is drawn with its item models (session 18b; the placeholders of session 9 became the first models): helmet, body armour, rig, backpack, and the same gun as first person. Mount points are bones: helmet on the head, armour and rig on the chest, the backpack on the upper chest, and the gun on its own weapon bone.
- **First-person arms** are low-poly forearms and hands in the player's outfit, drawn with the gun in its pass. From the hip the gun hides most of them; better framing waits for real gun models, now made in the item editor (session 18b).

## Editors (admin only, desktop browser)
The editors run on the Mac in a desktop browser and save to JSON files in the repo. The game itself is played on iOS.

**Map editor**
- **Snapping:** adjustable grid snap (coarse for buildings, fine for props, or off). Rotation at any angle, with adjustable angle snapping and **"match the rotation of the selected object"**.
- **Placement:** objects **snap down onto the surface** below (a chair onto the floor, a lamp onto a table). Overlapping props get a **red warning** but are allowed; **buildings can't overlap other buildings**.
- **Prefabs** (cars, trees, trash cans, mailboxes, whole buildings) are placed by reference, so editing a prefab updates every copy. Individual copies can be recoloured.
  - **Session 16b (the user's calls, 2026-10-27):** a prefab is **made from a selection**, which is replaced by one copy of it standing where it was, and **edited by entering a placed copy** on a map (there's no prefab-only editor). A change to a prefab that would **break any map is refused at save**, not only the open one. A placed copy can be **unpacked** into a group of its parts, to make a one-off variant without changing every copy (it then isn't a building: no concrete base).
- **Editing safety:** a placed prefab or building is selected and deleted as **one object**, and you can enter it to edit its parts. Full **undo/redo**. **The ground layer is locked** against accidental deletion.
  - **Groups (session 15, the user's call 2026-10-27):** anything can be grouped, and groups nest; a group is moved, turned, copied and deleted as one, and you enter it to edit inside. **Grouping never changes ids**, so quests and patrols that name a marker keep working. The ground layer is the terrain: it can't be selected, so it can't be deleted.
- **Terrain:** raise and lower brushes, water level, and automatic concrete bases under buildings.
  - **Session 16 (the user's calls, 2026-10-27):** a **building** is a prefab that says so, with its footprint (`building.size`); the base fills under that rectangle, and buildings' footprints may not overlap. **An edit that would make buildings overlap is refused** at once (the building shows red while dragged); other things that cut into each other get a red warning and are allowed. **Things standing on the ground move with a brush**; buildings stay put and their base grows or shrinks. Brushes: raise, lower, flatten and smooth.
- **Gameplay markers:** random player spawns, enemy spawns (type, loadout roll, loot weighting), loot containers and spawn points (loot table), extraction points, quest markers, doors and keys, named zones.
- **Navigation overlay:** shows where AI can walk, and **highlights spots where a player can stand but AI can't reach them or shoot them**, so you can fix them while building.
  - **Session 17 (the user's call, 2026-10-28):** a hiding spot is one enemies can't walk to **and** can't see a standing player on from anywhere they can walk within their 70 m sight range. Roofs they can shoot onto aren't flagged. It's worked out when you ask (a button), since it's thousands of rays; walkable and cut-off areas show live. Doors count as unlocked (a key room opens up once unlocked).
- **Easier editing (the user's call, 2026-10-30):** overlaps that are only **joints** (thin on two axes: wall corners, a wall through a slab) don't warn; a **cutaway** hides everything above a height, stepped a floor at a time, to work inside buildings; drags **snap to the faces of things beside them**; a **wall tool** draws walls a corner at a time and **cuts doors and windows** into them, windows sized to the vault rules (Movement). Walls and openings are plain boxes, so the game needs nothing new. **Curves** (the user's call, 2026-11-01, for the Mall's atrium): a curve tool draws curved walls, round floors with a round hole and curved stairs as plain boxes round a circle, the circle clicked being the inside edge; and **cuts a round hole in a floor**, with a railing, placed by number from the floor's middle so nothing is eyeballed. Floor slabs at the same level may overlap without a warning. **Quick floor and wall** buttons (2026-11-01) place thin boxes: a floor 0.2 m thick drawn by clicking two opposite corners (snapping to wall ends, like the wall tool), or, in one click, a 4 m wall at the wall tool's default height and thickness, for blocking out fast. **Named colours** (2026-11-01): the map's settings list its colour slots (wall, road…), added and recoloured there, and the toolbar's **paint** picks what new shapes, walls, floors and curves are coloured, a colour or a slot; a shape coloured by slot changes with it. ## Zombies (2026-11-04, the user's request and calls)
A separate game mode, round-based survival in the style of other wave shooters' zombie modes, alongside the extraction game. PvE only.
- **Separate from the stash:** everyone starts with the mode's fixed kit (a pistol, the knife, a chest rig and 9 mm); nothing from the stash goes in and nothing comes out, so it can't touch the extraction economy. **There's no inventory in it** (the bag button is gone; changed after the first play-test). It needs no server: it starts from the Zombies menu's **Start game** (solo only; it's disabled in a party) or `?zombies`, and the results screen shows the round reached, kills, points left and what hit you.
- **One health bar, not limbs (after the first play-test):** 150 HP, no broken limbs, no bleeding, no hit-zone multipliers. A zombie's claws take 50, so three hits kill. It refills (60 HP/s) once you've gone 3 s without being hurt.
- **Rounds are endless:** each has more zombies (6, then 3 more a round) and tougher ones (half a player's health in round 1, +15% a round). **They never get faster** (the user's call, 2026-11-04: running zombies came out; every zombie walks). At most 20 are alive at once; they appear one every 2 s, and there's a 10 s break between rounds (and before the first). You play until you die.
- **Zombies come from outside, through barricaded windows (after the first play-test):** they appear in the fenced yard round the building, walk up the paths to their window, tear its planks off one at a time (1.5 s each), then climb in over the sill and hunt you. A spawn names its window; it's used only while a living player can walk to that window's inside, so buying the door opens up the back room's windows. Players can never climb out. **Shots and claws go through a window:** you can shoot zombies through the planks, and a zombie at the window claws you if you stand in reach, so rebuilding while one is tearing at it costs health.
- **Barricades:** 6 planks a window. **Hold use** at a window short of planks and they go back up one at a time (0.8 s each); a full window takes no more. Each plank pays 10 points, up to 500 a player a round, so it can't be farmed.
- Inside, zombies are melee rushers: they always know where you are, take the shortest way to the nearest player they can walk to, and claw from close up. No guns, no hiding. Their bodies go 4 s after they die and have nothing to loot.
- **Points:** 500 to start, +10 for a hit (once per zombie per tick, so a shotgun blast is one hit), +60 a kill, +100 a knife kill, +10 a plank, all doubled under double points.

## Future (designed for now, built later)
- **Co-op is hard, like Tarkov (the user's call, 2026-11-01):** friendly fire is on, and there are no name tags, teammate health bars or map markers for teammates. Auto-fire and aim assist never target a teammate.
- **Enemies never hurt each other (the user's call, 2026-11-06):** an enemy's shot, knife, blast or fire does nothing to another enemy (`byEnemyOnEnemy` in `sim/health.ts`, checked in `woundZone` and `ignite`). They already only target players and hold fire with an ally in the line of fire, but that check is along the aim, so spread, recoil and shotgun pellets could still wound or kill an ally (a test fight of five poor shots took 63 HP off one in 30 s). The round still stops on the ally's body, so allies are still cover. Players still hurt enemies and each other, and enemies still hurt players.
- **Co-op vs. AI:** one player's device hosts the raid, running the same simulation code, and the others connect to it directly (WebRTC); guests predict their own movement. Phones and PCs can play together. **Changed 2026-10-31 (the user's call):** this replaces a raid server on Cloudflare (a Durable Object), whose free plan allows only a few raids a day. The plan is [coop.md](coop.md).
- **Weekly admin challenges:** a raid config that limits play to part of an existing map, sets an objective, and starts you with no items.
- **The Hangar (2026-11-07, designed with the user):** a base between raids whose modules you upgrade with loot and which craft gear over real time; it never boosts your character. The design is [hangar.md](hangar.md).
- **NPC flea market:** simulated listings with prices that move with supply and demand. Still to be explained and decided.
