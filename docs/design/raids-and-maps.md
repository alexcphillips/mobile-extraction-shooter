# Game design: Raids and maps

Part of the [design.md index](../design.md). Record decisions here, in the topic they belong to.

## Raids and maps
- Small maps, roughly 10–15 minutes to explore thoroughly.
- **Raid timer: 15 minutes by default**, set in the raid config. It's deliberately a little shorter than exploring the whole map, so you have to choose where to go and can't clear every corner. **Northpoint is 20 minutes** (the user's call, 2026-11-07): it's about 195 × 190 m but has 70-plus rooms worth entering, and a full sweep was estimated at 18–25 min before any fighting.
- **Player spawns:** the editor places several possible spawn points, and one is picked at random each raid. Co-op players spawn together at the same point. Enemy spawns near player spawns should be avoided (a minimum distance). **Built 2026-10-30 (the user's call, after "enemies see you from everywhere"):** an enemy spawn within 60 m of a player spawn, or in sight of one (within 70 m with a clear line between standing eyes), is a warning in the map editor's panel, and a content test keeps every map in `content/` free of them. Player spawns go on a map's edges, Tarkov-style; enemies hold the points of interest in the middle.
- **A raid is a data config:** map + playable area + starting loadout rules + objectives + enemy spawns. Normal raids and future weekly challenges use the same format.
- Terrain is a **height grid** (smooth hills, Unturned-style). Water is a flat see-through surface; you walk on the ground under it, but **water deeper than 1.2 m stops you like a wall** (MT-1, 2026-11-09, the user's call), for players and the AI, so a river is crossed only at its crossings. Shots and sight pass over it. **Water comes in rectangles you size in the editor, each at its own level** (2026-11-09, the user's call, over one level for the whole map), so a pond can sit above the creek below its weir and a tunnel stays dry. Nodes marked **landmark** are also drawn past the fog as a flat silhouette, so they show from anywhere on the map.
- On slopes, buildings sit on a concrete base that fills the gap down to the ground.
- **Stairs and ladders** work for players. AI uses stairs and slopes, but never ladders.
- **Key rooms:** locked doors that open with key items.
- **Named zones** (for example "Science lab" or "Gas station") are drawn in the editor and used for map labels and loot weighting.
- **Loot:** containers (backpacks, desks, cabinets…) and loose item spawn points each reference a loot table ("household", "academic", "medical", "military", …). Everything is rolled once when the raid starts.
  - **Loose spawns (session 11b):** a loose spot holds **one item** from its table (the table's item count is for containers), and has a **chance** of holding anything at all (1 if not given), so loose loot isn't in the same places every raid. The small house's table spot is 0.7.
  - **One of several spots (2026-10-30, for the mall's keys):** loose spots that share a `oneOf` name are a set, and only one of them, picked at random, is used each raid (it then rolls its chance as usual). For an item that must be somewhere but not always in the same place, like a key, without making every spot roll it.
  - **Loose loot setup (2026-11-10, the user's calls):** loose loot behaves differently from containers, so it's set up spot first. Loose loot can be anything, guns, armour and ammo included.
    - **Spots go on prefabs** where they can, so every copy brings its own (a `lootSpawn` with id `loose` on the furniture's top surface). Hand-placed spots are for one-offs (keys, a gun on a crate). The first ten: desk, executive desk, monitor desk, nightstand, coffee table, TV unit, kitchen run, shop counter, medical cart and gun wall rack, at chance 0.25–0.5. That's about 44 items on Northpoint and 9 on Silver Creek per raid.
    - **A spot rolls a mix of tables**, as a container does (`mix`, with an optional `empty` weight), then one item from the table it picks. Weights stay in tables, never in a list of items per spot, so changing a weight is one edit.
    - **N of a group:** placed prefabs given the same `lootGroup` (and hand-placed spots with that `oneOf`) are a group. The map's `lootGroups` says how many of it are used each raid (`pick`, 1 if not set, all if more than there are, 0 to switch a group off). A placed copy counts as one, and each spot on a used copy then rolls its chance. In the editor, select several placed prefabs and name their group. The tutorial switches off its furniture's spots with a group whose pick is 0, so only its scripted bandage lies loose.
  - **Tables are where you'd find a thing, not what it is (2026-11-10):** an item's category (Electronics) is for the UI and vendors. Military electronics go in `military-tech`, kept out of laptop bags and office computers, and military computers, the freight elevator and gun racks roll it. The user fills `military-tech`; for now it holds only the military binoculars.
- **Quest items** spawn only for a player who has that quest accepted (in co-op, only for the quest holder).
- **Extraction (decided with the user, 2026-10-26, session 11):** Tarkov-like. Stand inside an extraction point's radius for **7 seconds**, with a countdown on screen; stepping out resets it, being shot doesn't. Every extraction point was open in every raid until 2026-11-07; now an extraction zone is either **always open** or **rotating** (see Extraction points are zones). **When the timer runs out you're "missing in action"**, which counts as dying: you keep only your vault and your knife.
  - Session 11 details: the countdown names the point ("Extracting at Pond dock · 4.2"). Extraction points are drawn as **faint green columns** so you can find them. **They stay now that the map tab exists** (the user's call, 2026-10-27): extracts are easy to find on a small screen, and removing them later is one line (Tarkov shows nothing). The timer is in the top left and turns red under 2 minutes.
  - **Extraction points are zones (map format 4, the user's call, 2026-11-07):** not circles any more. In the map editor, draw a zone with the zones tool (click its corners), then tick **Extraction point** in its inspector: its name is the point's name, and it gets a **floor height** (filled in from the surface under it, looking down from the floor shown with the cut, else from the top, so a roof's helipad finds the roof; editable, with a button to measure it again). Players leave by standing **inside its outline, from 0.5 m under that floor up to the zone's height** (its "counts up to" field, 2 m if left blank; the user's call, same day: a zone round a shipping container can count its inside, round it and its roof, but not the building's floor above it), for the usual 7 s. **In the raid it's a short light-green border** round the outline on its floor, 25 cm tall whatever the height, with no fill; the map tab draws its outline only. The editor shows the zone's top too, its corners joined, so the height can be seen. Maps from before (format 3) turn each old circle into an octagon zone of the same size when loaded; the three maps were converted.
  - **Always open or rotating (the user's call, 2026-11-07, as Tarkov's random extracts):** an extraction zone is always open unless ticked **Open in only some raids (rotating)**. The map says how many of its rotating ones each raid opens ("rotating extractions open each raid", e.g. 3 of 5; blank opens them all, 0 none), and the pick is **each player's own** (see below). A closed one doesn't exist for you that raid: no green border, not on the map tab, and you can't leave by it. The editor's zones list tags them "rotating extraction".
  - **Extractions depend on where you spawn (the user's call, 2026-11-07):** each **player spawn** says which extractions a player starting there **can't use** (in the editor, select the spawn: a checkbox per extraction, all on by default). So a spawn in the warehouse loading yard can have the yard's car extract switched off, while spawns across the map keep it. **Each player's extractions are their own**: the always-open ones their spawn allows, plus the rotating ones it allows out of their own random pick: the map's "N per raid" picked from **all** the map's rotating extractions, drawn from the raid and the spawn, *then* the spawn's rule applied. So a rotating extract stays a chance (3 of 5 is 60%) for every spawn that allows it, however many others that spawn rules out; picking among only the allowed ones (the first version, the same day) made it certain for a spawn with N or fewer left, which the user ruled out. The cost: a spawn that rules some rotating ones out gets fewer in some raids; one with no always-open extraction that a raid could leave with none is warned about, so a co-op party, which starts together at one spawn, shares them, and in PvP someone who spawned elsewhere can have extracts you don't. The green borders, the map tab and leaving all go by your own. The editor lists any spawn left with no extraction it could use (map settings, under the zones), and a content test fails on one.
  - **Results screen:** extracted (green), killed in action (red, with what killed you) or missing in action (orange); time in raid and kills; what you brought out, or what you kept (vault and knife) and lost. It comes up 1.5 s after a death, so you see it happen. **After a death it lists what hit you** (the user's request, 2026-10-30): one line per enemy and weapon ("Yard boss shot you 4 times for 142 (chest, head) · armour took 30"), falls, and HP lost to bleeding, with the killing blow's source in red. The client keeps this from the sim's damage events (bleeding is the HP lost that no hit explains) and saves it with the raid for resuming. **Raid gain** (the user's request, 2026-11-03): every outcome lists the found-in-raid items you keep, vault included, at vendor prices (cash at full value), with the sum, which is 0 if none survived. Found in raid is a saved mark on each item (`Item.found`, the user's call, 2026-11-03, because quests need it from the stash): **found and not-found stacks never merge**, and a `handOver` objective with `foundInRaid: true` takes only found items (opt-in; existing quests don't use it). In a raid the mark is the other way round: `markBrought` flags what you take in, so anything unmarked came from the raid, however it reached you, and `settleFound` turns that back into `found` on the gear sent to the server. Something you brought stays not found through dropping it and picking it up. **What comes off a gun, attachments and rounds, is never found**: guns don't record their parts' marks, and otherwise any bought round could be made found by passing it through a found gun. A found gun's parts do count towards its gain. Since session 12b it also says whether the raid is saved; since session 13 its **Continue** button goes back to the main menu.
- **Game modes (2026-11-04, the user's request):** a switch at the top of the main menu, **Extraction | Zombies**, picks which mode's menu shows (since 2026-11-09 it's the lobby's mode button, top left, which opens a drawer of mode cards); the pick is remembered on the phone (and with no zombies modes in content the switch is hidden). Extraction's menu is the one above (party, maps, Start raid, stash, vendors, quests, friends, money). Zombies' menu is green rather than amber and has no money, stash, vendors or party: a map pick (with more than one mode in `content/zombies`), **Start game**, cards for the starting kit and points and the mystery box's guns, how it works, and the Admin button in dev builds.
- **Picking the map (2026-10-30, the user's call):** with more than one raid in `content/raids`, the main menu shows a button per map above Start raid; the pick is remembered on the phone. Since 2026-11-09 the maps are on the lobby's map screen, behind Deploy, with an overview of each (see The lobby).
- **Silver Creek, a planned starter map (2026-11-09, idea only, nothing built; "Mill Town" while planning, named by the user's pick of Claude's suggestion):** Northpoint is the hard map, so the next map is a beginner one, after the feel of Delta Force's Zero Dam (an original layout, not a copy). A port was considered first and kept for a later map: its open quays give long flat sightlines that new players can't survive against the AI, and it wants systems we don't have yet (a boat extract, water that slows you, paid extracts). Silver Creek is a sawmill company town in a valley (the mill is the Silver Creek Lumber Co.), a rectangle of about 320 × 150 m with a 20-minute raid, laid out like Tarkov's Customs: **players spawn at both ends and leave by the far end** (the user's call), using per-spawn extractions. Trees stay on the ridge, the south edge and a few small groups used as cover, because a forest hides landmarks and loses players (the user's point). The creek (a river in size) runs north to south through the middle with five crossings: log jam, log boom over the mill pond, the sawmill itself (the hot core), road bridge, weir ford. A mill race tunnel under the mill bypasses the core. There are three landmarks, drawn past the fog so you always know which end you face: fire lookout on the ridge (west; the steeple first planned was dropped, 2026-11-10: the user wants no churches in the game), teepee burner (centre), water tower (east). Perches are kept within about 60 m of AI positions or reached by stairs, because the AI sees only 70 m and can't climb ladders, so a distant perch would be a free sniper spot. The whiteboard sketch is at https://claude.ai/artifact/9v38f2y7U7tuf5cdKBxc84. **Agreed with the user the same day:** deep water (over about 1.2 m) blocks movement, so the creek is crossed only at the crossings. Easier because of **how many enemies there are and what they carry, not dumber AI** (no mercy still holds): fewer, poorer-geared scavs at the two ends, 2–4 PMCs (Northpoint 3–6), the strong ones around the mill. First in the map list with a "starter" tag; Northpoint stays open to everyone (no level lock). 3–5 starter quests on Silver Creek that teach the crossings, landmarks and tunnel.
