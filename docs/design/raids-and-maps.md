# Game design: Raids and maps

Part of the [design.md index](../design.md). Record decisions here, in the topic they belong to.

## Raids and maps
- Small maps, roughly 10–15 minutes to explore thoroughly.
- **Raid timer: 15 minutes by default**, set in the raid config. It's deliberately a little shorter than exploring the whole map, so you have to choose where to go and can't clear every corner. **Northpoint is 20 minutes** (the user's call, 2026-11-07): it's about 195 × 190 m but has 70-plus rooms worth entering, and a full sweep was estimated at 18–25 min before any fighting.
- **Player spawns:** the editor places several possible spawn points, and one is picked at random each raid. Co-op players spawn together at the same point. Enemy spawns near player spawns should be avoided (a minimum distance). **Built 2026-10-30 (the user's call, after "enemies see you from everywhere"):** an enemy spawn within 60 m of a player spawn, or in sight of one (within 70 m with a clear line between standing eyes), is a warning in the map editor's panel, and a content test keeps every map in `content/` free of them. Player spawns go on a map's edges, Tarkov-style; enemies hold the points of interest in the middle.
- **Spawn clearance per map (2026-11-07, the user's call):** a map's `enemyClearance` (meters, editable in the map settings) replaces the 60 m rule. **Northpoint uses 0**: 60 m left about 1% of its floor legal between nine edge spawns. Only the sight rule applies there (no enemy spawn in sight of a player spawn within 70 m). Other maps keep 60 m. The content test checks every map again (it was off since 2026-11-05). Checked in the sim: with all 32 possible enemies present at once, none noticed a player standing at any spawn in the first 20 s. The cost is that some enemies stand 20–30 m from a spawn behind walls, and a gunfight there can be heard straight away.
- **A raid is a data config:** map + playable area + starting loadout rules + objectives + enemy spawns. Normal raids and future weekly challenges use the same format.
- Terrain is a **height grid** (smooth hills, Unturned-style). Water is a flat see-through surface; you walk on the ground under it, but **water deeper than 1.2 m stops you like a wall** (MT-1, 2026-11-09, the user's call), for players and the AI, so a river is crossed only at its crossings. Shots and sight pass over it. **Water comes in rectangles you size in the editor, each at its own level** (2026-11-09, the user's call, over one level for the whole map), so a pond can sit above the creek below its weir and a tunnel stays dry. Nodes marked **landmark** are also drawn past the fog as a flat silhouette, so they show from anywhere on the map.
- On slopes, buildings sit on a concrete base that fills the gap down to the ground.
- **Stairs and ladders** work for players. AI uses stairs and slopes, but never ladders.
- **Key rooms:** locked doors that open with key items.
- **Named zones** (for example "Science lab" or "Gas station") are drawn in the editor and used for map labels; a zone with `extraction` is an extraction point. **They don't weight loot** (the user's call, 2026-11-11): every loot spot and container already names its own table or mix, and prefab loot groups handle "one of these", so a zone multiplier would only hide changes from the spot.
- **Loot:** containers (backpacks, desks, cabinets…) and loose item spawn points each reference a loot table ("household", "academic", "medical", "military", …). Everything is rolled once when the raid starts.
  - **Loose spawns (session 11b):** a loose spot holds **one item** from its table (the table's item count is for containers), and has a **chance** of holding anything at all (1 if not given), so loose loot isn't in the same places every raid. The small house's table spot is 0.7.
  - **One of several spots (2026-10-30, for the mall's keys):** loose spots that share a `oneOf` name are a set, and only one of them, picked at random, is used each raid (it then rolls its chance as usual). For an item that must be somewhere but not always in the same place, like a key, without making every spot roll it.
  - **Loose loot setup (2026-11-10, the user's calls):** loose loot behaves differently from containers, so it's set up spot first. Loose loot can be anything, guns, armor and ammo included.
    - **Spots go on prefabs** where they can, so every copy brings its own (a `lootSpawn` with id `loose` on the furniture's top surface). Hand-placed spots are for one-offs (keys, a gun on a crate). The first ten: desk, executive desk, monitor desk, nightstand, coffee table, TV unit, kitchen run, shop counter, medical cart and gun wall rack, at chance 0.25–0.5. That's about 44 items on Northpoint and 9 on Silver Creek per raid.
    - **A spot rolls a mix of tables**, as a container does (`mix`, with an optional `empty` weight), then one item from the table it picks. Weights stay in tables, never in a list of items per spot, so changing a weight is one edit.
    - **N of a group:** placed prefabs given the same `lootGroup` (and hand-placed spots with that `oneOf`) are a group. The map's `lootGroups` says how many of it are used each raid (`pick`, 1 if not set, all if more than there are, 0 to switch a group off). A placed copy counts as one, and each spot on a used copy then rolls its chance. In the editor, select several placed prefabs and name their group. The tutorial switches off its furniture's spots with a group whose pick is 0, so only its scripted bandage lies loose.
  - **Tables are where you'd find a thing, not what it is (2026-11-10):** an item's category (Electronics) is for the UI and vendors. Military electronics go in `military-tech`, kept out of laptop bags and office computers, and military computers, the freight elevator and gun racks roll it. The user fills `military-tech`; for now it holds only the military binoculars.
- **Quest items** spawn only for a player who has that quest accepted (in co-op, only for the quest holder).
- **Extraction (decided with the user, 2026-10-26, session 11):** Tarkov-like. Stand inside an extraction point's radius for **7 seconds**, with a countdown on screen; stepping out resets it, being shot doesn't. Every extraction point was open in every raid until 2026-11-07; now an extraction zone is either **always open** or **rotating** (see Extraction points are zones). **When the timer runs out you're "missing in action"**, which counts as dying: you keep only your vault and your knife.
  - Session 11 details: the countdown names the point ("Extracting at Pond dock · 4.2"). Extraction points are drawn as **faint green columns** so you can find them. **They stay now that the map tab exists** (the user's call, 2026-10-27): extracts are easy to find on a small screen, and removing them later is one line (Tarkov shows nothing). The timer is in the top left and turns red under 2 minutes.
  - **Extraction points are zones (map format 4, the user's call, 2026-11-07):** not circles any more. In the map editor, draw a zone with the zones tool (click its corners), then tick **Extraction point** in its inspector: its name is the point's name, and it gets a **floor height** (filled in from the surface under it, looking down from the floor shown with the cut, else from the top, so a roof's helipad finds the roof; editable, with a button to measure it again). Players leave by standing **inside its outline, from 0.5 m under that floor up to the zone's height** (its "counts up to" field, 2 m if left blank; the user's call, same day: a zone round a shipping container can count its inside, round it and its roof, but not the building's floor above it), for the usual 7 s. **In the raid it's a short light-green border** round the outline on its floor, 25 cm tall whatever the height, with no fill; the map tab draws its outline only. The editor shows the zone's top too, its corners joined, so the height can be seen. Maps from before (format 3) turn each old circle into an octagon zone of the same size when loaded; the three maps were converted.
  - **Always open or rotating (the user's call, 2026-11-07, as Tarkov's random extracts):** an extraction zone is always open unless ticked **Open in only some raids (rotating)**. The map says how many of its rotating ones each raid opens ("rotating extractions open each raid", e.g. 3 of 5; blank opens them all, 0 none), and the pick is **each player's own** (see below). A closed one doesn't exist for you that raid: no green border, not on the map tab, and you can't leave by it. The editor's zones list tags them "rotating extraction".
  - **Extractions depend on where you spawn (the user's call, 2026-11-07):** each **player spawn** says which extractions a player starting there **can't use** (in the editor, select the spawn: a checkbox per extraction, all on by default). So a spawn in the warehouse loading yard can have the yard's car extract switched off, while spawns across the map keep it. **Each player's extractions are their own**: the always-open ones their spawn allows, plus the rotating ones it allows out of their own random pick: the map's "N per raid" picked from **all** the map's rotating extractions, drawn from the raid and the spawn, *then* the spawn's rule applied. So a rotating extract stays a chance (3 of 5 is 60%) for every spawn that allows it, however many others that spawn rules out; picking among only the allowed ones (the first version, the same day) made it certain for a spawn with N or fewer left, which the user ruled out. The cost: a spawn that rules some rotating ones out gets fewer in some raids; one with no always-open extraction that a raid could leave with none is warned about, so a co-op party, which starts together at one spawn, shares them, and in PvP someone who spawned elsewhere can have extracts you don't. The green borders, the map tab and leaving all go by your own. The editor lists any spawn left with no extraction it could use (map settings, under the zones), and a content test fails on one.
  - **Results screen:** extracted (green), killed in action (red, with what killed you) or missing in action (orange); time in raid and kills; what you brought out, or what you kept (vault and knife) and lost. It comes up 1.5 s after a death, so you see it happen. **After a death it lists what hit you** (the user's request, 2026-10-30): one line per enemy and weapon ("Yard boss shot you 4 times for 142 (chest, head) · armor took 30"), falls, and HP lost to bleeding, with the killing blow's source in red. The client keeps this from the sim's damage events (bleeding is the HP lost that no hit explains) and saves it with the raid for resuming. **Raid gain** (the user's request, 2026-11-03): every outcome lists the found-in-raid items you keep, vault included, at vendor prices (cash at full value), with the sum, which is 0 if none survived. Found in raid is a saved mark on each item (`Item.found`, the user's call, 2026-11-03, because quests need it from the stash): **found and not-found stacks never merge**, and a `handOver` objective with `foundInRaid: true` takes only found items (opt-in; existing quests don't use it). In a raid the mark is the other way round: `markBrought` flags what you take in, so anything unmarked came from the raid, however it reached you, and `settleFound` turns that back into `found` on the gear sent to the server. **A found item taken back in is marked `refound`** (2026-11-11, the user's report: vault items from earlier raids showed as earned every raid): it comes out found again, but the raid gain (victory value, defeat match total) leaves it out, and `refound` and fresh stacks don't merge in the raid. `settleFound` clears it with `brought`. Something you brought stays not found through dropping it and picking it up. **What comes off a gun, attachments and rounds, is never found**: guns don't record their parts' marks, and otherwise any bought round could be made found by passing it through a found gun. A found gun's parts do count towards its gain. Since session 12b it also says whether the raid is saved; since session 13 its **Continue** button goes back to the main menu.
  - **Victory screen (the user's request, 2026-11-11):** extracting shows its own screen instead (MIA still uses the old screen): a thin top bar (Extracted, map, extraction point, duration); the **extracted value** (the raid gain above: found in raid, not what you brought) and **kills** in big numbers on the left, each with a button opening a list (the value by item, dearest first; who you killed with the raid clock, the latest at the top; names are the enemy types for now); finished quest objectives under them; **you on the main menu's plinth on the right** (`CharacterStage`, green ring). Money shows with `$`, as in the menu. The kill list (`killList`) is kept with the raid snapshot. Dev: `?victory` opens it with made-up results (`ui/results-preview.ts`; `npm run look -- --query victory`).
  - **Defeat screen (the user's request, 2026-11-11):** a death in a raid shows the victory screen's layout in red: the bar reads **Extraction failed** with the map, **Killed by** (the enemy type, or "Bled out" and the like) and duration; **Match total** (found-in-raid items you kept, so the vault's; never gear you brought) and **kills**, each with its list button beside the number. In place of you, the **last fight**: every hit between you and anyone, both ways, newest (the fatal one) at the top, hits more than 30 s apart starting a new fight (`lastCombat`). Taken rows are red with a body ghost (hit zones lit), damage and what the helmet or armor took on the left, and who and **From: Nm** on the right; the fatal row adds **With: gun | round**. Dealt rows are light gray and mirrored: who you hit and **Hits: Nm** on the left, a gray and white ghost on the right. Each logged hit keeps the distance and the shooter's gun and round from when it landed. The lost-gear list isn't shown. Dev: `?defeat` (`npm run look -- --query defeat`).
- **Game modes (2026-11-04, the user's request):** a switch at the top of the main menu, **Extraction | Zombies**, picks which mode's menu shows (since 2026-11-09 it's the lobby's mode button, top left, which opens a drawer of mode cards); the pick is remembered on the phone (and with no zombies modes in content the switch is hidden). Extraction's menu is the one above (party, maps, Start raid, stash, vendors, quests, friends, money). Zombies' menu is green rather than amber and has no money, stash, vendors or party: a map pick (with more than one mode in `content/zombies`), **Start game**, cards for the starting kit and points and the mystery box's guns, how it works, and the Admin button in dev builds. **Zombies was archived on 2026-11-13** ([zombies.md](zombies.md)): Extraction is the only mode; the mode button and drawer stay for a later mode.
- **Picking the map (2026-10-30, the user's call):** with more than one raid in `content/raids`, the main menu shows a button per map above Start raid; the pick is remembered on the phone. Since 2026-11-09 the maps are on the lobby's map screen, behind Deploy, with an overview of each (see The lobby).
- **Saving raids (session 12, decided with the user 2026-10-27):** the server owns the profile (money, stash, and the gear you'll take in, vault and knife included). **Starting a raid needs a connection**: the gear is then in the raid, and the profile can't be edited until the raid ends. **A raid that ends offline** is kept on the phone and sent before the next raid starts (session 12b; the results screen no longer shows whether it's saved, since 2026-11-09); the result replaces your gear with what you kept (everything after extracting; the knife and vault after dying or going missing). **A raid that never reports** (the phone lost, or one not saved on the phone, below) counts as missing in action the next time the game opens (since session 13, so the stash can be edited; before, when the next raid started): you keep the knife and the vault as they went in.
  - **Resuming (the user's call, 2026-10-27):** iOS may unload a backgrounded home-screen app (reading a long text, say). So the raid is saved on the phone each time the game goes to the background, and the next start says "Your raid is still on" and **resumes it from that moment**; the raid clock stops while you're away (solo raids pause whenever the app is hidden, or with the settings button). Swiping the app closed resumes too, so closing it is no longer a loss. Only the phone that played it can resume it; anywhere else it counts as missing, and the next start tells you it happened. A result that turns up after that (say from another phone) is refused. **With no connection, the game shows "No connection" with Try again** (the user's call, 2026-10-27), rather than an unsaved practice raid.
- **Loot tables (session 11):** each table has a range of how many items a container gets, and items picked by weight, with a count range for stacks (cash, rounds). A container's items depend only on its table, not on the container's size; what doesn't fit is left out. **Enemies carry loot too:** an enemy type lists tables with weights and how many items, and an enemy spawn's loot weights multiply those (the yard boss doubles military). The first tables are household, medical and military; the numbers are first guesses. **Mixes (2026-11-02, the user's call):** a container can draw on several tables by weight, plus a chance of being empty, either one table for the whole container (a house fridge is food 8 times in 10, parts 2 in 10) or a table per item (both in one fridge). Each kind of container (fridge, desk…) has a default mix, and any placed one can have its own.
  - **Weights follow rarity (the user's call, 2026-11-11):** common 4, uncommon 2, rare 1, epic 0.5, legendary 0.2, mythic 0.05, used in every generic table (cash counts as common). Guns, attachments and grenades are all common by rule, so their weights are set by hand (weapons, attachments, the molotov at 1). The hand-made map tables (kiln-stash, sawmill-tools, rail-cargo) keep their own weights. Big tables thin out each item: cash is about 5% of household rolls now.
  - **Mythics:** each one is in its theme's table at the mythic weight, so any matching container has a small chance at it. **Bosses, safes and locked rooms get a boost** from the shared `high-value` table (every legendary and mythic loot item, about 15% of its rolls mythic), mixed into their loot: Jug 0.4 (about a 25% chance he carries one), the office-safe kind 0.25 per item. Locked-room loose loot should use a mix with `high-value` too.
  - **Themed tables (2026-11-11):** `art` (paintings, sculptures, ornaments), `medical-loot` (equipment and lab finds, kept apart from `medical`, the meds that PMCs, scavs and Jug drop), `intel` (secret files; `documents` keeps the everyday papers found in desks and nightstands), `lab-tech` (reactors, nuclear fuel, the rocket thruster), `fridge-parts` (fridges roll food 0.66 / parts 0.34 per item). `industrial` holds every general Industrial item. **Where they roll (per item, by weight):** briefcases documents 0.75 / intel 0.25; filing cabinets and drawers documents 0.85 / intel 0.15; medical crates medical 0.75 / medical-loot 0.25; toolboxes and tool cabinets sawmill-tools 0.6 / industrial 0.4; scavs industrial 1 (with household 4, electronics 3). `art` and `lab-tech` aren't placed yet.

- **Switches (2026-11-05, the user's design):** anything solid in a map can be tagged as a switch, with rows that each open a door, at once or after a timer, with a noise heard all over the map for as long as you set, and another played at the door as it opens. It's used once a raid, by any player, and a door named by a switch stays locked until then. A switch can have a **use time**: the use button is held that long (2026-11-05), with a ring round the crosshair filling, and letting go, looking away or walking off starts it over. **Enemies never use one and don't react to its noises:** drawing them is left to balancing, by placing enemies. The tag and links go on the placed copy, not in the prefab, as they name doors in that one map.
  - **Freight elevator:** the first use. One trip down, 45 s: the klaxon sounds across the map, the lamp over the gate flashes yellow and the cab comes down in sight. Then a buzzer, the shutter rolls up and the lamp goes solid green (dull red before any of it). It's a one-way trip nobody can ride, so the real cab and the loot sit behind the shutter from the start, and the cab you watch is drawn only. A cab that's raised or on its way down can't be hit or stood on.

- **Map select is a region map (2026-11-11, the user's call, after Delta Force's raid select; our own art):** the deploy screen's map list and overview give way to one made-up valley ("Ashford Valley", Claude's name) drawn in faded dark colors, with a marker per raid at its `region` spot: Silver Creek, the creek village south-west, and Northpoint, the town on the highway north-east. Tap a marker to pick it. A **Layout** button shows the picked map from above (its extracts) as before. **Northpoint is locked until PMC level 2** for now (`minLevel`): its marker is dimmed and dashed, with a lock and "Level 2" over it, and can't be picked. A party raid needs every member at its level (the user's call, same day): the leader's Start is off and the status line names who's short ("Northpoint: bob needs level 2"), and the server refuses the start the same way. A solo start is checked only in the menu (PvE, no anti-cheat).
