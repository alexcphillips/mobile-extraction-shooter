# Game design: Quests and vendors

Part of the [design.md index](../design.md). Record decisions here, in the topic they belong to.

## Quests and vendors
- **Quest types:**
  - **Investigate:** a marker visible through walls, with a live distance readout. Go to it and tap "Investigate".
    - Session 14b: a yellow diamond with the distance in meters, for each of your active quests' markers on the map not yet investigated; one off screen sits at the screen's edge in its direction. **Removed 2026-11-09 (the user's call): the diamonds are on the map tab only now**, not over the world. **Removed from the map tab too, 2026-11-11 (the user's call, after Tarkov): no quest spot is shown anywhere.** The objective's text names the place, the map tab's labels say where that is, and the investigate prompt says you've found it, so quests teach the map. Every investigate spot must be somewhere the text can name and a player can recognize. Within **2.5 m** the use button says **investigate** (beating a container or body, but not an item or door under the crosshair) and the objective's text shows under the crosshair; a tap ticks it ("Investigated · …") and the marker goes.
  - **Extract with an item:** a quest item that spawns only while the quest is accepted.
    - Session 14b (decided with the user 2026-10-27): the objective lists **its own spots**, quest markers on its map, and the item lies on the ground at **one picked at random each raid**. Spots are **not shown** on screen: the objective's text hints where ("by the car, or in the locked shed"), so finding it is the job. It counts if you **extract with it, or keep it in your vault** after dying (as in Tarkov). **The server takes every quest item** out of the gear you keep when the raid ends, wanted or not, so quest items never reach the stash. Only one quest may want a given quest item.
  - **In the raid** (14b): the raid screen's **Quests** tab lists your active quests with each objective's standing this raid ("Not investigated yet" (was "Marked on your map" until 2026-11-11), "Investigated", "Not found yet", "Got it: now get out", "Not on this map", hand-overs "from your stash"). The **results screen** lists the objectives the raid finished, and leaves quest items out of what you brought out.
  - **Hand over** (session 14): a normal item is handed over **from the stash** between raids, not by extracting with it (the user's call, 2026-10-27), so a gold watch already in the stash counts.
  - More types can be added as data.
- **Quests between raids (session 14, decided with the user 2026-10-27):**
  - Each vendor gives a **chain** of quests: a quest lists the ones that must be done first, and opens when they are. A quest with nothing before it is open from the start.
  - **Quests live inside the vendors (2026-11-10, the user's call: Tarkov's unified trader screen).** There's no Quests page or menu button: each vendor's page has a **Quests** tab beside its **Shop**. Before that, the **Quests** page (from the menu) had a tab per vendor with quests, as cards: what needs handing in first, then active, new and locked ones (dimmed, with what they come after); done ones in one line at the bottom. A vendor's tab shows a count of quests ready to hand in; the menu's button says how many are ready, active and new.
  - **Accept** takes one tap. **Hand over** takes as many of the item as the objective still needs from the stash pages and the bags in them, never from what you wear or carry, and only plain items (a bag with nothing in it, a gun with nothing fitted); a second tap confirms, since they're gone. Handing over can be done a bit at a time.
  - Once every objective is done, **Hand in** gives the rewards: money, and items into the stash (the page last shown first). With no room for them nothing happens and it says so, so a reward is never lost.
  - **Investigating counts even if you die** later in that raid (the user's call, 2026-10-27); a quest item you carried is lost like anything else. Both happen in raids (session 14b).
- **Vendors** buy and sell, always stock cheap basic gear, give quests, and sell armor repair kits.
- **Economy (session 13b, decided with the user 2026-10-27):**
  - **Every item has a value** (in its item file). Vendors sell new items for their value and **buy for 50% of it** (`VENDOR.sellShare`), scaled by condition (armor's durability, a med's uses or HP left, a repair kit's points, a key's uses); a gun sells with its attachments and loaded rounds. **A bought gun comes empty** (the user's call, 2026-11-01), and so does one given as a quest reward: its own magazine but no rounds and no attachments (`newStashItem`), so rounds are always bought separately, and buying a gun to sell it back can't pay out for rounds it never cost. So selling always loses money and nothing can be bought and sold back for a profit. Items worth 0 (none yet) can't be sold, nor a rig or backpack with anything in it, nor the knife.
  - **Unlimited stock**: money is the only limit. Each vendor is a content file listing what it sells (rounds come 30 at a time). Two to start: **Kovacs** (pistol, AKM, 9 mm and 7.62×39 rounds of levels 1–3, the 24-round pistol magazine, red dot, muzzle brake, helmets and vests of levels 1–3, chest rig, small backpack) and **Doc** (bandage, splint, medkit, surgery kit, armor repair kit). The plate carrier, scope, suppressor, large backpack and 45-round magazine are only found in raids. *(The vendors themselves were replaced on 2026-11-06: see Vendor levels below.)*
  - **Cash is money**: whatever cash a raid brings out (everything after extracting, the vault's after dying or going missing) goes into your balance **at its full value** when the server takes the result; the results screen says how much. It can't be taken back out as cash. Cash already in a stash from before can be sold at full value.
  - **Where:** the **Vendors** page (from the menu, or the stash's top bar) shows each vendor's offers as rows with their price; a tap, then a second tap ("Buy for 12,000?"), buys into the stash, the page shown first. **Selling is done in the stash**: select any item of yours (gear, carried, vault or stash) and press **Sell · price**, then confirm. Every vendor pays the same, so a sell screen per vendor would only add steps. **Several at once** (the user's request, same day): **Select to sell** (with nothing selected; the details card's **Sell several** was removed at the user's request, 2026-11-11) makes taps pick and unpick items, outlined green, with the count and total in the bar; **Sell N** and a second tap sells them all in one request, or none if one can't go (a backpack can go with everything in it picked too). Drags do nothing meanwhile; **Cancel** leaves. A slower second tap on the selected item now lets go of it. The stash's top bar shows your money.
  - **Sell everything (the user's request, 2026-10-31; removed at the user's request, 2026-11-09):** a stash top-bar button that sold everything sellable (gear and stash, never the knife) in two taps. It's gone; selling is item by item or with **Select to sell**.
  - **First-guess values** (cash is 1): AKM 24,000, pistol 6,000; 7.62×39 rounds 50 each, 9 mm 20 (other levels: see Damage → Ammo and armor levels); plate carrier 30,000, steel helmet 12,000; 4× scope 18,000, suppressor 14,000, red dot 5,000, muzzle brake 4,000, 45-round mag 6,000, 24-round mag 2,500; medkit 4,000, surgery kit 9,000, splint 600, bandage 400; large backpack 20,000, chest rig 6,000, small backpack 5,000; gold watch 15,000, shed key 8,000; armor repair kit 12,000. The starting kit is worth about 84,000 (94,000 before its armor went to level 3; the stash page prices were set against 94,000 and weren't changed).
- **Vendor levels and the five vendors (the user's call, 2026-11-06, after Tarkov's traders):**
  - **Levels I to IV** per vendor, per account. Everyone starts at I. Reaching II, III and IV each needs **reputation** with that vendor and **money spent** with it (both, and the levels before). Reputation comes from that vendor's **quests** (a quest's `rewards.rep`); spending counts every purchase from it. There was **no player level** at first (the user chose rep + money over adding XP); the **PMC level** came on 2026-11-11 to gate quests (below), and vendor levels still go by rep + money only. Standing is in the profile (`vendors`, format 8) and only the server changes it: a stash edit that changes it is refused.
  - **Each offer is sold from a level** (`level` in the vendor file). Offers above your level show **locked** (🔒 and the level) so you can see what's coming. Stock stays **unlimited**, and selling stays **one price for every vendor** from the stash (the user kept both).
  - **The vendors** (first guesses by rarity and protection level; mythic level-6 gear stays loot-only): **Kovacs** (the Quartermaster until 2026-11-10, renamed at the user's request; id `guns-ammo-vendor` since 2026-11-11 (was `quartermaster`; renamed so ids say what each sells, profile format 15 moves saved rep and running decryptions); basic guns, every caliber's rounds by level, grenades, C4 at IV); **Parker** (the Gunsmith until 2026-11-10, renamed at the user's request, id `attachments-vendor` since 2026-11-11 (was `gunsmith`); optics, muzzles, magazines, and the better guns: rare at II, M416/MP7/G3 at III, epic at IV); **Harlan** (the Armorer until 2026-11-10, renamed at the user's request, id still `armorer`; helmets, vests, rigs, packs and repair kits, level 1–2 armor at I–II, 3 at III, 4 at IV); **Doc** (the Medic until 2026-11-10, renamed at the user's request, id still `medic`; bandages and splints, medkits at II, surgery kits at III); **Fence** (loot, keys and rare finds: phones and laptops at I up to the AWM and level-5 armor and rounds at IV), **×1.6 prices** and a **rotating stock**: 8 of its offers at a time, a new pick every 3 hours, the same for everyone. Levels need rep 0.2 / 0.4 / 0.6 and 50k / 250k / 750k spent (Doc 20k / 80k / 200k, the Fence 100k / 400k / 1.2M). Every vendor has a two-quest chain giving 0.2 then 0.4 rep, so IV is reachable once both are done; more quests should come with more rep.
  - **The vendors page (reworked 2026-11-10 as Tarkov's unified trader screen, the user's call; works on PC and a phone held sideways):** it opens on a **hub** of tall trader cards in each vendor's color (a hexagon emblem with its initial, a level stamp, a bar to the next level, its blurb on a PC, and pills for quests **ready** (green) and **new** (red)); a phone fits them in one row that scrolls if there are more. A tap opens that vendor's page: **down the side** its emblem, name, level, blurb (PC only), the next level's rep and spending bars, markup and restock (PC only), big **Shop** and **Quests** tabs (Quests with the same pills), and the other vendors' emblems at the bottom (with a dot for ready or new quests) to switch without going back. A vendor opens on Quests when one is ready to hand in, else on Shop. Escape or **‹ Vendors** goes back to the hub. Before that rework: the vendors down the side (initial in their color, name, level), the one picked with its blurb, markup and restock countdown, and **bars for the next level's rep and spending**. Then a **search**, the **category chips** (as the stash's), a **sort** (level, price up or down, name) and **Hide locked**, over the offers as **cards** (picture, name, count, price in red if you can't afford it, level). A tap opens the item's details with **how many to buy** (−, +, Max, up to 50) and **Buy**, which asks for a second tap with the total. The quests page lists a quest's rep reward.
- **PMC level (2026-11-11, the user's call, after Tarkov's; reverses "no player level" above, for gating quests):** one XP total per account (profile `xp`), levels 1 to 50, level n at 500 × n × (n − 1) XP in all (1,000 for 2, 45,000 for 10). Numbers are Claude's first guesses, approved by the user.
  - **Quests** are the fastest way up: `rewards.xp` (first pass 1,000–3,000 on the five quests). A quest can need a level (`minLevel`): **below it, it's hidden** everywhere (the user's call; unlike locked vendor offers, which show a 🔒). A quest once taken stays shown.
  - **Raids:** each kill is worth its enemy type's `xp` (scav 40, PMC 100, boss 400, the foreman's guards 150; a type without one goes by its faction), **+50% for a headshot kill** (the killing hit to the head), and **10 per container you search first**. **Getting out multiplies it by 1.3**; dying or going missing keeps the base.
  - **Run-through:** getting out in under 4 minutes with under 150 base XP (Tarkov's 7 min / 200 XP scaled down for 10–15 minute raids) **halves the XP, with no extract bonus, and nothing new comes out found in raid** (the user's call to strip FIR too); found items you took back in stay found.
  - No XP in the tutorial or zombies. In co-op each player gets their own kills and searches.
  - **Shown:** the level badge with a bar to the next level in the lobby's top bar; the results screen has a PMC XP stat with a sheet of the lines, the multiplier and the level after (a level-up says so).
- **Silver Creek's starter quests (MT-10, 2026-11-10, Claude's first pass):** a Kovacs chain of four that walks a new player through the starter map, each worth 0.05 rep (Kovacs had one quest worth 0.4, so couldn't reach the 0.6 for level IV; the chain makes up the 0.2). Investigating counts even if you die, so a beginner keeps progress.
  1. **Get your bearings:** investigate the three landmarks (the lookout's landing, the burner's ash door, the pump house under the water tower). 10,000 and two bandages.
  2. **Ways across:** the crossings that aren't the mill or the road bridge (the log jam, the boom man's shack, the weir ford). 15,000 and a field first aid kit.
  3. **Under the mill:** extract with the **headgate logbook** (`race-logbook`, a new quest item), lying at one of three spots along the race tunnel. 20,000 and the **kiln key**, which teaches keys.
  4. **Kiln four:** unlock the fourth kiln with that key and investigate inside; the room's loot is the real reward. 25,000 and a medium backpack.
- **Armor repair kits (session 13b):** select a piece of armor and press **Repair**: the first kit with points left mends it **back to its max durability**, spending that many points; a spent kit is gone. **Repairs wear the max down, as in Delta Force (the user's call, 2026-11-06):** mending x of the durability it's missing takes x × the armor's **repair loss** off its max, and adds the rest, x × (1 − loss), which is what the kit spends. So a vest at 10/65 with a 12% loss comes back to 58.4/58.4 (6.6 of the 55 missing is lost for good), and a kit too small to finish wears the max by the share it mends. Each armor has its own `repairLoss` (Delta Force's "durability cost", measured there at about 6–18%): first guesses go by level, 16% at level 1 down to 8% at level 6, and 10% when an item file leaves it out. Nothing raises the max back up; the server refuses a stash edit that does. Tiles and details show durability out of the current max, to a tenth; details also show the max out of the original and the loss. Different repair kits (for instance ones that wear less) come later. **Between raids it's at once**, with a kit anywhere in your gear or stash. **In a raid it takes the kit's use time (8 s)**, with a kit you carry (pockets, rig or backpack, not the vault, as for meds), and works like a med: walking is slowed, and firing, reloading, sprinting or climbing stops it (nothing is spent); the heal button shows its progress and cancels it. **Dragging a kit onto armor** does the same as Repair with that kit (the armor lights green if it can). **Any armor you can reach can be mended (the user's call, 2026-11-06)**, not only what you wear: armor in your bags, in the stash (on a page or in a stored bag), and in a raid a body's (worn or in its bags), a crate's or on the ground. The kit must still be one you carry in a raid. Walking out of reach before a timed repair ends mends nothing and spends nothing. **While a med or repair is in use, a pill in the raid screen's top bar** shows its name, the seconds left and a bar filling up; **tapping it stops it** (the user's request, same day). A selected med you carry also has **Use** in the inventory bar (or "Nothing to treat"), the same as picking it from the heal button. A kit is priced so that **repairing never makes money**: a point costs more than it adds to the armor's sell price (a content test checks every kit against every armor).

## Quest objectives overhaul (the user's brief, 2026-11-13; settle in QO-1 before building)
The roadmap's **Quest objectives overhaul** section has the sessions. This is the brief; QO-1 turns it into decisions and rewrites this part.

- **Any combination.** A quest is ordered **steps**, and each step has one or more objectives, so "fetch the logbook, then leave by the backpack extract" is two steps. Every objective kind below can take any of the **conditions**, all of which must hold (AND only; no OR or NOT in v1).
- **Objective kinds:**
  1. **Eliminate:** a target (scav, PMC, boss, or one enemy type), a count, and conditions: map or zone (mapwide, or a named zone such as the mill on Silver Creek), weapon (one gun, or a class), attachments fitted (the P226 with a given suppressor), worn gear (a given vest, or no armor), range (under 25 m, over 60 m), hit zone of the killing hit (legs), and the player's own status (a broken limb).
  2. **Hand over:** **found in raid** items (FIR), **any-source** items, or **money**. FIR rules: dying strips FIR from everything gained that raid, the vault included; an item brought in already FIR keeps it after a death (the existing `refound` mark).
  3. **Quest items:** spawn only while the quest is active, only for its holder, on their map (the logbook in the tunnels under the mill). Not regular loot: can't be sold, dropped, given to others or vaulted. They go into a **quest inventory** that you can't move items out of. You use one up by extracting with it (documents for a vendor) or by placing it at a spot (an explosive you plant).
  4. **Discover:** step into a room, building or hidden trigger volume to tick "located", optionally **and survive the raid** it happened in.
  5. **Place / mark / interact:** a vendor-given marker, tracker or camera sits in the quest inventory, is **never lost on death**, and is placed at spots (a choke point, a boss room, a vehicle, a supply crate). **Timed interactions** (within x minutes of the raid's start): plant a jammer, pull data from a terminal, cut the power, calibrate an antenna, each one hold-to-use with its own drawn screen. Only power will change the world, later; for now it changes nothing. **Stashing:** bring regular items in and leave them at a spot (they're gone).
  6. **Weapon build:** a gun meeting stat thresholds (ergonomics, stability, weight, capacity with a loaded magazine, range) and/or carrying named attachments (the "thermal scope 9000"). The gun's details panel shows these stats.
  7. **Extract:** by a given exit (a paid car extract, a no-backpack extract), or with at least x worth of FIR items from one raid. Raid-wide modifiers such as "without killing any scavs" can sit on any objective.
  8. **Progression:** reach a vendor level (Kovacs wants you at Parker III). Quests can give rep to one vendor and take it from another.
  9. **Search a place:** enter a loot area (through the District freight elevator's alarmed door) or open a vault's UI after breaching or drilling it (the breach mechanic is undecided).
- **No refetching across maps (the user's rule):** in a chain across maps (explosives from map A, planted on map B), getting the item out of map A is a **checkpoint**. Dying on map B never sends you back to map A. Tarkov makes you refetch, and players hate it; it causes burnout.
