# Game design: Inventory and UI

Part of the [design.md index](../design.md). Record decisions here, in the topic they belong to.

## Inventory and UI
- **A grid inventory with item sizes** (Tarkov-style), designed for touch:
  - Auto-placement puts an item in the first free space. Items can be rotated.
  - **Double-tap** moves an item between your inventory and the open container (or stash), fitting it automatically.
  - **Touch: scroll, or hold to lift (UX-1, 2026-11-08):** a finger on an item that moves within **200 ms** scrolls the panel instead; one held still for 200 ms **lifts** the item (it pops up under the finger, with a shadow) and then drags it. A mouse still drags as soon as it moves. 200 ms is under the double-tap's 350 ms, so both taps of a double-tap stay taps. A lifted item let go without moving is a tap (its details open), not a drop on itself. A finger moving sideways before the lift lets go too (nothing scrolls sideways). Android's long-press menu is blocked while a finger holds an item.
- Backpacks and rigs have their own grids. **No containers inside containers.**
- **Backpack sizes (the user's call, 2026-11-07):** small holds a **3×2** grid (one pocket, 2×2 outside, common, 5,000), medium **3×4** (one pocket, 3×3 outside, uncommon, 10,000; armorer level 2, military loot, PMC kit), large unchanged at 5×6 (4×4 outside). The small one was 4×4 in three pockets; saved bags are reshaped on load and what no longer fits goes to the stash.
- **Opening a stored rig or backpack (the user's call, 2026-11-01):** a single tap on a rig or backpack lying in the open container or stash page opens a window with what's in it; drag items in and out of it, double-tap one to take it. **Windows (2026-11-11, the user's call):** each bag opens in a **floating window over the whole inventory** (stash, filter buttons, gear), **up to 5 at once** (a sixth closes the oldest), the newest on top. The first opens at the top of the screen left of center, each further one 16 px right and down from the last (back to the start if it would leave the screen). A window is **dragged by its title bar** (kept on screen) and a touch brings it to the front. The item details card stays above them. Windows close when the open container or stash page changes. **While one is open, a double-tap of yours goes into the top one with room** rather than the container or stash page, except another rig or backpack, which can't go in it and still goes to the container. The window has no Details button (removed 2026-11-11; right-click on a PC still opens them at once); a tap on the bag again, or ✕, closes it. A backpack keeps what's in it in the stash, and so does a rig. Only one level: containers still don't nest. Inventory refs name such a grid with `inside` (the bag's index and id in the grid it lies in).
- **Gear slots (session 9, decided with the user 2026-10-26):** two primary guns (**not pistols**, the user's change 2026-10-30; it was any gun), a sidearm (pistols only), the knife, helmet, body armor, a rig and a backpack. What's in your hands is one of the two primaries, the sidearm or the knife.
- **Switching (session 10b, chosen by the user 2026-10-26): four slot buttons**, one per hand slot, where the quick slots were: tap one to take it out (keys 1–4 on desktop). Empty slots are dimmed and the one in hand is outlined. **Drawing takes the item's draw time** (rifle 0.8 s, pistol 0.4 s, knife 0.3 s), with the gun coming up from below; until it's drawn you can't fire, swing, aim or reload. Switching stops a reload and a med being used.
- **Pockets** (decided with the user): **five 1×1 slots** everyone always has (the user's call, 2026-11-08, UX-3; a 4×1 grid until then), lost on death like the rest. Nothing bigger than 1×1 goes in: a molotov, a medkit, a surgery kit or a repair kit has to go in a rig to be in reach of the quick actions. Built as a 5×1 grid of five 1×1 sections (`POCKET_SECTIONS`), so it works as a rig's pouches do and is drawn a gap apart; old 4×1 pockets are split on load (`settlePockets`, as `settleVault`), and what's bigger than 1×1 goes to the stash. A player with no rig or backpack can still carry a few rounds and a bandage, and enemies' spare rounds go there, so their bodies have something to loot.
- **Not doing (the user's calls, 2026-11-08, from the mobile UI notes):** valuables do **not** go to the vault by themselves (the vault stays a choice made under pressure); **no item or ammo cases**, since category pages replace them (hangar.md → Stash) and containers still don't nest; **no extra vaults from quests**, since the secure locker stays the one way to a bigger vault.
- **The vault is part of the in-raid inventory**, kept on death. Players only. Its size comes from the Secure locker (H4, hangar.md): 2 wide × 1 tall unbuilt, up to 3×3.
- **Searching the stash (the user's request, 2026-11-05):** a search box at the top of the stash column, staying in view while it scrolls (the top bar has no room on a phone). Matching items stay lit and everything else dims: **nothing moves or hides**, so a page keeps its layout and dimmed items can still be dragged and dropped on. It looks at **every page**: each page's tab shows how many it has, a tab with none fades, and the box shows the total. Every word typed must be in an item's name or the name of what's fitted to it or stored in it, so "akm red" finds an AKM with a red dot and a backpack lights up for what's in it; an `x` matches the `×` in names like 7.62×39. A stack is one item. Your own gear and grids aren't searched. Esc clears the search, then leaves the stash as usual. Client only (`ui/stash-search.ts`); nothing is saved.
- **Unloading a gun (the user's request, 2026-10-30):** select any gun you can reach (yours, a body's, one in a container or in the stash) and press **Unload**: its rounds go into what you carry, pockets first; with no room they're dropped (in the stash, put in the stash). It's instant, like any inventory move, and stops a reload. The server's "no items from nothing" check between raids counts a gun's loaded rounds as its ammo, so unloading makes nothing.
- **Loading a gun by dragging rounds onto it (the user's request, 2026-10-30):** drop a stack of rounds the gun fires on any gun you can reach (a grid, a gear slot, a body, a crate; outlined green when it can). More of the round in it tops it up from the stack; another round swaps, and the rounds that were in it go into what you carry (dropped with no room; in the stash, put in the stash). What doesn't fit stays in the stack. **Onto the gun in your hands in a raid it starts a normal reload with those rounds** (the user's call), so it takes the reload time and can't skip a reload mid-fight; that needs the stack carried (pockets, rig or backpack: by hand the backpack is in reach, though R doesn't reach it), the gun drawn and not already reloading. Onto any other gun, and between raids even the one in hand, it's instant. Not "packing rounds into magazines" (still out): it's one drop, with no per-round time.
- **Ammo is loose rounds in stacks** in your grids (60 per cell for the current rounds). Reloading (R) takes the gun's rounds from **pockets, then rig, not the backpack** (as in Tarkov, the user's call, 2026-11-01; until then the backpack too), **never the vault**, so the vault stays a place to keep things. Rounds in the backpack are loaded by dragging them onto the gun. The HUD's round count is what R can reach. One rule for every quick action (R, the heal button, the grenade button and G): `QUICK_GRIDS` in `sim/gear.ts`. Rounds that no longer fit when a bigger magazine comes off go back into what you carry, and **are dropped on the ground if there's no room** (session 11b; until then such a change was refused).
- **Splitting stacks (the user's request, 2026-11-01):** a stack of more than one in any grid (yours, the stash, a body's, a crate's) has a **Split** button in its details: pick how many with **¼, ½ or ¾** or a slider (1 to one fewer than the stack), then **Split here** makes a new stack of that many in the first free spot of the same grid (grayed out as "No room here" when the grid is full), or the move button (**Move n**, **Take n**, **Put in n**) sends just that many where a double-tap would, so rounds come out of a full vault into what you carry. Chosen as a button in the details, not a modifier on drag as on a PC, since a phone has no modifier keys; drag the new stack anywhere afterwards. Sim actions: `split`, and `quick` with a `count`; between raids a partial double-tap into the stash goes on to the next page with what's still to go.
- Session 9 details: auto-placement goes row by row from the top left, trying the item unrotated everywhere before rotated. A stack first tops up stacks of the same item, then takes new cells a full stack at a time. Dropping a stack on a stack of the same item merges as much as fits. **No nesting** means a rig or backpack can't go in pockets, the vault, or another rig or backpack, even empty; only the stash and loot containers (desks, crates, a body's gear) take them. A gun's size doesn't change with attachments.
- **The raid keeps running while the in-raid screen is open** (decided with the user, 2026-10-26, session 10): you stand still and can be shot, as in Tarkov, and co-op works the same way. The red hurt flash and the health bar show over it.
- **Looting (session 10):** the use button turns into **loot** when a container or dead body is within 2 m and you're looking at it, and opens it beside your inventory. **A body has to be looked at (the user's call, 2026-11-01, after looting one by accident instead of closing a door):** the crosshair must meet the floor within 1.2 m of its feet (which way it fell isn't known, so that covers most of it lying any way), not just be in front of you; one right underfoot no longer counts unless you look down at it. **Containers are their geometry (the user's call, same day):** in the editor, a box, cylinder, group or placed prefab can be made a loot container (`loot: { container, lootTable }` on the node, ticked "loot container" in the inspector). It's looted by **aiming at what collides in it**, the first solid under the crosshair as for doors (ahead of a door's doorway, container markers and bodies), and **reached from anywhere within 2 m of its footprint** (the bounds of its solids, in its yaw), so a long counter can be looted from either end. It needs something that collides, and containers don't nest. Its id is the node's path id (a group's is "#" and its index, as it has no id). It's an optional field, so maps stay format 3. **One piece of furniture can hold several containers** (the user's call, 2026-11-03, for the filing cabinet): each drawer is its own group with `loot`, so you search the drawer you aim at; such kinds get a smaller grid (a filing drawer is 3×2) so a cabinet doesn't hold double. **Searched looks (built 2026-11-03):** when anyone searches a container with a searched look, its look changes for everyone in the raid, such as a drawer left pulled out. **Searched means the search action completed, whether or not anything is taken** (the user's call). There's no timed search yet, so that's when the container screen opens. **It lasts the rest of the raid; nothing resets it.** **Each container swaps by itself and they add up:** search the bottom drawer and it opens, then the top and both are open, so all combinations work without extra prefabs and a cabinet has no "which drawer is open" value. One drawer's looks replace only that drawer's group. The look is drawn only: collision and aiming stay the closed container's. No decoration-only copies of containers with a different look (the user's call): they'd pass for another kind of container; the cabinet's looks are `filing-cabinet-top-drawer-open` and `filing-cabinet-bottom-drawer-open`, one open drawer each in the cabinet's own frame, never placed on a map. **The old lootContainer marker still works** as a point: the crosshair must pass within 0.6 m of half a meter above it. The existing maps keep their markers, since they're to be replaced by hand-made ones (the user's call). A container marker or body, whichever the crosshair is nearest, beats looking through an open doorway. **Not through walls (2026-10-30):** a container, body or item on the ground counts only with a clear line from your eye to it (to half a meter above a container's or body's feet), so one in the next room within 2 m doesn't. A **bag button** opens just your inventory. **Double-tap** sends your item to what's open, or theirs to you; with nothing open, it equips an item from your grids into an empty gear slot, or stows a gear item into what you carry. Taking a gun fills an empty gun slot first (a pistol only the sidearm), then goes into your grids. **Anything taken by double-tap goes into your vault last** (the user's call, 2026-10-30): pockets, rig and backpack first, then the vault, so loot still comes along when you're full. Dragging can still put anything in the vault. **Dropping on a gear slot swaps** with what's there, if that can go where the dragged item came from. **Dropping on items in a grid swaps too** (the user's call, 2026-10-30): everything under the dropped item goes where it came from, keeping its place relative to the drop, so a 1×2 medkit dropped on two 1×1s puts them in the two cells it left. One that doesn't fit there is turned there, or else put anywhere inside the cells the dropped item left, either way round (so an upright medkit turns flat into the place a flat 2×1 left), biggest first. If any still doesn't fit, the drop is refused (red). Turning an item in place (R) never pushes others aside. A stack dropped on the same item still tops it up instead. Between grids too (pockets ↔ backpack, yours ↔ a body's). **While dragging, a second finger turns the item** (R on desktop).
- **The in-raid screen has tabs:**
  - **Inventory:** includes looting containers and bodies, plus the attachment manager.
  - **Map:** a simple SVG generated from map data, showing building outlines, named zones and **your own position**.
    - **Floors, labels and quest spots (2026-11-09, the user's call, after Northpoint's map tab showed only its roof on a huge field):** a map can list its **floors** (a name and the height of each, lowest first, in the editor's map settings; Northpoint: Ground at 0 m, Upper at 6 m). The map tab then shows **one floor at a time**, cut 1.5 m over it as the editor's cutaway is, with a button per floor (top floor first; yours marked •), opening on the floor you're on. What tops out well under the floor shown (an atrium's ground floor) is dimmed. A map without floors is drawn from above as before. The view is **framed on what's built** and the markers, 8 m round, not the whole terrain. **Map labels** are a marker (`mapLabel`: text, and size *area* or *place*), placed and dragged in the map editor like any marker and drawn there as flat text on the floor; on the map tab each shows on the floor its height is on, areas bigger, and a name that would run into a bigger one is left out until zoomed in. Named zones still label the map too. **Extractions** are filled light green with a way-out sign, and **your quest spots** not yet investigated are yellow diamonds; one on another floor is dimmed and marked ↑ or ↓. Chosen over an editor for drawn map images (Claude's suggestion): an image goes stale with every map edit, and the map data already has everything.
    - Session 11b: looking straight down with −z up, everything that stands is drawn as its footprint in its own color, highest on top (so buildings show as roofs, trees as dots), with water, named zones (dashed, named), extraction points (green circles, named) and you as a yellow arrow. **"Near me"** zooms to 60 m around you and "Whole map" back; labels stay the same size on screen, and a zone's name that would run into another's is left out until you zoom in. There's no pinch zoom (the game blocks pinching).
  - **Quests.**
- **Swapping bags (the user's call, 2026-11-06):** dropping an **empty** rig or backpack on the slot of one with things in it swaps the bags *and* moves the contents into the new one, each item at the same spot if it's free there, the rest auto-placed. Any rig (armored or not) or backpack. **What doesn't fit (2026-11-11, the user's call)** is dropped at your feet in a raid, or put on the stash pages between raids (no room there: it stays in the old bag, as before 2026-11-11). The old bag leaves empty, so it can swap into a backpack's grid too (a plain rig dragged from your backpack onto a full armored rig). An empty bag lying in the bag it would replace can't be dropped on its slot. A bag that isn't empty swaps on its own, as any slot swap does. This works from anywhere a slot swap does: a crate, a body, the ground, the stash.
- **Swapping guns (the user's call, 2026-11-06):** dropping a gun from a grid (a crate, a body, your own bags) on a gun you have equipped equips the new one and puts yours in **your backpack**, else rig, else pockets. **Each gun keeps its own magazine and rounds**: nothing is swapped between them. With no room in what you carry, it's the usual swap (yours goes where the new one was). A gun from the ground still swaps in place, onto the ground, as above. In the **stash** it's the usual swap too, since a gun put in the backpack would go into the next raid.
- **Rig and backpack sections (the user's call, 2026-11-06):** a rig's or backpack's grid can be split into sections, as in Tarkov, and **an item has to fit wholly inside one section**. Each section is drawn as its own box with a small gap around it, so it's clear they're separate. The **chest rig** is four 1×2 pouches (nothing wider than 1 goes in; a 2×1 goes in turned upright). The **small backpack** is a 4×2 main space over two 2×2 pockets. The **large backpack** is a 5×4 main space over a 2×2 pocket, a 1×2 pouch and another 2×2 pocket (5 is too wide for two 2×2s alone, so the 1×2 fills the middle). The admin backpack is one big section. **Rows of sections are centered on each other** (UX-3, 2026-11-08): sections whose rows overlap form a band, and each band is centered across the widest, so the large backpack's main space sits centered over its pockets instead of left-aligned. Bags saved before this are reshaped when loaded: whatever no longer fits moves to a free spot in the bag, else to the stash.
- **Putting things back on a body (the user's call, 2026-11-06):** a double-tap of yours with a body open (or a drop on its panel) fills **an empty gear slot of the body's that fits it first**, as a double-tap into your own gear does, then its pockets, rig and backpack. Before, it only tried the grids, so a gun taken off a scav, whose pockets are full of spare rounds, couldn't go back. Dragging onto a body's slot already worked, swapping with what's there: yours goes in its slot and its gun goes where yours came from.
- **The vault is an equipped item (the user's call, 2026-11-06):** the inventory shows it as the **Small vault**, equipped in a 2×2 slot beside its 2×2 grid, as a rig sits beside its grid, under "Kept if you die" (in a raid only since 2026-11-09, the user's call: the stash shows no heading). It can't be taken off, moved, dropped, sold or given: it's only shown, never an item you can touch. No grid accepts it either, and the admin page doesn't list it.
- **Weapon attachments** can be managed both in the main-menu stash and during a raid.
- **The attachment manager (session 10c)** is in the bar along the bottom of the inventory: select a gun (yours, a body's or one in a container) and its optic, muzzle and magazine slots appear there. **Drop an attachment on a slot, or straight on the gun** wherever it lies (a grid or a gear slot, outlined green when it fits; added at the user's request, 2026-10-26) to fit it; **drag a fitted one off** to a spot in a grid, or **onto another gun** it fits (what that gun had in the slot goes onto the first gun if it fits there, a swap, else into what you carry; added at the user's request, 2026-10-26); **tap a slot** for a picker of the attachments you can reach that fit it (yours first, then the open body's or container's), plus "Take off"; **double-tap a fitted one** to take it off into what you carry. Fitting over an existing attachment swaps them: the old one goes where the new one was. Changing attachments is instant, like any inventory move, but stops a reload if it's the magazine.
- **Items on the ground (session 11b, the ground panel chosen with the user 2026-10-27):**
  - **Picking up:** look at an item within 2 m (the crosshair within about 20° of it) and the use button says **take**, the item is drawn lighter and its name shows under the crosshair. A tap takes it as a double-tap would: into an empty gear slot it fits (a gun into a gun slot), else into what you carry; a stack that only partly fits leaves the rest. With no room, "No room for …" shows under the crosshair for 1.5 s. Aiming at an item beats a container or body nearby.
  - **The ground panel:** with nothing open, the right column of the inventory is **On the ground**: everything within reach, as tiles. **Drag** one of yours there (or select it and press **Drop**) to drop it; drag a tile into a grid or onto a gear slot, or **double-tap** it, to take it. Dropping a ground item on a gear slot swaps them: what was in the slot goes on the ground in its place. So a full bag is sorted out without closing the screen.
  - **Dropping** puts an item 0.9 m in front of you (where you can see it past the gun), or at your feet if a wall is closer, with a little scatter, on the surface below (so off a ladder it falls to the ground).
  - **What has nowhere to go is dropped, not refused:** the rounds a smaller magazine hands back, an attachment swapped off a gun, and one taken off "into what you carry". Taking one off to a spot that doesn't fit is still refused.
  - Items on the ground are drawn with their models (session 18b): guns, knives and magazines lie on their side, worn gear on its back.
- **The inventory layout (the user's call, 2026-10-30), closer to Tarkov's:** the gear is on the left **in body order**, as Tarkov's paper doll is (the user chose body-order slots over a silhouette or a live 3D model): helmet, then armor, centered, then the two primaries as wide slots with the sidearm beside primary 1 and the knife beside primary 2 (2026-11-09, the user's call; they were a row under both primaries before, and the gear column is now six cells wide instead of four); **on a phone (anything but a mouse) the loadout and what you carry are one column** (2026-11-09, the user's call): primary 1 and the sidearm, primary 2 and the knife, the helmet and armor side by side (each half the row, so together they are as wide as a primary and the knife, their pictures centered and names top left), then the rig, pockets, backpack and vault, each on its own row, beside the stash, body or container; beside it, the **rig and backpack slots each sit to the left of their own grid**, in the order rig, pockets, backpack, vault; the open body, container, ground or stash is on the right. A body shows its rig and backpack the same way, and **its gear, rig, pockets and backpack flow side by side when the column is wide enough** (a PC), so nobody misses the pockets and backpack below the fold (the user's call, 2026-11-01); on a phone they stack. **On a narrow screen (a phone, up to 1000 px wide) the stash goes in a row below the gear instead**, at full width, and the whole screen scrolls as one; in a raid, what's open stays on the right. **Holding a dragged item within a cell of a scrolling area's top or bottom edge scrolls it**, so you can drag from the stash up to your rig.
- **The stash and inventory rework (the user's call, 2026-11-06, after Delta Force's warehouse):** three framed panels side by side, on a phone too: **Loadout** (your gear slots, with what it would all sell for), **Carried** (rig, pockets, backpack, vault), and on the right the **stash page, body, container or the ground**. **Columns never move:** the Carried column is as wide as the biggest rig or backpack there is (not the admin ones), and the right one is a stash page wide, so putting on or taking off a bag moves nothing, and the right panel is anchored to the right with room past it. The cell size is the largest that fits all three across and about ten rows down (24 to 44 px). The long hint line is gone; a **?** in the Loadout panel shows it (in a raid only since 2026-11-09, the user's call: the stash has no **?**). **Stash panel:** its title shows what the stash would sell for; its header has the page tabs (a **+** buys the next page, tap twice; since H4 (2026-11-10) only once the Stash module allows it, else a **lock** whose tap says the Stash module level it needs; category pages, from the Stash module level 3, follow as their chip icons: hangar.md), the cells used on the page shown (e.g. 153/300) and **Sort**; the bar is shorter than the other panels' (2026-11-09), and on a phone the page tabs sit right against the panel's right edge, with no padding past them (by type, rarity or value: the page is repacked from the top left and stacks topped up; if it wouldn't all fit, nothing moves and it says so). Under it, the search and **category chips** (All, Guns, Ammo, Gear, Meds, Loot), which dim the rest as the search does. **Since 2026-11-09 (the user's call):** the chips are a column down the left of the page, **as icons** (a grid for All, a pistol, rounds, a vest, a cross, a gem; their names as tooltips), **on a phone too** (they were a row along the top there for a while the same day), 30 px squares, and the search box is hidden for now (its code is kept, may come back). **Select to sell** moved to the top bar. **Since 2026-11-09 (the user's call):** the stash screen's top bar is a **back arrow** (a 44 px wide tap target in a 32 px tall bar), **STASH**, and the **money on the right as "$1,234"** (no Vendors or raid button, and no line under it), and a **thin bar along the stash panel's bottom**, which stays put while the page scrolls, holds **Sort** (its menu opens upward) and **Sell** (what was Select to sell, the same picking mode); the header keeps the stash's worth and the cells used. On a PC, **hovering an item shows a card** with its name, rarity and kind, condition and what a vendor pays. **The look:** a darker, cooler palette, panels with title bars and an amber edge (Loadout and Carried lost their titles on 2026-11-09: Loadout's bar keeps only its worth, on the left, and **?** on the right, Carried has none; on a PC they sit 48 px apart (24 until later on 2026-11-09) and 24 px off the screen's left edge, tight on a phone), framed buttons, tiles in their rarity's color rising from the bottom edge (translucent, so the grid lines show through, 2026-11-09), and the main menu's cards to match.
- **Quick equip (the user's call, 2026-11-09):** a **Quick equip** button in a bar along the bottom of the equipment panel, in the stash only (`stash-quick-equip.ts`). It puts everything worn and carried into the stash (not the knife or the vault), then equips the best of what lies loose on the stash pages (not what's inside a stored rig or backpack). **All or none:** if what comes off won't fit back in the stash, nothing changes and a toast says **Stash full**. No undo.
  - **Guns:** only a gun with rounds of one kind for a full load and 3 reloads (rounds, not stacks, since stack sizes differ). The best gun is the one with the highest ammo level it has that many of, then a favorite, then the name A–Z (rarity and value dropped 2026-11-09, as guns are all common). It's loaded with that round and the 3 reloads are carried. Guns have a `class` (assault, smg, shotgun, dmr, sniper, lmg, pistol, special; optional, assault if left out): **special** guns (admin, zombies, launchers, the flamethrower, the crossbow) are never picked. The best **sidearm** with rounds is always taken. With a **sniper** primary, primary2 gets the best SMG or shotgun; with neither and no sidearm, any other primary.
  - **Armor:** the highest level, a piece under 30% durability ranking two levels lower, then durability, then rarity. **Rig and backpack:** the most cells, then the name; a stored one's contents go to the stash. Admin bags are never picked.
  - **Meds:** the heal with the most HP, a surgery kit, one tourniquet or two bandages (bandage meds by uses left, up to 4 uses or 2 items), a splint; whatever's missing is skipped. Meds and the reload rounds go in pockets, then the rig, then the backpack; what doesn't fit stays in the stash.
- **Favorite items (the user's call, 2026-11-06):** any item's details has **☆ Favorite** (★ once it is). A favorite is a kind of item, not one copy: every one of it gets a **gold star** in its tile's corner, everywhere. Where one turns up in a **body, a container or on the ground**, its tile also gets a **glowing gold outline**, so it's seen at a glance (not in your own gear or the stash, where it would be noise). A gun with a favorite fitted, or a rig or backpack with one inside, lights up too. The PC hover card says ★ favorite. Favorites live in the settings (`favorites`, item ids, at most 200, the oldest dropped past that), so they follow the account to other devices; resetting the settings keeps them.
- **Short names on tiles (the user's call, 2026-11-06, as Tarkov's):** an item can have a `shortName` (no length limit since 2026-11-11, the user's call; a long one shows only the whole lines that fit) that its tile shows where the full name won't fit: rounds as caliber and type ("5.56 M855", "9mm Pst", ".308 M80", "54R 7N1", "12ga Buck"), and short forms for small things ("Band.", "Med", "Holo", "Brake", "M67", "P226 20" for that magazine). The details and the hover card keep the full name, and the stash search finds either. A tile's name breaks only between words, never inside one, and shows only the whole lines that fit above its badges, smaller on a phone's smaller cells.
