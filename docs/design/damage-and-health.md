# Game design: Damage, armor and health

Part of the [design.md index](../design.md). Record decisions here, in the topic they belong to.

## Damage, armor and health
- **Hit zones (GZ-3, 2026-11-12, the user's call): head, chest (the thorax), stomach, left and right arm, left and right leg.** HP 35, 85, 70, 60, 60, 65, 65 (440 in all); shot multipliers 1, 1, 1, 0.7, 0.7, 0.75, 0.75. **Head and thorax are vital; the stomach isn't.** The stomach is its own pair of capsules under the thorax, from about 1.11 m down to the hips (0.86 m), where the chest used to reach; the thorax runs from there to the shoulders. The 2026-11-08 note below, which skipped the split, is history. In code the thorax is still `CHEST` ("chest"), as carriers call it `thorax`.
  - **The face (GZ-3)** isn't a zone with HP: it's a head hit **from within 70° of where the body faces and under the brow** (the head capsule's upper end), sharing the head's HP. Only armor cares: a face hit meets a fitted visor, else a helmet that **covers the face** (`coversFace`: Jug's mask), else **nothing: the face is bare** (the user's call, 2026-11-12; until GZ-3 the helmet took every head hit a visor didn't). Any other head hit meets the helmet. The damage log and the post-raid fight log name stomach and face hits (a face lights the head with a face on it).
  - **Coverage (the user's call, 2026-11-12):** the tactical vest's soft armor and the heavy plate carrier's plates cover thorax and stomach; the plate carrier and assault armor stay thorax-only. An armored rig covers the thorax only.
  - **Measured (GZ-3, `client/src/shots-to-kill.measure.test.ts`):** a PMC's AKM (PS) on someone standing still in the open, facing it, no helmet, 40 runs each; mean hits to kill at 10 / 25 / 50 m. Before the split: unarmored 2.5 / 2.4 / 2.9, tactical vest 3.6 / 3.5 / 4.7, plate carrier 4.7 / 4.6 / 5.6. After: unarmored 3.0 / 2.6 / 3.5, tactical vest (now covering the stomach) 4.2 / 3.7 / 5.3, plate carrier (thorax only) 5.3 / 4.8 / 6.5. So thorax-only armor came out **a little tankier, not rougher**: a bare stomach hit is full damage but not vital, and its 70 HP is new. Head and face together still take about a fifth of the hits at 10 and 25 m. Levers if it should be rougher: a stomach multiplier over 1, less stomach HP, or more of the blacking hit spilled.
- **Session 6 hit zones (before GZ-3):** head, chest, left arm, right arm, left leg, right leg. Each has its own HP pool and damage multiplier. Session 6 values: HP 35, 85, 60, 60, 65, 65 (370 in all); shot multipliers 1, 1, 0.7, 0.7, 0.75, 0.75. Damage per shot is the ammo's. **The head is ×1** (the user's call, 2026-11-08; it was ×1.5): every bullet of 35 damage or more still one-shots an unarmored head, but a single buckshot pellet (33) no longer kills through it, 4.6 AP and Igolnik need two head hits, and a round level with a helmet gets half its damage through rather than 75%, so a matching round no longer kills through a helmet in one hit (AKM 52 → 26). A stomach zone (70 HP, non-lethal) and a 100 HP chest were weighed the same day and skipped for now: nothing felt too fast yet; the stomach split, with the chest kept at 85 and the vest covering both, is the fix if unarmored fights later feel over too soon. All tuning is in `HEALTH` in `packages/shared/src/sim/health.ts`.
- **Hit zones are a fixed capsule layout** (decided 2026-10-26, session 6), worked out from the character's position, yaw and crouch, not attached to animated bones. That keeps hits in the simulation, the same on a co-op server with no renderer. The drawn body (session 8b) is posed to match.
- **The head zone follows the pose (the user's report, 2026-11-04):** shots could hit the drawn head and miss its capsule, because the animation clips carry the head well forward of the layout (measured: 3.5 cm standing, 14 cm walking, 20 cm jogging, 38 cm sprinting, plus crouching and looking down). A sphere wouldn't help: the position was wrong, not the shape. The head capsule now moves with speed and direction, crouch and pitch, in the sim, so a co-op host needs no skeleton. The chest and limbs are still fixed.
  - **Arms are held forward** (decided with the user, 2026-10-26, session 8b): every armed pose has both arms out in front, so the arm capsules run from the shoulders down to the elbows and in to the hands in front of the chest. From the front, arms now shield part of the chest (×0.7 instead of ×1). A sprinting or unarmed body doesn't quite match. The chest became two narrower capsules side by side, so it's wider than deep like the drawn torso and doesn't swallow shots at the forearms.
  - AI still aims at 0.6 of body height (about 1.08 m standing), just under the forearms, so some of its shots now land on arms. Retune if fights feel softer.
- **Armor:** helmets cover the head, chest armor covers the chest. Armor loses durability as it's hit; at 0 durability it stops nothing. Armor can be repaired with armor repair kits.
  - ~~Session 6: it absorbs a fixed percentage until it breaks.~~ Replaced by levels (below, 2026-10-30).
- **Ammo and armor: Tarkov's numbers on fixed levels (the user's call, 2026-11-05; levels since 2026-10-30).** Rounds and armor take their numbers from Tarkov (damage, penetration as a level, armor wear, blunt damage); the hit itself is decided by a **fixed level comparison** (from Delta Force, chosen 2026-10-30 over Tarkov's random penetration chance), and **rarity follows level**. Both are this game's own and stay. In the docs, "levels" means the comparison and "Tarkov" where the numbers come from.
  - **Levels 1–6** for every round and armor piece, colored gray, green, blue, purple, gold and red (a small level tag in item details and the ammo readout; item rarity colors the tile, so tiles have no level tag, the user's call, 2026-11-09).
  - **A hit on armor with durability left** compares levels: **level with it: half** gets through; **one level above: 75%; two or more above: all** (the user's call, 2026-11-04). **Below it: nothing gets through** (the user's call, 2026-11-08: the "absolute gate"), and it never starts bleeding. From 2026-11-05 a round below the armor's level let blunt damage through (25% through a helmet, 15% through body armor, after Tarkov), but that let cheap rounds kill through the best armor (PMCs killed Jug with level 2–3 headshots through his level 6 mask) and favored high-damage, low-level rounds. Every hit still wears the armor. Once broken, everything gets through. No dice: the same shot always does the same thing. Arms and legs are never covered.
  - **Wear:** every hit takes the round's `armorDamage` in durability, ×1 against armor at or below the round's level, ×0.8 one level above, ×0.6 two, ×0.4 three or more. So low rounds can still strip armor, slowly.
  - **From Tarkov to levels (2026-11-05):**
    - **Level = Tarkov penetration ÷ 10, rounded down** (1–6; Tarkov's armor classes are about 10 apart). A round within one of a boundary may move across it to fill an empty level (TCW SP 30 → 2, T-45M1 30 → 2, PBP 39 → 4).
    - **Damage = Tarkov damage × 0.85**, anchored on the rounds that already matched (PS 61 → 52, M855 54 → 46). Lower-penetration rounds do more damage, often much more (Hydra-Shok-style JHP 85 against .45 AP 56).
    - **Armor damage ≈ 0.9 × Tarkov penetration × Tarkov armor-damage %** (Tarkov's durability lost per hit; M80 43 × 67% × 0.9 = 26).
    - **Guns don't change damage** (Tarkov's barrels change velocity slightly); a gun's power is its round's. Rounds the game invented (incendiaries, bolts, dragon's breath) keep their numbers, scaled with their caliber where it moved.
    - **Low-level, high-damage rounds** (Ultra Nosler, HP, JHP, HP BT) kill unarmored targets fast and do nothing through better armor: for scavs, limbs and heads without helmets. The knife counts as level 1, so armor stops it outright (it wore it down only, since 2026-11-08).

  - **Top armor is cheaper than it "should" be**, because of the rule that repairing never makes money: a kit point costs 200, so armor can't sell for more than that per point of durability (value under 400 a point). Making high-level armor pricier needs repairs to cost more on it (points by level, say): **open**.
  - **Picking rounds:** a gun fires any round of its caliber. A **tap on reload** loads more of what's in it if you carry any, else the highest level you carry. Rounds aren't mixed in a gun. **No rounds picker (the user's call, 2026-10-30):** holding reload used to open one; to load a different round, drag it onto the gun in the inventory. The sim still takes a chosen round with a reload (`PlayerInput.ammo`), unused by the client for now.
  - **Four starting kits (the user's call, 2026-11-05, as Tarkov gives a fresh account several mid-tier kits):** `new-player` is now **four rare-tier (blue) kits**, one worn and three loose in the stash, with no money. Each is an M416, 3 stacks (180) of M855, an M9A3 with 45 rounds of Pst, a tactical vest, a steel helmet, a chest rig, a medium backpack (small until 2026-11-07, when the small shrank to 3×2), 2 bandages, a splint, a medkit and a surgery kit. This replaces the AKM kit, and `new-player` is also the play-test kit. There's no rare rig, and the backpack wasn't asked to be rare. Worth about 110,000 a kit, 440,000 in all, which is about 165,000 at vendors for the three in the stash. Existing accounts keep what they have until wiped. Everything below that describes the kit is from before this.
  - **Guns come empty (the user's call, 2026-11-05, extending 2026-11-01's rule for vendors and quest rewards):** a gun in a starting kit has no rounds unless the kit's `loaded` names one for it. In `new-player` that's all four M416s, full of blue M855, and all four M9A3s, full of Pst. The zombies kit names `m9a3` too. Guns on the ground, in loot and on enemies still come loaded. **A found gun's rounds (the user's call, 2026-11-09):** a gun rolled from a loot table (a container, a loose spot, an enemy's pockets) comes with one full magazine of a round picked at random from its caliber's **common or uncommon rounds of level 1 or 2** (`createLootGun`), never its own `ammo` round unless that qualifies; a caliber with none would leave it empty, and a content test makes sure every looted gun has some. 6.8x51 had none, so it gained **6.8 HP** (level 1, common) and **6.8 SP** (level 2, uncommon), sold by Kovacs from levels 1 and 2 and in the ammo table. Guns an enemy is armed with still load their loadout's round.
  - **Kit guns can come fitted (the user's request, 2026-11-08):** a starting kit's `fitted` names attachments by gun id, and every copy of that gun in the kit gets them (worn or in the stash); a gun not named comes bare. In `new-player`, all four M416s carry a **red dot**, adding about 4,000 a kit. Existing accounts keep what they have until wiped.
  - **A dead enemy's gun keeps its state (the user's call, 2026-11-05):** a body's gear is the enemy's own, so its gun has the rounds it had when it died, of the same kind, and a reload it died during never finishes. Tests cover this in `sim/looting.test.ts`.
  - **Who has what:** Kovacs sells levels 1–3 (plus the snipers' level 4 M80 and LPS) and armor up to level 3; level 4 and up is found in raids (the military table, rarer the higher) or on bosses. Scavs carry levels 1–3 rounds and mostly no or worn level 1–3 armor (a plate carrier now and then); the yard boss loads PS, BP or AP and wears level 4–5. **The starting kit wears level 3** (the user's call, 2026-10-30, after first trying level 2): steel helmet and tactical vest, with PS (level 3) rounds; it used to be the steel helmet (3) and plate carrier (4), which made a fresh player tougher than most scavs could hurt. Accounts made before keep what they have.
  - **Rarity by level:** rounds 1–6 are common, uncommon, rare, epic, legendary and mythic, so a round's tile matches its level tag, with no exceptions (the user's call, 2026-11-04: a round's rarity follows only its penetration, so incendiary rounds, bolts and flamethrower fuel follow it too); armor the same, levels 1–6 common to mythic (the user's call, 2026-11-04). Armored rigs follow their armor level too, not their room (2026-11-11).
  - **Armored rigs (the user's call, 2026-11-11):** a rig with an `armor` block (level, durability, repair loss) is chest armor as well as a rig: the same coverage (chest only), level comparison, wear, breaking at 0, full-heal reset and repairs (kits, with repair loss) as body armor. **Body armor can't be worn with one**: while one is on, the armor slot is shut (hatched, naming the rig), and putting on either over the other is **refused, nothing moved**, with a message on the tile. Item details show its protection, durability and repair loss beside its compartments; its tile badge is its durability. Quick equip picks the best chest protection of the two kinds by level, then durability. An enemy loadout rolling both keeps the rig and drops the body armor. First one: `armored-chest-rig` (level 3, 55 durability, four 1×2 pouches), not yet sold or in loot.
  - **What rarity colors say (the user's call, 2026-11-09):** a round's or armor piece's level, a loot item's rarity or worth, and a **rig's or backpack's room**. **Guns and attachments are all common**: a gun is just a gun, not a special find like a gold bar (zombies' and admin ones included). Rigs and backpacks go by the slots they hold (their sections' cells, or the whole grid's): **2026-11-11, the user's call**, up to 6 common, 8 uncommon, 12 rare, 16 epic, 20 legendary, more mythic (red) (`BAG_RARITY_CELLS`, `bagRarity`). This is an **auto-fill, not a rule**: the item editor sets the rarity whenever a bag's slots change, and it can then be picked by hand (a note under the picker shows the size's rarity and a button back to it). The existing bags' sizes are to be cut down, so they don't set the scale. The vault keeps its own color. `checkItems` holds only guns and attachments to common.
  - **Rarity color means something you can check, or it isn't there (the user's call, 2026-11-09).** Every color stands for a fact a player can confirm: a level (ammo, armor), room (rigs, backpacks) or worth (loot). It's never a designer's sense that something is "nicer", which is how gun rarity had become meaningless. So **attachments stay common**: a 4x scope isn't a better holo sight, and a suppressor and a muzzle brake trade different things, so coloring some of them would claim a ranking that doesn't exist. Common is already the neutral look, so attachments need no separate "no rarity" style either; what tells them apart is their picture, name and category. **If a kind of item gains a real, measurable tier** (suppressors by loudness, say), its color can follow that stat, as ammo's follows its level, with `checkItems` holding it to it. Before giving anything new a rarity, name the fact its color states.
  - **Consumables rank by strength (the user's call, 2026-11-09):** meds, surgery kits and repair kits that do more (more HP or repair points, more uses, faster to use) are a higher rarity than weaker ones that do the same job. That ranking is real, unlike attachments', so the color can carry it. It's a comparison within a job (a better bandage against a bandage, a bigger medkit against a medkit), not one stat, so it's set by hand and `checkItems` doesn't check it.
- **A limb at 0 HP is "broken"** (there's only one state):
  - Legs: slower movement.
  - Arms: more weapon sway and slower aiming.
  - **Damage to a broken limb spills over** at a reduced amount (about 70%, tunable), **spread evenly over every zone with HP left, the head included** (as in Tarkov; the user's call, 2026-11-01). It used to go all to the chest, so a chest could run out while the total still looked healthy, and the total meant little. A share bigger than a zone has left is lost, not passed on.
  - **So does the rest of the hit that breaks it** (session 6): a 40 damage hit on a leg with 10 HP left breaks it and spreads 30 × 70% = 21 over the five other zones, 4.2 each. Without this, huge hits and falls to the legs could never kill. With the spread, the head (35 HP) is usually what gives out first.
  - Session 6 values: each broken leg multiplies speed by 0.75; each broken arm multiplies spread by 1.6 and aim time by 1.5.
- **A blacked stomach (GZ-3, the user's call, 2026-11-12):** the stomach at 0 HP. **Further hits on it spread 1.5× over the zones with HP left** (limbs stay at 0.7), and so does the rest of the hit that blacks it. Until **surgery** (a splint doesn't treat it): **stamina is capped at half and refills at half speed, and held breath lasts 0.6 as long** (design/movement.md → Stamina), and **you groan and cough every 4–8 s, heard by enemies as far as sprinting footsteps (15 m)**, walls or not, who come to check as for footsteps. Enemies with one groan too (players hear it) but get nothing else: they have no stamina. HUD: the 7th zone and a 🤢 chip. Painkillers and timed effects will be able to quiet it (Pain and tremors, PS-1). No hydration or food, ever. Tuning: `HEALTH.stomachSpillover`, `STAMINA.stomach*`, `PAIN`.
- **Head or chest at 0 HP = death.**
- **Bleeding:** heavy hits can cause bleeding, which slowly drains HP until a bandage stops it.
  - Session 6: each ammo type has a **bleed chance** per hit that gets through armor. A bleeding zone loses 1 HP per second (through the same spillover rules), so bleeding out of a broken limb drains the chest.
  - **Weakened (the user's call, 2026-11-01):** it was too strong in the short term, with 1 HP/s per bleeding zone stacking. Now bleeding takes **0.6 HP/s in all, however many zones bleed**, split evenly between them (a broken limb's share spreads like any other spillover). More wounds no longer bleed faster; more wounds only need more bandages. **One kind of bleed only**: Tarkov's light and heavy bleeds were considered and left out for simplicity (the user's call, same day).
- **Fall damage** (session 6, retuned 2026-10-26 because the user found it hurt too soon): landing faster than 12.5 m/s (a drop of about 4 m, so one-storey roofs are safe) hurts both legs by 15 per m/s over that, each. About 5 m bruises, about 7 m breaks both legs, and above about 14 m the spillover kills (through the head; 11 m while spillover went to the chest). A jump never hurts. (It was 10 m/s and 12 per m/s: hurt from about 2.5 m.)
- **Treatment items:**
  - Splint: fast and cheap. It removes the broken effect, but the limb stays at 0 HP and spillover still applies.
  - Surgery kit: slow and rare. It brings a broken limb back to 1 HP.
  - Medkit: has an HP pool and heals HP.
  - **The medkit is the field first aid kit (the user's call, 2026-11-09):** id `field-first-aid-kit` (was `medkit`; profile format 10 renames old ones), "First aid" on its tile, 6 s a use, 220 HP. Like every heal it neither stops bleeding nor treats broken limbs. "Medkit" elsewhere in these docs means this kind of med.
  - **A heal can stop bleeding (`bleedCost`, the user's call, 2026-11-09, for the medical supply crate: 400 HP, 5 s, 30 HP a bleed):** a heal with `bleedCost` first stops every bleeding zone, down the treatment order, for that much of its pool each (or what's left), then heals with the rest, all in one use. The heal button offers it for bleeding after any bandage, so a tap still uses the quick, cheap bandage first; it's in the long-press picker, and the tap's choice when you have no bandage. The AI doesn't use it for bleeds. Heals without it never stop a bleed.
  - **Heal prices (the user's call to balance them, 2026-11-09):** anchored on the field first aid kit (4,000, about 18 a point of HP, the one the medic sells). The medical pouch is 1,500 (about 17 a point: common and small, but 1×1). The medical supply crate is 10,000 (25 a point): 400 HP is worth about 7,300 at the kit's rate, plus stopping bleeds (a bandage is 200 a bleed), a faster use and an epic's rarity. Vendors pay half, by HP left.
  - Bandage: stops bleeding. **2 uses, 3 s each** (the user's call, 2026-11-09; it was single use, 2 s).
  - **Tourniquet (the user's call, 2026-11-09):** also stops bleeding, **4 uses, 2.5 s each**, common like the bandage. In the medical loot table (half the bandage's weight) and sold by the medic.
  - Items target limbs in a **fixed priority order: head, chest, stomach, left leg, right leg, left arm, right arm** (the stomach since GZ-3). There's no body diagram. Surgery treats a blacked stomach before broken legs; a medkit skips it, as it skips broken limbs.
  - Session 9: each use of a bandage, splint or surgery kit treats **one zone**, the first in that order that needs it (a splint can have several uses). A medkit heals down the order from its pool, **skipping broken limbs** (they need surgery first), and keeps what's left of its pool for next time.
