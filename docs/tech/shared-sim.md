# Tech stack: Shared simulation

Part of the [tech-stack.md index](../tech-stack.md). Record decisions here, in the topic they belong to.

## Shared simulation (`packages/shared`)
- Plain TypeScript.
- **Fixed 30 Hz tick:** `step(state, world, inputs)` advances the state **in place** (decided in session 3, so a tick allocates nothing). `FixedTimestep` turns frame times into ticks. The renderer interpolates between ticks, copying the few values it draws before each tick. Look direction is applied to the camera every frame, not every tick, so turning never lags.
- **Serialisable inputs and state.** This is what lets co-op run the same code on the host's device (and, if ever needed, a server). Bit-exact determinism isn't needed (the host is authoritative in co-op).
  - The state (`SimState`) is plain JSON-safe data: entities, the tick count and the RNG state. A test checks that a JSON round trip continues identically.
  - The static map is not in the state. It's a `CollisionWorld`, built once from a plain `WorldDef` (terrain grid, boxes, ramps and ladders) that the map loader produces.
  - Inputs are per entity per tick: the move stick, absolute yaw and pitch, and sprint, crouch and jump. Crouch is the wanted stance, so hold vs. toggle stays a client setting.
  - **Prone and vaulting (2026-10-30):** `PlayerInput.prone` is optional (left out, not prone) and wins over crouch; stance is still just `Character.height` (prone below `crouchHeight`). A vault is its own `MoveMode`, `'vault'`, with the move in `Character.vaulting` (start, end, the top cleared, heights before and after, time in), so it survives a JSON round trip and a resumed raid; snapshots from before simply have no vault in progress. `startVault` (`sim/movement.ts`) finds one from the collision world when jump is pressed on the ground: a ray at knee height, `groundAt` every 10 cm across the top, `overlaps` for the gap (sliding sideways in 2.5 cm steps) and the landing. That's a few dozen grid queries, only on a jump press, and it allocates only when a vault starts. During the move nothing collides and characters aren't separated.
- **Combat (session 6):** `step(state, world, items, inputs)`. The item database (`ItemDb`, every item by id) is static like the world, so it's passed in rather than kept in the state. Each tick everyone moves (then takes fall damage), then everyone's gun updates (so shots see this tick's positions), then bleeding. Inputs gained `fire`, `aim` (both held) and `reload` (a press).
  - Character state gained `health` (HP and bleeding per zone, dead), `helmet` and `bodyArmour` (item and durability), `weapon` (item, rounds, spare rounds, cooldown, reload timer), `aim` (0–1) and the recoil offsets. Session 9 moved armour and guns into `gear` and spare rounds into the inventory (see Inventory model).
  - **Attachments (session 6b):** the weapon state has `attachments` (the item id in each slot, or null). `weaponStats(items, weapon, out)` works out the gun's stats with them into a scratch object, so a tick still allocates nothing. `attach` and `detach` change them (an attachment that doesn't fit throws; check with `fits`). Since session 10c the player changes them only through the `attach` and `detach` inventory actions (see Looting and inventory actions); `createWeapon` and loadouts still call them directly. Shot events carry `loudness` for AI hearing.
  - `state.events` lists what happened during the last tick (`shot`, `damage`, `death`) for effects, hit markers and the HUD. It's cleared each tick; events are the only allocation, and only when something happens.
  - **Hit zones** are six capsules in a fixed layout (`HIT_CAPSULES` in `sim/hit-zones.ts`), turned by yaw and squashed by crouch height. `raycastCharacters` checks each living character's bounding sphere, then its capsules. **No spatial hash for moving things:** with tens of characters, a sphere test each is cheaper than keeping a hash up to date. Revisit if a raid gets past about 100 characters.
  - **Head offset (2026-11-04):** `headOffset(c, out)` in `sim/hit-zones.ts` gives the head capsule's side, height and forward shift from `vx`/`vz`, `height` and `pitch`, measured from the mannequin's clips (head vertices averaged over each clip). `capsuleEnds` adds it before squash and lean, and `CapsuleBody` draws it for `?hitzones` and `/bodies`. Re-measure if the clips or the `spineLean`/`headLean` numbers change.
  - **Leaning (2026-11-01):** `PlayerInput.lean` (−1..1) and `Character.lean` (optional; missing is 0), eased in `moveCharacter` and cut short by `fitLean` (a ray from the upright eye to the leaned one, called in `step` after bodies push apart). Everything above `MOVEMENT.leanPivot` × height turns about the forward axis through that point: `eyeX`/`eyeZ`/`eyeHeight` for the eye, `bodyPoint(c, h, out)` for a point on the centre line, and `capsuleEnds` for each hit capsule's ends (squashed first, then tipped). Anything measuring from a character's eye must use all three eye functions, not `c.x, eyeHeight(c), c.z`. Enemies' sight (`sightRate`) and aim (`fight`) use `bodyPoint` on their target; their own eyes stay at `c.x, c.z` since they don't lean. `sightRate` multiplies by `AI.leaningRate` scaled by how far the target leans.
- **Seeded RNG** for loot rolls, enemy loadouts, AI decisions, shot spread and bleeding.
- **Collision:** written in-house, no physics engine:
  - A capsule for the player and AI, against rotated boxes, the terrain height grid and stair ramps.
  - **Footprints rotate about Y only** (session 3). A standing capsule against a solid is then a circle against a rectangle in XZ plus a height overlap: simple and robust.
  - **Tilted solids are slabs (the user's call, 2026-11-05, replacing "a tilted solid collides as its upright bounding box"; a bounding box made a container tilted as a ramp a 5 m invisible block):** `BoxDef` stays the solid's bounds, so loot, overlaps and the cut view need no change, and an optional `slope` (`BoxSlope`) adds a sloped top and underside over the footprint. You can walk up the top, walk under the raised end and walk around inside something hollow, and rays hit the same planes. Tilted boxes and cylinders (as their box) become these, with `tiltedBox` in `compile.ts` picking as top and underside the pair of faces with the most floor under them, so a thin board stays a board however steep. The ends are upright, not leaning, and the slab is cut to the box's bounds. A tilted wedge has a top and underside of its own (`tiltedWedge`). `pushOut` backs a head up a sloped underside rather than out through a side. Full oriented boxes were turned down: they'd rewrite the circle-against-rectangle core for little gain on a phone.
  - **Touching isn't under (2026-11-03):** `ceilingAt` ignores solids that reach less than 1 mm (`SOLID_CONTACT`) over the circle. `pushOut` leaves a capsule against a wall at its radius, give or take rounding, so a roof flush with the wall's face (every shipping container) stopped any jump made against it. **`groundAt` does the same (2026-11-04):** the container's floor slab is flush with the wall's face too, so walking into the wall lifted you onto a floor on its far side. Its reach is clamped at radius 0, since point queries pass 0.
  - Ramps are wedges (solid underneath). Stairs are drawn as steps but collide as a ramp.
  - Terrain cells are two triangles split along the diagonal from (i+1, j) to (i, j+1). The renderer must use the same split, or feet float or sink.
  - The character controller is kinematic. It pushes out of walls horizontally (ignoring anything at or below step height), then follows the ground up steps and down slopes, or falls. When landing, it looks for surfaces between the old and new feet, so fast falls don't tunnel through thin floors.
  - A spatial hash (`StaticGrid`, 4 m cells) for the static solids; raycasts walk its cells in order.
  - **Doors (session 11c)** are solids after the boxes and ramps (`WorldDef.doors`, `firstDoor`, `doorOf(solid)`), each with three poses precomputed (closed, open into its +z, open into its −z); `setDoorOpen(door, open, flipped)` copies one in, and the door's grid cells cover all three. A door swings away from whoever opens it: `useDoor` sets `DoorState.flipped` from which side of the doorway they stand, and it's kept while closed so the renderer swings it back from that side. That's the only thing in the world that changes. The truth is `SimState.doors` (`{ open, locked }` by index, the order of `CompiledMap.doors`); `syncDoors(state, world, nav)` brings the world and nav grid in line, at the start of every `step` and once after loading, so a restored state on a fresh world is right. Using a door is `PlayerInput.door` (an index, a press), applied after inventory actions; `useDoor` checks reach (2 m from the doorway or the open slab) and keys, and pushes a `door` event (`opened`, `closed`, `unlocked`, `locked`, `blocked`). Opening snaps; a body it lands on is pushed out by its next move. The renderer eases the swing over 0.2 s (`updateDoors`).
  - **Deep water (MT-1, 2026-11-09, the user's call; design.md → Raids and maps):** `WorldDef.water` is a list of bodies (`WaterDef`: a rectangle centred on x, z with half-extents hx, hz, a yaw, and its surface `level`), from `water` nodes and the map's own `water` (one body over the whole terrain). `waterLevelAt` gives the highest surface over a point (a plain loop: maps have a handful); `CollisionWorld.waterDepth(x, z)` is that minus the terrain (0 over a hole or outside every body). Deeper than `DEEP_WATER` (1.2 m) stops bodies like a wall: after `pushOut` in `walk`, and in `shove`, `keepOutOfDeepWater` refuses a move whose spot is that deep, keeping whichever axis it can so you slide along the bank. Depth counts only when the terrain is what's underfoot (`groundAt` finds no solid), so bridges, decks and tunnel floors over water are fine, and walking or jumping off a bridge's side is refused too. Someone already in deep water (a spawn, or a shove before this) may move to anywhere no deeper, so they can wade out; that also lets them walk a flat bed, accepted because nothing normal puts you there. A vault that would land in deep water is refused (a low walkway's rail). Shots, sight and throws ignore water. The nav grid has no terrain node where it's deep, so the AI paths only over crossings.
  - **Foliage (MT-3, 2026-11-10, the user's call):** `WorldDef.foliage` holds sight-only boxes, kept in a `CollisionWorld` of their own (`leaves`) so movement, shots, grenades and the nav grid never meet them. `foliageWithin` asks it along a ray, sharing raycast's grid walk (`nearestSolid`). `clearLine` in ai.ts checks it after the solid raycast, so the AI can't see through leaves; hearing and a grenade landing's blast line pass `throughLeaves`. Raw `raycast` callers (cover slack, open reach) ignore foliage, which isn't cover.
  - Raycasts for hitscan and line of sight, against solids and terrain, with the hit normal. Queries reuse scratch space and allocate nothing.
- **Navigation:** a navigation grid with multiple layers (floors), generated from the map data. It links stairs and slopes, but not ladders. A* pathfinding.
  - **Searched looks (2026-11-03, design.md → Inventory and UI → Looting):** `loot.searched` on a node names a prefab (optional, so maps and prefabs stay format 3); it's placed in the node's frame, drawn only, and takes the colours of slots it shares with the container's scope. `compileMap` gives `searchedLooks` ({ id, closed, searched }, by container id). **`CompiledMap.shapes` keeps the closed shapes**, so the map tab and anything else reading it get the closed look, and `compileNodes` has no looks, so the editor and thumbnails do too; only `buildMap` leaves those shapes out of the chunks (`closed` are the same objects). A prefab holding such a container, or one that is the container, is flattened even if `instanced`. A missing prefab is a content error. The truth is `LootContainer.searched` (missing is false, as in older saves) in `SimState.loot`, set only by `searchContainer` from `PlayerInput.search` (a container id, a press), which checks reach and pushes a `search` event the first time. The client sends it in one place, where the use button opens a container; a timed search would send it when it ends. The renderer draws each such container's two looks as baked meshes of their own, one visible, so that's one draw call per container with a look; `updateSearchedLooks` follows the sim every frame and once on loading, so a resumed raid shows them open. If maps fill with cabinets, merge a chunk's unsearched looks. **Aiming follows the look:** `lootableInReach` also tests the crosshair against the bounds of a searched container's `searched` shapes, up to the first solid it meets (`searchedAimedAt`, boxes cached per `sim.loot`). The look still has no solids, so it doesn't block walking or shots.
  - **Glass (2026-11-02, design.md → Movement → Glass)** are solids after the doors (`WorldDef.glass`, `firstGlass`, `glassOf(solid)`): a thin box per pane. `setGlassBroken` moves a broken pane's solid 1e9 m away, so every query misses it without a check of its own. `raycast(..., hit, seeThrough)` skips glass for lines of sight (AI sight and hearing, `exposure.ts`, blast reach to glass). The truth is `SimState.glass` (true is broken, by the order of `CompiledMap.glass`; filled in all whole for a state saved before glass); `syncGlass` runs with `syncDoors`. `sim/glass.ts`: `breakGlass` pushes a `glass` event (loudness 12); `castShot` breaks panes in the way (up to 4) and re-casts, unless someone is hit first; the knife breaks the pane its swing hits; `explode` (not flash or smoke) runs `breakGlassNear`; an impact fuse breaks the pane it hits. The nav grid is unchanged: a window was never walkable. The renderer draws all panes as **one instanced mesh** (`MeshBasicMaterial`, opacity 0.3, no depth write, a colour per instance) and scales a broken one to 0 (`updateGlass`); breaking scatters 10 puffs of its tint (`Effects.shatter`) and plays `glass-break`.
  - **Links through thin things (2026-11-05):** a side link also needs its halfway point as clear as a node, because two nodes can straddle a 4 cm fence bar (a strip 0.44 m wide on a 0.5 m grid). It took Northpoint's nav build on the Mac from about 360 ms to 580 ms (857k nodes, one run each).
  - **Session 7:** `NavGrid` (`nav/nav-grid.ts`) is built at load from the `CollisionWorld` (about 80 ms and 146k nodes for the 192 m test map on the Mac). It's static like the world and never part of the state. Columns every **0.5 m**; each holds a node for every surface (terrain, or a solid's top) where a standing body fits: no solid in the way from a step's height (0.45 m) to 1.8 m above it, within **0.2 m** (less than the body's 0.35 m radius, so 1 m doorways are always open however the grid lines up; bodies slide along the walls). A surface less than a step under another is dropped (stood on through it). Terrain steeper than 45° isn't walkable. Each node stores a bitmask of links to its 8 neighbours (a neighbour within a step's height; diagonals only if both sides are linked, so no corner cutting). Neighbours are found by looking in the column, not stored. A flood fill numbers the **connected components**, so "can it get there at all" is a lookup; the overlay and the editor's unreachable-spot view (session 17) use it.
  - `findPath` (`nav/path.ts`) is A* with an octile estimate plus climb, on preallocated typed arrays (it allocates nothing but its output). It stops after 20,000 nodes. Between different components it heads greedily for the closest spot it can find, with a smaller budget, and says `partial`. The path is smoothed by walking straight lines cell by cell over links (`straight`), so it only turns where it has to.
  - **Doors (session 11c, see design.md → AI → Doors).** The grid is **built with every door open**, both ways in turn (only spots clear of both slabs are kept, since a door can swing either way), so the open slab is kept clear of and the closed doorway is walkable. It records which links cross each door's closed footprint; `setDoorLocked` clears or restores those link bits (so A*, `straight` and the components all see a locked door without any per-search check), and `updateComponents` renumbers. Closed, unlocked doors stay links: instead of the path follower knowing which links cross a door, an enemy moving along a path opens a closed, unlocked door within 1.2 m that the line to its next waypoint crosses (`openDoorAhead`, called from `updateAi`). **The components lookup is recomputed after an unlock** (decided 2026-10-26): about 11–13 ms for the test map on the Mac (perhaps 15–25 ms on the iPhone), one dropped frame at a rare, deliberate moment. If that hitch shows on the phone: unlocking only ever joins components, never splits them, so relabelling just the smaller side (a key room's few hundred nodes) is enough.
- **AI (session 7):** each enemy is an ordinary `Character` with `ai: AiState` (plain data: awareness, activity, timers, focus, patrol and path as flat number arrays), so the state still saves and restores as JSON. **AI writes a `PlayerInput` for its character**, which moves and shoots through the same code as a player's; an input passed to `step` for that entity overrides it.
  - `step(state, world, items, inputs, nav?)`: after moving, shooting and bleeding, `updateAi` runs, so it perceives this tick's positions and events (shots, hits) and acts next tick. Without a nav grid enemies stand still (tests and the perf scene).
  - Sight and footsteps are checked every 3 ticks, staggered across enemies; shot and hit events every tick. **At most 2 path searches per tick across all enemies**; the rest wait a tick. An enemy plans again when its goal moves more than 1.5 m, or after 1.5 s stuck (and gives up after 3 tries).
  - Bodies push apart after everyone moves (`separateCharacters`, every pair, then out of walls).
  - **Combat (session 8):** `updateAi(state, world, items, nav)` (it needs the items for gun stats). Fighting is more activities in the same state machine (`engage`, `takeCover`, `cover`, `peek`, `hold`, `loiter`), with the extra state in `AiState` as plain numbers, so a save mid-fight restores exactly (tested). The enemy's skill is copied from its type into `AiState.skill` at spawn, so the state doesn't depend on content files. Its aim error wanders as a smooth function of the tick and a phase per enemy, so it needs no RNG per tick.
  - **Cover search** samples 12 random nav nodes within 10 m and keeps the nearest one that's hidden from the threat's eye at crouched-eye height, at its centre and 0.4 m either side (up to 4 raycasts per candidate, and a candidate further than the best so far is skipped before any raycast). **At most 1 cover search per tick across all enemies**, like path searches. Before each shot an enemy casts one ray through the characters to check no ally is in the way, and one through the world to choose chest or head.
  - Reachability is a components lookup (`nav.nearest` for both, then compare), every perception tick while alert. The way up to an unreachable target is the end of a partial path towards it.
  - **Factions (AI rework session 1, 2026-11-07; `sim/factions.ts`):** `AiState.faction` (missing in older saves: scav). `hostile(a, b)`: a player and any enemy (zombies too); never two players; never a zombie and another AI; scav and boss are allies; a PMC is hostile to every other enemy, other PMCs included unless they're in the same squad (AI-3). Sight (`look`) notices any hostile character, so enemies fight each other on sight and pick up targets that are other AI; `alarm`, cover, aim and the rest already worked on any `Character`. Damage and fire from a hostile alarm it as a player's do. A shot from an ally goes to `allyShot` (join the fight, as before, only towards a target hostile to the listener); a hostile enemy's shot is heard exactly like a player's (near miss and the 15% tier alarm it to the shooter, the rest is a noise to check). **Footsteps stay players' only**, so enemies don't track each other down. The 2-responder cap counts only the listener's allies. `clearOfAllies` holds fire only for an ally in the way; `byAlly` (was `byEnemyOnEnemy`, `sim/health.ts`) cancels only allies' hits.
  - **Hearing scale:** `SimState.hearingScale` (missing is 1), set from `CompiledMap.hearingScale` (`MapFile.hearingScale`, optional, so the map format stays 4) by `startRaid` (since MT-9; before, only the client set it, so sim tests heard at 1) and again by the client on resume. `hearEvents` multiplies every shot's, blast's and pane's `loudness` by it; footsteps' fixed ranges aren't scaled. Editor: a number field under the spawn clearance. Northpoint is 0.6.
  - **Cost (measured 2026-11-07, Mac, Node 22):** 20 armed AI on Northpoint (14 scavs at its enemy spawns, 6 PMCs at player spawns, nobody meeting): `step` 0.10 ms a tick on average, `updateAi` 0.04 ms (worst 0.5 ms). 10 scavs and 10 PMCs in open view of each other 30 m apart on flat ground, fighting to the end: `step` 0.075 ms on average, worst 1.8 ms, against a 33 ms tick. No extra staggering needed; `perceiveEvery` stays 3.
  - **Scav waves (AI rework session 2, 2026-11-07; `sim/waves.ts`):** `startEnemies(state, world, map, items, { types, loadouts, tables, patrols })` replaces the client's own spawning: it rolls the enemy spawns as `rollEnemySpawns` did, puts every non-scav in, makes a `WaveSlot` per scav sector (`spots`, `entity`, `due` tick) and starts a third. Everything later spawns need (types' skill, faction, outfit count and loot, loadouts, loot tables, patrols) is copied into `SimState.waves` (`WavesState`), as zombies mode copies its rules, so `step` needs no content and a saved raid keeps its queue. `updateWaves` runs in `step` after the AI: the trickle every tick (reads this tick's `shot` events into `waves.gunfire`), slots once a second. Its spot check is one ray per living player per candidate spot, and only when a slot is due. A spawn radius that lands somewhere seen or too close puts the scav back on its spot. `SimState.waves` is null without scav spawns and missing in older saves (no waves, as before). Tuning in `SCAV_WAVES`; a map's optional `scavStartShare` (`MapFile`, compiled into `CompiledMap`) overrides the start share.
  - **`Character.look`** (`{ type, outfit }`, set by `spawnEnemy` when given a `type`) replaces AI-1's `AiState.outfit`. Co-op guests aren't sent `ai` (or `waves`), so the look has to be on the character for them to draw the right outfit and draw enemies that arrive mid-raid. The client draws any character with a look it hasn't drawn yet, every tick (`drawNewEnemies` in `play/index.ts`), host and guest alike. `Welcome.spawned` is gone; `RaidSnapshot.enemies` is optional and read only for raids saved before looks.
  - **Erratic and narrower sight:** `EnemySkill.erratic` (0–1) and `fov` (degrees), both optional. `quirks` in `sim/ai.ts` runs at the end of `behave` and changes only that tick's wish and stance, with `AiState.quirk`, `quirkTime` and `quirkNext` (missing in older saves). A stop resets `stuck`, so it isn't taken for being stuck.
  - **PMC squads (AI rework session 3, 2026-11-07; `sim/squads.ts`, squad AI in `sim/ai.ts`):** a map's optional `pmcs` (`{ enemy?, count: [min, max] }`, `MapFile` → `CompiledMap`; the format stays 4) makes `startEnemies` call `startSquads`: `squadSizes` splits the count into squads of 2–4, `pickSpawn` gives each an unused player spawn (none within 30 m of or seen by a living player, by `playerDistance`, moved here from `waves.ts`; preferring 60 m+ and 50 m apart and out of sight of the other squads), and the members spawn straight from the content (not through `SimState.waves`: they don't replenish), leader on the spawn and the rest at their formation places when clear. RNG is drawn only when a map has `pmcs`, so other maps' seeds play as before. `pmcs.pmcSpawnsOnly` (MT-9, Silver Creek) leaves the player spawns out, so squads start only at `pmcSpawn` markers.
    - `AiState.squad` (the first leader's entity id) and `squadSlot` (0 leads), both missing for lone enemies; `hostile` makes two PMCs of the same squad allies, so allies' shots, `byAlly`, `clearOfAllies` and the responder cap all apply inside a squad for free. `joinSquad(c, squad, slot)` sets them. `squadLeader` is the living member with the lowest slot; a follower that finds its leader gone and itself next (`leadSquad`) takes slot 0 and guards where it stands.
    - **`follow`** activity: what `calmDown` gives a member with a slot over 0. `followSpot` (perception ticks only, and only while the leader moves or the place isn't set) puts it at `SQUAD.formation[rank]` (back, side, watch angle) from where the leader will be in 0.6 s, by the leader's heading (its home yaw while it guards, so it doesn't shuffle); else straight behind; else 2.5 m from the leader on its own side. `standable` checks height, overlap, a clear line from the leader and no drop half way (4 rays/queries at most). A turn of over 120° swaps a follower's sides (`followYaw`, `followSide`) so the squad doesn't cross over. Pace is `patrolPace + 0.1 × metres off`, up to `alertPace`. Once it gets as close as it can, that spot is its place until the leader moves (otherwise a place off the nav grid replanned every tick: it cost 1 ms a tick on average on Northpoint before the fix).
    - **Shared awareness:** `shareTarget` on a member's perception tick while it's alert and sees its target (or lost it under `AI.intuition` ago): mates not alert are `alarm`ed to it, mates fighting it out of sight get its position as their focus. **A follower's noise goes to the leader** (`tellLeader`, when its `look` ends wanting to investigate): the leader checks it unless fighting or already checking something, and the follower goes back to following.
    - **Cost (Mac, Node 22):** Northpoint, 10-minute raids from all 9 spawns with 3–6 PMCs and the scavs, nobody fighting: `step` 0.06–0.11 ms on average, worst 1–3 ms (one 15 ms outlier). A squad of 3–4 fighting you at 10–25 m: 0.07–0.3 ms on average, worst 6 ms warm.
  - **PMC routes, looting and extracting (AI rework session 4, 2026-11-07; `sim/routes.ts`, behaviour in `sim/ai.ts`):**
    - **`poi` marker** (map format stays 4: a new optional node): `kind` `loot` or `choke`, `weight` (default 1), `radius` (default 8 for loot, 2 for a choke). A `CompiledMarker` like the others; in the editor's marker tool for extraction maps, with kind, weight and radius in the inspector, drawn teal by the debug overlay.
    - **State:** `SimState.pois` (the map's, copied in by `startPois` from `startSquads`) and `SimState.squads` (`SquadState` per squad: current `stop`, `visited`, `extract` and a standing spot in it, `leaveTick`, `leaving`, `looted` container ids, `kills` to search). Missing or empty pois (older saves, maps without any) leave squads guarding as in AI-3: a squad only gets a `SquadState` when its map has pois, and the leader's `guard` switches to `route` only when it has one. All plain data, so a saved raid resumes its routes.
    - **Activities:** `route` (the leader: `walkRoute`), `loot` (any member: `lootStep`), `extract` (every member, standing in the zone). The leader's `wait` at a choke ends with `endStop`. Followers join the leader's searching at a loot stop (`joinLeader`, on perception ticks: the nearest unclaimed container in the stop's radius) and go into the extraction with it from within 20 m. `AiState.loot` (`LootTarget`: search key, position, seconds left searching), `reroute`, `hunt`, `legTries`, `detour`/`detours`, `unstick`/`unstickSide`: all optional.
    - **Stops:** `pickStop` weights each poi by `weight` × a walk factor (full from 15 to 70 m, less nearer, (70/d)² beyond), visited ones ×0.2, never the one it just left, and only those in its nav component. `nearestStop` after a fight or a check (`reroute`); `stopToward` for far gunfire.
    - **Looting:** `pickContainer` (in the stop's radius and 2 m of its height, not looted by the squad or seen searched and empty, not claimed by a mate, reachable), `pickBody` (its kills within 40 m). Searching takes 0.6 s an item (at least 1.5 s), then `searchContainer` (the searched look and event, as a player's) and `takeLoot`: the most valuable for its size first, into rig then backpack, by `addItem`; worn gear is never touched, its own or a body's.
    - **Leaving:** `updateSquads` (after `updateWaves` in `step`) counts `Character.extracting` for members in `extract` inside their zone (`inExtraction`), and after `RAID.extractTime` takes them out of `state.entities` in place with a `leave` event (not into `state.left`, which is players'). The client draws out any enemy no longer in the state (it did that only in zombies mode), and `EnemyBars.remove` drops its bar. `chooseExtraction` keeps only zones whose standing spot is in the leader's nav component *now* (`syncDoors` updates components for locked doors), always-open first.
    - **Paths:** `findPath` takes a `greed` (weighted A*); squads' calm walking uses 2.
