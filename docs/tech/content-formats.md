# Tech stack: Map, enemy and loot formats

Part of the [tech-stack.md index](../tech-stack.md). Record decisions here, in the topic they belong to.

## Map and prefab format
Decided in session 4. The schemas are in `packages/shared/src/content/schema.ts`, whose comments are the field-by-field reference.
- **Files:** `content/maps/<id>.json` and `content/prefabs/<id>.json`. The `id` inside must match the file name.
- **Loading:** `loadContent(format, json, source)` checks `formatVersion`, runs the migrations from the file's version up to the current one (`migrations[v]` turns v into v + 1), then validates with zod. A file newer than the build is rejected. Objects are **strict**: an unknown field is an error, so typos are caught and a migration must remove fields it renames.
- **Units and conventions:** meters; **angles in degrees** in files (radians everywhere after loading); y up, yaw 0 faces −z. **`pos` is the middle of a shape's base**, so y is where it stands. Sizes are full sizes, not half-extents. Colors are `#rrggbb`.
- **A map** has `environment` (sky color, fog near and far), `terrain` (origin, cell size, `heights` as rows along +z of corners along +x, one ground color), optional `water` (level, color, opacity: water over the whole map, compiled as one body covering the terrain; deeper than 1.2 m blocks movement since MT-1, see Collision), optional color slots, `nodes` and `zones`.
- **Nodes** (in maps and prefabs alike, told apart by `type`):
  - Shapes: `box`, `cylinder` (a `radiusTop` of 0 makes a cone), `ramp` (a wedge rising towards local +z), `stairs` (drawn as steps, collides as a ramp; optional `sides` (2026-11-03) adds a 0.1 m solid side wall each side whose top is one straight slope that far above the steps, a box plus a wedge each, drawn only) and `ladder`. All collide unless `collide: false`. **A box's `invisible: true` (2026-11-07, the user's request, to keep players in Northpoint's loading yard):** it isn't drawn, and it compiles to a blocker (`WorldDef.blockers`, after the barricades' so their order still matches) rather than a box, so movement and the nav grid respect it but shots, sight and throws pass through, and nobody sees a shot stop in mid-air over a low wall. It can't tilt, be a loot container or a switch, or have `collide: false` (compile errors). The editor draws it faint blue (`compileNodes(...).invisible`), pickable but not a surface to place things on, and the box panel has an "invisible" checkbox. Use it for map edges, 10 m high. **A box's `bulge` (2026-11-03, the user's request for bags of concrete, then per face):** `[−x, +x, bottom, top, −z, +z]` in meters, before turning; each face's middle is pushed out that far, making it a low pyramid of four triangles. Drawn only: it collides as the plain box, so keep bulges small. A bag of concrete bulges its four sides and leaves top and bottom flat. Optional, so the map format stays 3. **A box's `top` and `lean` (2026-11-03, for a jacket hanging from a hook):** `top` is the top face's `[width, depth]` (0 tapers to an edge or point) and `lean` moves its middle `[x, z]` from over the bottom's. Still 8 corners and 12 triangles, drawn only, collides as the plain box. `TriangleBuilder.poly` finds a face's normal from all its corners (Newell's method) so faces with corners that meet still wind the right way. The user wants everything kept low-poly: taper and bulge change outlines, never add curves.
  - **Boxes, cylinders, prefabs and groups tilt** (`pitch`, `roll`). A box or cylinder turns about its center; a prefab or group (2026-11-05) turns about its `pos` with everything in it, its `Frame.tilt` carrying the whole turn down through the compiler. A tilted solid collides as a slab (see Collision). A door inside is a panel fixed shut, as doors only swing about y; ladders, glass, barricades and buildings inside are a compile error. An instanced prefab is flattened when tilted. The editor refuses to ungroup a tilted group, as ramps, stairs and walls have no tilt to take on; unpacking a tilted prefab gives a group with the same tilt. Pitch and roll are typed in; the gizmo stays yaw only.
  - **`water` (MT-1, 2026-11-09, the user's call: "let me size up a square of water"; a new node type, so still format 4):** pos (the middle of its surface, so y is the water level), yaw, size (width along local x, depth along local z), color, optional opacity (0.7). Each body has its own level, so a mill pond can sit over the creek below its weir; overlapping, the highest surface counts. It can't tilt. Compiles to `CompiledMap.water` (`CompiledWater`, the map's own `water` first), which is also `world.water`. The map editor's **water** tool (beside floor) draws one from two opposite corners, its surface at the first corner's height; the panel sets size, opacity and color, and y is the level.
  - **`landmark: true` (MT-1, 2026-11-09; still map format 4, prefab format 3, being optional)** on a box, cylinder, group or placed prefab, or on a prefab file (every placement): drawn as usual, and its shapes again in `CompiledMap.landmarks` for the silhouette past the fog (tech/client.md). `Compiler.nodes` collects them through a draw-only sink and hands the subtree a sink without `landmarks`, so a landmark inside a landmark isn't doubled. A checkbox on those nodes and on the prefab in the map editor.
  - **`foliage: true` (MT-3, 2026-11-10; optional, so no format change)** on a box or cylinder: drawn as usual, never collides (`collide: true` with it is a compile error), and compiles to sight-only boxes in `WorldDef.foliage` (tech/shared-sim.md → Collision). A box hides as itself. A cylinder or cone hides as two stacked boxes, each 0.8 × its mean radius over that half, so a cone hides less near its tip. The pine's two leaf cones use it. No editor checkbox yet: set it in the JSON.
  - `prefab`: a reference by id, with a position, a yaw (no tilt) and color overrides. Prefabs can contain prefabs; a prefab that contains itself is an error.
  - `door`: width, height, hinge side, optional key item, starts open or closed.
  - `wall` (2026-11-02): a straight wall with its openings as data: pos (middle of its base), yaw, size (length along local x, height, thickness), color, collide, and `openings` ({ along, width, bottom, top, glass? }: along is the middle's distance from the wall's middle; glass is a tint). Compiles (`content/walls.ts` → `wallPieces`) to boxes, the stretches between openings and each one's sill and lintel, and a glass pane for each glazed one (id `<path>/glass<k>`); an opening past the end or overlapping another is a content error. Doors aren't openings (they swing, and are their own nodes). A new node type, so no format change.
  - `glass` (2026-11-02): pos (bottom middle), yaw, size (width, height), thickness (default 0.02), color. A new node type, so no format change.
  - `wire` (TU-2, 2026-11-09): barbed wire, pos (middle of its base), yaw, size; can't tilt. A new node type, so still format 4. It's neither drawn nor solid: `compileMap` puts it in `WorldDef.wires`, and `CollisionWorld.touchesWire(x, z, r, low, high)` tests an upright capsule against them (looped, not gridded: few maps have any). `snagOnWire` (`sim/wire.ts`), after moving each tick, starts a leg bleeding (which leg is a hash of the tick and the id, not the rng) and pushes a `wire` event when a character goes in, tracked by `Character.inWire` (optional). Not for one health bar or infinite health. The `barbed-wire` prefab draws a 1.2 m coil with one inside it.
  - Markers: `playerSpawn`, `enemySpawn` (enemy type, loadout, loot weight multipliers, optional `patrol`), `lootContainer` (container type, loot table), `lootSpawn` (loot table, optional `chance`) `questMarker` (id required) and `poi` (PMC routes: `kind`, `weight`, `radius`; AI-4); until map format 4, `extraction` (name, radius), now a zone's `extraction: { y }`. Enemy types, loadouts, loot tables and keys are plain ids for now; they're checked against their own files once those exist.
  - `patrol` (session 7): a required `id`; `points` (at least 2, in the enclosing frame), each either a plain `[x, y, z]` to walk through or a stop `{ pos, wait, radius }` (wait defaults to 3 s, radius to 0); `repeatFrom` (default 0: the points before it are the way in, walked once; at least two must repeat); and `loop` (default true; false walks back and forth over the repeating points). An `enemySpawn`'s `patrol` names a patrol **beside it** (in the same map or prefab), so a prefab can carry its own guard and route; it compiles to the patrol's path id, and a missing one is an error. `enemySpawn.radius` spawns at a random clear spot within it. `CompiledMap.patrols` has them in world space, every point as `{ x, y, z, wait, radius }` (walk-through points have 0 and 0).
- **Format 2** (session 7) added patrols, for maps and prefabs alike; 1 → 2 changes nothing.
- **Named zones** are polygons in x, z, at map level only.
- **Color slots (recoloring):** a prefab declares slots with default colors (`"colors": { "paint": "#c0392b" }`), and its shapes use either a hex color or a slot name. A placement overrides slots, and an override can name a slot of the enclosing prefab or map. Overriding a slot the prefab doesn't have is an error.
- **Ids:** any node can have an `id`, unique among its siblings. Markers and doors come out of compilation with **path ids**, the ids from the map down joined by `/` (for example `house-a/desk`); a node without an id is `#` and its index. Anything other content refers to (quest markers, keyed doors) should have an explicit id.
- **Compilation:** `compileMap(map, prefabs)` (in shared, so the server can use it for co-op) flattens everything into a `CompiledMap`: the `WorldDef` for collision, world-space shapes to draw, instanced groups, doors, markers and zones.
- **Buildings (session 16):** a prefab's optional `building` (`size` as width and depth, optional `center`, optional base `color`, a hex color or slot) makes it a building whose floor is its y = 0. `compileMap` gives each placed building a **concrete base** (`concreteBase`): a box under the footprint from the floor down to 0.3 m below the lowest ground under it (`groundUnder` samples every half meter or less), drawn and collided with like any box; none if the ground is nowhere below the floor. `CompiledMap.buildings` has the footprints in world space. The format stayed 3, the field being optional.
- Deferred: per-cell terrain colors (roads, dirt) and scaled prefab placements. Both can be added later with a migration.


- **Switches (2026-11-05, map format still 3: new optional fields and one new node):** `switch: { name?, useTime?, rows: [{ opens, timer?, noise?, lasts?, openNoise? }] }` on a box, cylinder, group or placed prefab, compiled like `loot`: its solids are what you aim at, in `world.switches` (`SwitchDef`, reach 2 m from their edge). `opens` is a door's id beside the switch, else its path id in the map; a row naming no door fails the compile, and a door named by a row is `switched`, so locked. `lasts` defaults to `timer`. The editor's `doorTargets` lists doors in placed prefabs too and gives whatever holds the door an id on linking (`linkSwitch`), as a `#index` path breaks when the list changes.
  - **Sim (`sim/switches.ts`):** `state.switches` (used, by index) and `state.noises`; the timer lives on the door (`DoorState.opening`, `openingFor`, `openSound`), so the gate, lamp and mover all read one number (`openingProgress`). `PlayerInput.switch` is sent while the use button is held, merged for co-op guests; `holdSwitch` counts `useTime` on `Character.switching`, which starts over on letting go, looking away or leaving reach, and the HUD's `#use-ring` reads it. Events `switch` and `doorSwitched`. Noises are client sounds (`SoundCues.noises`, repeated end to end, centered, 0.5–1 gain by distance over 80 m), with no part in the AI's hearing.
  - **Door fields:** `rollUp` (a look that's drawn rolling up: scaled from its top) and `lamp` (`pos`, `size` in the door's frame, one draw call). A door with a look `vanishes` when a switch opens it, and nav leaves out its swing, as for a door with a cost.
  - **`mover` node:** `prefab`, `travel` (its own frame), `door` (beside it). `CompiledMover`, one mesh each, moved by `updateDoors` and hidden on arrival. Drawn only, so what it arrives as must already be solid in the map; `compileNodes` draws it where it starts.

## Enemy types and loadouts
Session 8. The schemas are in `packages/shared/src/content/enemies.ts`, whose comments are the field-by-field reference. Loaded like items (`formatVersion` 2 for enemies since AI-1, 1 for loadouts; strict zod objects, the id must match the file name).
- **`content/enemies/<id>.json`:** `name`, `loadout` (the default loadout id) and `skill` (`reaction` s, `aimError` and `aimSettled` degrees, `aimSettle` s, `holdChance` 0–1).
- **`content/loadouts/<id>.json`:** `weapons` (at least one: `weight`, `item`, `spare` as `[min, max]` rounds, optional `attachments` as `{ item, chance }` where the first to roll in a slot wins), and optional `helmets` and `bodyArmor` (`weight`, `item` or `null` for none, optional `condition` as a `[min, max]` fraction of full durability), and since AI-6 optional `grenades` (`weight`, `item` or `null`, optional `count` as `[min, max]`). Each part is picked by weight on its own.
- `checkEnemies` checks the references (loadouts exist, items are the right type and slot, attachments fit their gun); the client checks each map's enemy spawns name a type and loadout that exist. Both run in the client's content test.
- `equipLoadout(c, items, loadout, rng)` (`sim/loadout.ts`) rolls one onto a character, using the sim's RNG so a raid's rolls come from its seed.
- **Enemy loot (session 11):** an enemy type's optional `loot` is `rolls` (`[min, max]` items) and `tables` (loot table id → weight). Each item picks a table by its weight times the spawn's `lootWeights` for it (1 if not given; a weight of 0 turns a table off), then one item from it. The format stays 1, since the field is optional.
- The client's content test also checks that every locked door's `key` is a key item.
- **The tutorial's flags (TU-2, 2026-11-09; map format still 4, loadout still 1):** an `enemySpawn` may have `deaf`, `passive`, `harmless`, `once` and `noLoot` (each `true` or left out). They become `AiState.deaf`/`passive`/`harmless` (through `EnemySpawnOptions` and `WaveSpot`). **Deaf** skips `listenForSteps`, `listenForHands` and every event in `hearEvents` but damage, ignite and death. **Passive** skips `look` too, until a damage or ignite event from a hostile deletes it. Both keep it out of the trickle (`freeToCheck`). **Harmless** sends its hits down `woundZone`'s infinite-health branch: a `damage` event of 0 and the aim punch, no HP, armor wear or bleed (burning isn't covered; no harmless enemy carries fire). **`once`** spawns it at raid start like a boss, so a scav there isn't a wave slot and never respawns; **`noLoot`** skips `rollEnemyLoot`. A loadout's optional **`carried`** (`{ item, count? }`) is stowed right after the rig and backpack, before grenades, so it lands in the pockets: the tutorial scav's key.
- **Format 2 (AI rework session 1, 2026-11-07):** `faction` (`scav`, `pmc` or `boss`, required) and `outfits` (a list, at least one; a plain default if left out) in place of `outfit`. The 1 → 2 migration makes a file a scav and puts its outfit in a list; `boss-scav.json` was rewritten as a boss. `spawnEnemy` takes `faction` and `outfits` (the count) and rolls the outfit's index with the sim's RNG, only when there's more than one, so one-outfit types draw nothing and older seeds play the same. The client draws `type.outfits[c.look.outfit]` (`outfitOf` in `play/index.ts`), so co-op guests and a resumed raid see the same look (in `Character.look` since AI-2; it was `AiState.outfit`, which guests never got). Zombies keep their single `outfit`.

## Loot tables and raid configs
Session 11. Loaded like the other content (`formatVersion` 1, strict zod objects, the id must match the file name).
- **`content/loot-tables/<id>.json`** (`content/loot-tables.ts`): `name`, `rolls` (`[min, max]` items for a container) and `items`, each `{ item, weight, count? }` where `count` is a `[min, max]` only for items that stack. `checkLootTables` checks the items exist and counts stay within a stack; `checkEnemyLoot` checks enemy types name tables that exist. The client checks every `lootContainer` and `lootSpawn` marker and every spawn's `lootWeights` names a table.
- **Rolling** (`sim/loot-roll.ts`): `fillLootContainers` fills the containers `placeLootContainers` placed, and `rollEnemyLoot` stows an enemy's loot in its pockets, rig and backpack after its loadout. Both use the sim's RNG, so a seed gives the same loot. A rolled item is a new one (`createItem`): a gun comes with a full magazine and no attachments. What doesn't fit is left out. `byWeight` moved to `rng.ts` for loadouts and loot alike.
- **Loose spawns (session 11b):** `rollLootSpawns` puts one item from each `lootSpawn` marker's table on the ground (`SimState.ground`), if the marker's optional `chance` (0–1, default 1) rolls. The map format stays 2, since the field is optional.
- **Spot mixes and loot groups (2026-11-10, still map format 4, prefab format 3: optional fields):** a `lootSpawn` has either `lootTable` or `mix` (a `lootMixSchema` without `each`; `spotMix` in `sim/loot-roll.ts` reads either as a mix). A placed prefab's `lootGroup` is copied at compile time onto every `lootSpawn` inside it, as `oneOf`, with `unit` set to the copy's path (`CompiledMarker.unit`). The outermost grouped copy wins. The map's `lootGroups: { name: { pick } }` goes through to `CompiledMap.lootGroups`. `oneOfPicks` (`sim/spawn-roll.ts`) groups by `oneOf` and picks units (`unitOf`: `unit ?? id`) with a partial shuffle. That's one draw for a pick of 1, so seeds roll as they did before groups. Enemy spawns use the same function without counts.
- **`lootSpawn.oneOf`** (2026-10-30): an optional id naming a set of spots; `rollLootSpawns` first picks one spot per set with the raid's RNG (in marker order, so a seed gives the same pick), then rolls only that one's chance. Names are map-wide, not scoped by prefab. The format stays 3, the field being optional. The map editor shows it as "one of".
- **Loot mixes and container kinds' loot (2026-11-02, the user's request):** a `LootMix` (`content/schema.ts`) is `tables` (table id → weight), an optional `empty` weight, and `each`. Without `each`, a container picks one table (or nothing) by weight and rolls that table's `rolls` (a house fridge: household 80 / parts 20); with `each`, it takes the rolls of a table picked by weight and picks a table (or nothing) for every item, so one container holds a mix. A container's loot is its own `mix`, else its one `lootTable` (as before; a one-table mix rolls exactly as the old table did, same RNG draws), else its kind's default `loot` (`content/loot-containers/<id>.json`, optional, so the format stays 1). `lootTable` became optional on `lootContainer` markers and on a node's `loot`; the map format stays 3. `containerMix` resolves it, `rollMix` rolls it, and `fillLootContainers` now takes the kinds. The client checks every container resolves to loot whose tables exist (`checkLootContainers`), and every kind's tables exist (`checkLootKinds`).
- Quest items (session 14b) are put on the ground by `startQuests` (see Quests), not by loot tables.
- Named zones don't weight loot (dropped 2026-11-11, design/raids-and-maps.md); loot is set per spot and container.
- **`content/raids/<id>.json`** (`content/raids.ts`): `name`, `map`, and `duration` in seconds (15 minutes if left out). Every player spawn and extraction point in the map is used. It will grow a playable area, loadout rules, objectives and open extractions for weekly challenges.
- **`offline: true` on a raid (TU-2, 2026-11-09, still format 1):** played on the device only, never on the server: the client's `raidIds` (the raid list) and the item sources leave it out, and `POST /raids` refuses it as if it didn't exist. Only the tutorial's raid has it.
- **`order` and `tags` on a raid (MT-2, 2026-11-09, still format 1):** the map list is sorted by `order`, lowest first (left out, last; then by id), and `tags` (each up to 12 characters) show on the entry: Silver Creek is `order` 1 with `starter`, Northpoint 2. The menu still picks the last raid played.
