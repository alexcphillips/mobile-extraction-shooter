# Tech stack: Client

Part of the [tech-stack.md index](../tech-stack.md). Record decisions here, in the topic they belong to.

## Client (`packages/client`)
- **Build tool:** Vite + TypeScript.
- **3D rendering:** plain three.js, not React Three Fiber, so the game loop and rendering stay outside React.
  - Static map geometry is **merged per chunk** (32 m): one mesh per chunk holding its terrain and every shape whose centre is in it.
  - **Hidden faces and flat ground (2026-11-07, for the phone):** `buildChunks` leaves out faces nobody can see (`render/face-cull.ts`): a face that, moved 1 cm out along its normal, is wholly inside one chunk box (not one with a smaller top), or wholly under the ground (checked exactly where the ground can bend, never over a hole or past its edge). Only the chunks' own shapes cover, since they never change. Flat ground cells drawn whole are merged into rectangles, each a fan from its middle to every cell corner round its edge, so nothing meeting it has a T-junction. Northpoint went from 486k to 305k triangles, and in view at the 95th percentile from 331k to 243k.
  - **Doors and searched looks batched (2026-11-07):** every door at rest (not swinging, rolling up or gone) is in one merged mesh, `MapView.doorBatch`, rebuilt only when a door starts or stops moving; a moving door draws its own mesh. The looks of containers with a searched look are one merged mesh per 32 m chunk, rebuilt when one of them is searched. Northpoint's draw calls in view went from 143 to 69 at the 95th percentile.
  - **Instancing is opt-in per prefab** (`"instanced": true`, session 4), for small props placed many times, like trees and crates. Each instanced prefab and colour set becomes one `InstancedMesh` for the whole map. Merging is the default because it costs no extra draw calls; instancing saves memory and load time but adds one draw call per group, and a group is culled as a whole.
  - Colour is stored per vertex, with each face direction shaded differently; materials are unlit (`MeshBasicMaterial`); distance fog. Static geometry has the shading baked into its colours. Instanced and moving geometry (doors now, characters later) uses `shadedMaterial()`, which applies the same shading on the GPU from the world-space normal, so it stays right as the object turns.
  - Each door is its own mesh, pivoted at its hinge, so it can swing open.
  - Content is bundled into the client with `import.meta.glob`; in dev, saving a content file reloads the page.
  - The camera's far plane follows the fog distance.
  - **Water (MT-1, 2026-11-09):** `buildWater` (`render/map.ts`) draws every body as **one mesh**: two triangles each at its level, colour and opacity per body through 4-component vertex colours, `depthWrite: false`, both sides (so it shows from under the surface). One draw call however many bodies there are. The editor uses it too, for the map's own water and each `water` node (picked to select it, not stood on). The map tab marks a cell wet where its mean height is under `waterLevelAt` its middle, coloured as the first body.
  - **Landmarks past the fog (MT-1, 2026-11-09):** `render/landmarks.ts`. `CompiledMap.landmarks` (every landmark's shapes again, instanced prefabs flattened) becomes **one merged mesh** in a flat colour (the sky × 0.72, `SILHOUETTE_SHADE`; no vertex colours or normals, `fog: false`), leaving out shapes under 0.5 m (`SILHOUETTE_MIN`) so it carries no detail. `LandmarkPass` draws it first, with its own camera copying the eye's matrices but reaching across the whole map (its diagonal + 100 m); then depth is cleared and the world drawn over it. Where the world is drawn (up to the fog's far plane) it wins, so the silhouette shows only against the sky beyond, rising out of the fogged ground. **The sky is now the renderer's clear colour, not `scene.background`**, because a colour background makes three.js clear the screen, which wiped the silhouettes. One draw call with landmarks, none (and no extra depth clear) without; `landmarks.test.ts` holds it to one mesh however many landmarks there are. Checked in headless Chrome: a 32 m tower 150 m away with fog ending at 70 m shows as a hazy shape over the horizon.
  - **The first-person gun is drawn in a second pass** after the world, with the depth buffer cleared, so it never pokes into walls (`render/viewmodel.ts`). One extra draw call. It's built from the item models (session 18b), with the arms (session 8b) in the same mesh: `gunModel` (`render/gun-model.ts`) puts each fitted attachment's parts on its mount, leaves out the gun's parts hidden by it, and takes the sight from the optic (mount + its sight point) and the muzzle from the muzzle device. Aimed, the gun sits so its sight is straight ahead of the eye. The bodies use the same `gunModel` and `knifeModel`.
  - The viewmodel rebuilds its one mesh only when the gun or its attachments change. **Optic reticles and the scope mask are one SVG overlay** (`hud/optic.ts`, rebuilt only when the optic changes), not 3D, so they cost no draw calls; while scoped the gun isn't drawn at all.
  - Tracers and impact puffs come from fixed pools (`render/effects.ts`): one line mesh and one instanced mesh, so shooting allocates nothing and costs two draw calls.
  - Recoil is simulation state, so the camera interpolates it between ticks like the eye position. **The sights don't (2026-11-04):** the gun, reticle and scope mask point with the last tick's recoil, since the next round fires with it; eased, they trailed it by up to 0.4° in an AKM burst, so rounds landed under the reticle. Only the view's share is eased.
- **UI:** React DOM overlays for menus, inventory, settings and the editors.
  - **Graphics setting (2026-11-07, for the phone):** `Settings.graphics` is `high` (up to 2 pixels per CSS pixel, antialiased; the default, as before), `balanced` (1.5, antialiased) or `low` (1, no antialiasing), in `GRAPHICS_QUALITY`, under Display in settings. The pixel ratio follows a change at once; antialiasing is fixed with the WebGL context, so it changes from the next raid. `PlayOptions.pixelRatio` is gone.
  - **React is loaded lazily** (session 5): menus are dynamic imports, so React is a separate chunk (about 70 kB gzipped) fetched the first time a menu opens, not part of startup. No `@vitejs/plugin-react`: Vite compiles JSX itself (`"jsx": "react-jsx"`), so there's no fast refresh and a change to a component reloads the page. ESLint runs the React hooks rules on `.tsx`.
  - **The raid screen** (session 10) is lazily loaded like settings. React draws it from the live sim state and never changes it: it sends inventory actions instead. `InventoryController` (plain TS) holds the selection, taps and the drag; the drag (`ui/drag.ts`) is plain DOM, so a drag never re-renders React. Drop targets are described by a `data-drop` JSON attribute. The selected gun's attachment slots are drop targets too (`{ gun, slot }`), and a fitted attachment drags as a `PartRef` (shared with the sim) rather than an `ItemRef`, so it can drop on a grid or on another gun. The controller remembers the selected item object as well as its ref, so the selection follows a gun whose index changed (an attachment taken out of the same grid). Guns carry a `data-gun` attribute with their ref: while a loose attachment that fits is dragged over one, `gunZone` makes the gun itself the drop target (a `part` zone for the attachment's slot), and otherwise the drag falls through to the grid or gear slot as before. A drag swallows the mouse click it ends with, so dropping on an empty attachment slot doesn't also tap it. The game redraws the screen after a tick with an inventory event, and every 10 ticks for reach and changes made by others.
  - **Items on the ground (session 11b):** `render/ground-items.ts` draws them as one merged mesh of their item models (session 18b; one draw call), rebuilt only when the list or an entry's item changes (checked each tick by identity). Each item's model is laid down once and cached: guns, knives and magazines on their side, body armour, rigs and backpacks on their back, centred, resting on their lowest point. The lit item's vertex colours are scaled in place. `play/loot.ts` picks what the use button acts on: an item in reach whose direction from the eye is within 20° of the look wins over containers and bodies. The item it would take is drawn lighter, and `RaidHud` shows its name, or "No room for …" when the pickup's `inventory` event comes back refused, in `#prompt` under the crosshair. The raid screen's right column shows the ground panel when nothing is open: a `data-drop` zone for the ground (`DropZone` kind `ground`) with the items in reach as draggable tiles; the bottom bar has **Drop** (`quick` to the ground).
  - **The map tab (session 11b, `ui/map-tab.tsx`):** an SVG built from the `CompiledMap` (now in `RaidContext`): every shape's and instanced copy's footprint (box and wedge corners turned by yaw, cylinders as circles; under 0.08 m² left out), sorted by top height, water cells merged along rows, zones, extraction points and the player's arrow. The static layer is built once per map (a `WeakMap` cache) and memoised as one React element, so a redraw only reconciles the labels and arrow. Sizes are scaled by metres per screen pixel, measured with a `ResizeObserver`. **Floors (2026-11-09):** `MapFile.floors` (optional, so still map format 4) is passed through as `CompiledMap.floors`. Each footprint keeps its bottom and top (a tilted shape's reach included); a floor's layers are the footprints starting under its cut (`floor.y + CUT_ABOVE`, from `editor/cut.ts`) sorted by their top capped at the cut, split into those topping out 0.5 m under the floor (drawn under a dark veil) and the rest, built once per map and floor. `floorAt` picks a height's floor (the highest within 0.5 m over it). **On raised ground (MT-2):** a footprint's bottom and top are counted from the terrain under its middle where that's above y 0, so a cabin on Silver Creek's ridge shows on the Ground floor rather than being cut off above it. The view is framed on footprints, markers and zones (`frameOf`). `mapLabel` is a marker type (`CompiledMarker`), so labels in prefabs and groups come out in world space; `placeLabels` drops overlapping ones, biggest first, and labels are clamped inside the view. Within a size, places go in the map file's order, so list the important ones first (Silver Creek's generator puts landmarks and quest places first, MT-11). A label's height picks its floor, so one over water belongs at the water's or the ground's height, not the bed's. Quest spots come from `sim.raid.investigate` (yours, not done). In the editor, `render/debug.ts` draws a label as a flat `CanvasTexture` plane facing up (a mesh, not a sprite: the editor's picking raycasts without a camera), and the editor's `dispose` frees material maps. The HUD's `QuestMarkers` was removed; `screenSpot`, which the tutorial card uses, moved to `hud/screen-spot.ts`.
  - **The raid (session 11):** the play page starts a raid from a config (`?raid`; `test-raid` until it was deleted, 2026-11-09). `hud/raid-hud.ts` shows the timer (top left, red under 2 minutes) and, in an extraction point, its name and countdown. `render/extractions.ts` draws every extraction point as a faint green column of its radius, one draw call for all of them (kept after the map tab, the user's call). When the raid ends for you (a `leave` event, or your character dead, checked from the state since a debug hit kills outside the tick) the raid screen closes, and the **results screen** (`ui/results.tsx`, lazily loaded, 1.5 kB) comes up at once, or 1.5 s after a death: how it ended, where or by what, time in raid, kills, and what you kept and lost. The game then pauses (a new `ended` pause reason). Its button (**Continue** since session 13) reloads the page, which opens on the main menu. The old death overlay is gone.
  - **Saving raids (session 12b):** `prepareRaid` in `main.ts` runs before the play page: sign in if needed (`fetchProfile`, else the sign-in screen), sync settings, send a result left in local storage (`sendPending`), then `POST /api/raids` (`startServerRaid`); the player wears the reply's `profile.gear` (`PlayOptions.gear`). If the profile had a raid open with nothing pending, a notice says it counted as missing in action. Any request failing to reach the server shows **No connection** with Try again, which runs it all again. At the end, `saveResult` writes `{ raid, end }` to local storage (`mes.pendingRaid`) **before** sending it, and clears it once the server has it or refuses it (`raid-over`, `not-found`, `invalid`; `stale` is retried once); offline or signed out it stays for next time. The results screen used to show Saving…, Saved, Not saved yet, or why it was refused; that line (`SaveLine`) was taken out on 2026-11-09 at the user's request, and a failed send is only logged to the console. **No service worker** (decided 2026-10-27): raids can't start offline, so one would only replace Safari's offline page with ours, and cached clients outliving a deploy would talk to an API they no longer match. Add one if opening offline matters later.
  - **Main menu and stash (session 13):** `main.ts` runs `prepareMenu` (sign in, sync settings, send a pending result, and an unfinished raid: tell the player, then `POST /api/raids/:id/abandon` counts it as missing in action), then the main menu (`ui/main-menu.tsx`, lazily loaded, in `#menu`), then the raid it returns. With no connection when starting, it says so and goes back to the menu. The results button reloads the page, which lands on the menu again: simpler and leak-free compared with tearing down the renderer. Settings open over the menu (`#ui` is above `#menu`), with **Done** instead of Resume; the layout editor hides the menu while it runs (`body:has(#layout-bar)`).
  - **The stash reuses the raid inventory whole.** Shared `stash.ts`: `openStash(profile)` wraps the profile in a tiny `SimState`, one player wearing `profile.gear` beside a loot container (`STASH_ID`) whose grid is `profile.stash`, the same objects, so actions edit the profile in place. `applyStash` runs `applyInventory`, refuses drops to the ground, moves anything dropped into the stash, and puts the profile back (from a JSON copy taken first) if the stash had no room or the knife left its slot; if the slot in hand was emptied, the knife is in hand. **Pages:** each stash page is its own loot container (`stashOwner(i)`, id `stash-<i>`; `stashPageOf`), so dragging works per page as on any container; the controller's target is the page shown, and a `quick` into a page that can't take all of it is tried on the others in turn (`applyStash`'s `page` is also where dropped things go first). A page's tab is a drop zone of a new kind, `into` (`data-drop` `{ into: owner }`): `canDrop` wants another owner with a free spot or a stack to top up, and `drop` sends a `quick` to it. Buying: `StashSaver.buyPage` saves first, then `POST /api/stash/pages`, and the stash reopens on the new page. The raid screen's `Inventory` is exported and takes an `InventoryContext` (the raid's `RaidContext` adds the map); `stash: true` hides Drop. `StashSaver` (client `stash.ts`) sends the whole profile 800 ms after the last change, one request at a time, keeps editing the same objects (only the version moves on), and on `stale`, `invalid` or `in-raid` reads the profile afresh and reopens the stash with a note. Starting a raid calls `flush` first and won't start if it failed.
- **Resuming a raid (session 13):** on `visibilitychange` to hidden and on `pagehide`, `play/` writes a `RaidSnapshot` to local storage (`mes.raidSnapshot`: the server raid id, the raid config, the spawn, the player's entity id, each enemy spawn marker's entity id, kills, and the whole `SimState`, about 12 kB on the test map). `prepareMenu` resumes it if it names the raid still open on the server (`readSnapshot`; anything else is thrown away): `startPlay` with `options.resume` uses the saved state instead of spawning and rolling, matches enemies to their markers for bodies and names, and takes the look from the player. It's cleared when the raid ends for you. A snapshot that fails to load is abandoned (missing in action) and the page reloads to the menu.
- **Settings sync (session 12b):** the store keeps when they were last changed (`mes.settings.at`). At startup the newer of this device's and the account's wins (`syncSettings`); later changes are sent 2 s after the last one (`PUT /api/settings`), which the server only stores if newer than what it has (`accounts.settings`, `settings_at`), so an old device coming online can't undo a newer change. A failed send is fine: the next startup sends them, being newer.
  - **Signing in (session 12b, done early):** before the play page starts a raid, `account.ts` asks `GET /api/profile`. Signed out, `ui/account.tsx` (lazily loaded, `#account`, over everything) shows a plain `<form>` with `autocomplete` names (`username`, `current-password`, `new-password`), so Safari's Keychain fills and saves it. Creating an account asks for the password twice, since there's no reset. Inputs are 16 px (smaller zooms iOS on focus) and turn `user-select` back on (the page's `none` stops typing in iOS Safari). It also shows in portrait (it hides `#portrait`), where typing is easier. Settings has an Account section with Sign out, which reloads. If the server can't be reached the game starts anyway, until 12b's offline handling.
  - **Hand slots and the heal button** (`hud/hands.ts`, session 10b) are plain DOM: slot icons by item kind, the heal button's choice (worked out about 10 times a second, since `treatments` allocates), an SVG progress ring, and the long-press picker. The first-person knife and the draw, lowering and slash motions are in `render/viewmodel.ts`; the body's slash is IK on the right arm in `render/character-body.ts`, with the placeholder knife in `render/gun-model.ts`.
  - The HUD doesn't re-render through React every frame. Values that change every frame are written straight to the DOM, or throttled to about 10 Hz. **The in-game HUD buttons are plain DOM** (`hud/`), as is the layout editor, which moves the real buttons over the paused game.
- **Input:** Pointer Events for the virtual sticks and buttons (`input/controls.ts`). Moves and lifts are listened for on `window`, so a touch that started on a button keeps working when it slides off. **The layout is stored in settings** as each moved control's centre (a fraction of the screen) and a size multiplier; unmoved controls follow the defaults in `hud/layout.ts`.
  - **Desktop (2026-10-30):** `input/mode.ts` keeps `inputMode` (`touch` or `desktop`) from `(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)`, then from each pointerdown's `pointerType`, and writes it to `body[data-input]`; CSS hides the touch-only controls on desktop. `input/cursor-lock.ts` (`CursorLock`, owned by `Controls` as `controls.cursor`) does pointer lock on the canvas: the game says `want(true/false)` (the play screen each tick: not while the raid screen or heal picker is open or the raid has ended, and from `closeScreen` directly, since browsers only lock on a user gesture). **No overlay and no pause (2026-10-30, the user's call):** it requests the lock when the raid starts (the Start click may still count as a gesture), on `want(true)`, when the game unpauses (settings' Resume click), and on a mousedown on the canvas, which then also fires or aims (`cursor.resuming`). Browsers never lock from an Esc press (it isn't a user gesture, and synthetic events don't count), so until a click the raid simply carries on with the cursor free. **Esc opens settings** by clicking `hud.settingsButton`: when the lock is lost while wanted and unpaused (the browser's own Esc, or switching away), or on an Esc keydown while wanted, unlocked and unpaused. While a screen is open the lock isn't wanted, so Esc does nothing there; the raid screen and heal picker don't close on Esc either (the user's call), since the lock couldn't come back from it. Mouse pointers never drive the touch stick; locked `mousemove` turns the view by `movementX/Y` (deltas over 400 px dropped as browser glitches), and `mousedown`/`mouseup` (not pointer events, which merge chorded buttons) fire and aim. Settings gained `mouseSensitivity` (falls back on its own; no format change). `RaidHud.setPrompt` takes the key to show as a `<kbd>`. **Keys since 2026-11-01:** Q/E lean, F use, Z aim, no fire key. Lean buttons `leanLeft`/`leanRight` in `hud/layout.ts`, hidden on desktop like the other touch-only ones. The `lean` setting (`hold` or `toggle`, default toggle) covers keys and buttons: `Controls.leanOn` is the toggled side (or the button held, in hold mode), and `Controls.lean` is Q/E while down or else `leanOn` in hold mode, `leanOn` in toggle mode.
  - **Controller (CP-1, 2026-11-08):** `inputMode` has a third mode, `gamepad`: taken by any pad button or a stick pushed past 0.35 (so drift never takes it), given back to desktop by a trusted keydown or a mouse move of 3 px or more (trusted only, so CP-2's synthetic cursor events won't). CSS treats `gamepad` like `desktop` for hiding touch controls, hides the mouse pointer, and labels the heal, detonate and put-out read-outs L1, D-pad ↑ and ↓. No pointer lock with a pad; going back to desktop asks for it from the keydown, which is a gesture. `input/gamepad.ts` is pure and tested: `readPad` copies the first standard-mapping pad (`firstPad`; a pad without one gets a console warning) into a preallocated `PadState`, and `mapPad(state, prev, dt, out)` gives button masks (held, pressed, released; press over 0.5, release under 0.3, so triggers don't flicker), the move stick (radial dead zone 0.15, linear, so a partial push walks), and the look stick (dead zone 0.12, squared, with a rim boost to 1.8× over 0.3 s). Two `PadFrame`s are swapped each frame, so nothing is allocated. `input/pad-driver.ts` (`PadDriver`, `controls.pad`) runs from `Controls.frame(dtMs)`, called by the raid's frame loop before the ticks: each button **presses the touch control** through `Controls.press(id)` (the old `onButton` body, now returning its release), so crouch, lean, aim toggles and the heal/grenade long press behave exactly as on touch. The pad's own actions: △ tap sets `controls.swapPrimary` (the play screen resolves it with `otherPrimary(gear)`), △ held 400 ms draws the sidearm, R3 the knife, L3 latches sprint until you push forwards less than 0.3 or press L2/R2, and □ presses `interact` when the game's `controls.canUse` says a prompt shows (the use label isn't `use` or `locked`), else `reload`. Look turns at `PAD_TURN_SPEED` (3 rad/s) × `padSensitivity` (a new setting, falling back on its own) × the rim boost, through the same `turn` as touch and mouse (aim sensitivity, invert Y, assist and zoom apply). Aim assist with a pad uses `PAD_ASSIST` (0.7/0.5/0.35), stronger than touch's. **Rotational assist** (`input/aim-follow.ts`, pure and tested): each tick `followTarget` in the play screen takes the bearing from your eye to the character `onTarget` hit (`aimHit.id`, at 0.7 of its height), and if it's the same target as last tick, `followRate` gives its angular speed times the share (`PAD_FOLLOW` 0.3/0.45/0.6 by the aim assist setting, ×`HIP_FOLLOW` 0.5 unaimed), capped at `FOLLOW_MAX` 1.5 rad/s, into `controls.followYaw/followPitch`. A new target, or none, gives 0 for that tick. The bearing is in world angles, so only the target's motion relative to you counts, not your own turning. `PadDriver.frame` applies it with `Controls.follow(dt)` only while a stick is pushed past its dead zone, and not while a picker is open. The heal and grenade pickers: `HandsHud.stepPick`/`choosePick` and a `highlight` class; while one is open (`controls.picker`), the D-pad's ↑↓ or a flick of the look stick steps, the look is held still, and letting go of L1/R1 picks. While a raid screen is open (`controls.screenOpen`) the pad only closes it (○ or the touchpad). Prompts show □ as a round, coloured `kbd.pad`. **Menu cursor (CP-2):** `input/pad-cursor.ts` (`padCursor`, one per page, started from main.ts when a pad connects) draws `.pad-cursor` and `.pad-ring` over everything and works the page with synthetic events at its spot (`pointerType: 'mouse'`, pointer id 31, untrusted, so `inputMode` ignores them): ✕ sends pointerdown, then pointerup and a click on what both ends share (focusing what it pressed, as a mouse does); □ two taps at once (the inventory's double-tap); △ a `contextmenu`, or while dragging a pointerdown with id 32 (drag.ts's second finger, which turns the item); ○ a pointercancel mid-drag, else Esc (as a keydown at the focused element). Range inputs are set through the native value setter with `input` and `change` events, so React sees them. Targets come from one selector (buttons, links, inputs, labels holding a radio or checkbox, tabs, `.inv-item`/`.inv-slot`/`.inv-grid`/`.inv-ground`, `[data-pad]`), each clipped to what scrolls it and kept only if a hit test at its middle lands in it (so what's under a dialog or scrolled away isn't reachable). The geometry is pure in `input/pad-nav.ts`. Ownership: `PadDriver` calls `padCursor.claim()` each frame you play and `padCursor.feed(frame, dt, { close })` while `Controls.uiOpen`; with neither for 100 ms (paused: the raid loop stops; or no raid) the cursor reads the pad itself, and a read after a gap of 150 ms or more drops that frame's presses, as `PadDriver.read` does after a pause (so the ○ that closed settings doesn't crouch). **Sound:** `app/audio.ts` has `unlockAudio()`, which the pad calls on taking the mode; Chrome doesn't count pad input as a user activation (`navigator.userActivation.hasBeenActive` stayed false in headless Chrome with only pad presses), so if sound is still locked 300 ms later the raid shows "No sound yet: click or press a key once". Usually the click that started the raid has already unlocked it. **Rumble (CP-3):** `input/rumble.ts` is pure: `firePulse(kickUp)`, `hitPulse(amount)` and `blastPulse(distance, radius)` fill a reused `Pulse` (strong, weak, ms), and `Rumbler.play` sends a `dual-rumble` through `Gamepad.vibrationActuator` (skipped where missing; one reused params object; a newer effect preempts the older, whose rejected promise is swallowed) unless a stronger pulse is still running. `PadRumble` (`input/pad-rumble.ts`) checks gamepad mode and `settings.rumble`, and finds the pad with `firstPad`; the play screen calls it from its event loop for your own shots (pellet 0, sized by the held gun's `recoil.up`), damage to you, and explosions while you're alive. **Labels:** `input/pad-labels.ts` holds `PAD_GLYPHS` and `padLayout(settings)`, the rows the settings' Controller section draws (`.pad-layout`, its section spanning both columns while shown); keep it in step with the driver and cursor. The hand slots carry `data-pad` and CSS swaps the number for it in gamepad mode. **Diagnostics (CP-4):** `input/pad-diagnostics.ts` and the `?padtest` / Settings → Test controller panel (`input/pad-test.ts`) show what the browser reports and why a pad isn't used; `inputMode.changes` logs each mode switch with its cause. `settings.inputPreference: 'controller'` stops keys and the mouse taking the mode while `firstPad` finds a pad (touch still can).
- **Pistols out of the primary slots (2026-10-30):** `fitsSlot` refuses a pistol in `primary1`/`primary2`, which covers the sim, the raid screen's drop targets and the server's checks. Saves from before can have one there, and moving it needs the items, so it isn't a profile format migration: `settleGuns` (shared) moves it to an empty sidearm, else onto the first stash page with room. The server runs it in `parseProfile` and on a raid result before `checkGear`.
- **The vault shrank to 2×2 (2026-11-06, the user's call):** `GEAR.vault` was 2×3. Like the pistols above, fixing old saves needs the items, so it isn't a profile format migration: `settleVault` (shared) shrinks a 2×3 vault, moving anything past the new edge to a free spot in the vault, else onto the first stash page with room. It only touches the exact old size, so a client can't send a bigger vault and have the extra moved into its stash. The server runs it next to `settleGuns`, in `parseProfile` and on a raid result.
- **Items that change size (2026-11-06, when the MP-133 went from 4×1 to 4×2):** `settleFits` (shared) re-fits every grid of a stored profile: stash pages and the bags on them first, then the rig, backpack, pockets and vault. An item that's past its grid's edge, across its sections or over an item before it moves to a free spot in the same grid, else onto the first stash page with room; one with nowhere to go stays and `checkGear` refuses it, as before. `reshape` (vault and bags) uses the same `refit`. The server runs it in `parseProfile`, and on a raid result **only after extracting**: after a death only the vault is kept, and re-fitting an overstuffed vault would move the extra into the stash. Grids holding an item id that doesn't exist are skipped, so a forged result still gets `checkGear`'s error. So a size change in content no longer locks anyone out; the item editor's warning says things will move.
- **The vault as an item (2026-11-06, the user's call):** `content/items/small-vault.json` is a container with `slot: "vault"` (a third value of the container slot), and `VAULT_ITEM` (`sim/gear.ts`) names it. `Gear.vault` is still a plain grid, `GEAR.vault` in size (a content test keeps the item's grid the same), so nothing that reads the vault changed. The item is only drawn (`VaultSlot` in `ui/raid-screen.tsx`, with no ref, so no drag, details, selling or taking off). `canHold` refuses a vault-slot item in any grid, so it can't turn up in a stash, a bag or a crate even if content hands one out, and the admin page leaves it off its list. A bigger vault one day would mean storing which one a player has.
- **Grid sections (2026-11-06, the user's call):** a container's item file can list `sections` (`{ "at": [x, y], "size": [w, h] }`, inside `grid`, not overlapping; optional, so items stayed at format 10). `createItem` copies them onto the bag's `Grid.sections` as `{x, y, w, h}`, and `inGrid` (used by `isFree`, so `findSpot`, `addItem`, drops and swaps all follow) wants an item wholly inside one. A grid without `sections` is one section, as before; pockets, the vault, stash pages and loot containers have none. **Item positions stay in the one grid's coordinates**, so refs, actions, saves and co-op sync didn't change. The profile's grid shape accepts `sections`, and `checkGear` wants a bag's to match its def. `settleBags` (shared; the server runs it with `settleGuns` in `parseProfile` and on raid results) gives every worn or stashed bag its def's shape whenever it differs, through the same `reshape` as `settleVault`, so changing a bag's grid or sections in content no longer breaks saved profiles (the item editor's warning now says things will move instead). **Drawing** (`GridView` in `ui/raid-screen.tsx`): a sectioned grid is a `.inv-sections` box of absolutely placed `.inv-grid` boxes, one per section, `SECTION_GAP` (6 px) apart; each is its own drop target, with `data-at` holding its top-left cell, which `drag.ts` adds to the cell under the pointer. The gaps make a row with more sections slightly wider than one with fewer. The item editor shows how many sections a bag has but can't edit them yet: they're edited in the JSON.
- **Settings:** stored locally first (`localStorage`, key `mes.settings`) and synced to the account (session 12b, see Client → Settings sync). The schema is in `shared` (`settings.ts`), versioned like content files, and every field falls back to its default on its own, so a bad value never wipes the rest. `settings.ts` in the client is the store: changes save and apply straight away.
- **Pausing** (`app/pause.ts`): the game pauses for a reason (portrait, a menu, the page hidden). While paused the frame loop stops, every touch is let go, and the time away doesn't count as a frame. Co-op will need a different rule: the raid can't stop.
- **PWA:** web app manifest (`public/manifest.webmanifest`, landscape, standalone) and Apple meta tags, plus the Safari workarounds in design.md. The icons are placeholders drawn by a one-off script. **No service worker:** iOS doesn't need one to add to the home screen, and session 12b decided against one (see Client → Saving raids).
- **Audio:** Web Audio API, unlocked on the first tap (`app/audio.ts`: the first `touchend`, `pointerup` or key creates the context and plays a silent sample; later taps resume it after iOS suspends it). **Sounds are generated** with Web Audio (the user's call, 2026-10-30), not recorded samples (session 19).
  - **Sound files** (`content/sounds/<id>.json`, format 1, `packages/shared/src/content/sounds.ts`): `gain`, `range` (metres heard to), `pitchJitter` and `layers`, each `noise` (`colour` white, pink or brown) or `tone` (`wave` sine, triangle, square or saw, `freq` [from, to]), with `at`, `attack`, `hold`, `decay`, `curve`, `gain`, optional `drive` (tanh soft clipping), `filter` (lowpass, highpass or bandpass, `freq` [from, to], `q`) and `sweep` (how long the glides take).
  - **`audio/synth.ts`** renders a recipe to samples in plain TypeScript (seeded noise, a phase-accumulator oscillator, an RBJ biquad updated every 32 samples while gliding), not Web Audio nodes: the same in tests as on the phone, and done once per sound, so playing costs a buffer source. A sound peaking over 1 is turned down to fit; a content test keeps every sound under 1.5.
  - **`audio/sounds.ts`** (`SoundPlayer`, one for the game from `sounds()`, at the settings' volume): renders each sound the first time it plays (`prepare()` renders all at raid start, once audio is unlocked), then plays it as **buffer source → lowpass (only if muffled) → stereo panner (only if off centre) → gain → master**. 16 voices; when full, the quietest stops for a louder one; the same sound again within 20 ms is dropped. **`audio/spatial.ts`** (`place`) works out gain, pan and cutoff from the listener: (1 − d/range)² × 40/(40 + d), cutoff from 20 kHz to 700 Hz across the range, duller and quieter from behind, and 600 Hz and ×0.45 through a wall. Cheaper than `PannerNode`'s HRTF, and testable.
  - **`audio/cues.ts`** (`SoundCues`): after each tick, the sim's events and what it doesn't report become sounds: footsteps by distance walked on the ground (a step every 1.1 + 0.1 × speed metres), landing, ladder rungs, a reload or med starting, drawing, the extraction countdown and the two-minute warning. The listener is the player's eye and the controls' yaw. Each positional sound casts one ray (`CollisionWorld.raycast`) to decide if it's muffled. **The sim didn't change.**
  - Menus: a click on a button in `#ui` ticks (`main.ts`); `Stash.post` (buying, selling, pages, quests) plays money on success and a refusal on failure.
- **Item details (2026-10-30, design.md → Inventory and UI):** `ui/item-details.tsx` is the card; the inventory opens it for `InventoryController.selected` (a tap selects after `DOUBLE_TAP_MS`, unless a double-tap, a drag or a drop came first; `inspect` opens at once for right-click), and the vendors page for an offer (a `createItem` of it). The picture is `render/item-portrait.ts`: the item's model (a gun via `gunModel`, with its attachments) drawn once by **one small offscreen `WebGLRenderer`, made on first use and kept**, into a PNG data URL cached by item id (`gunKey` for guns). No WebGL, no picture: the card still opens. The price is `sellParts` (shared `vendors.ts`), which `sellPrice` now sums, so the breakdown can't disagree with what selling pays; `questsFor` (shared `quests.ts`) finds the quests whose hand-over or extract objectives name an item. `InventoryContext.quests` carries the quests in a raid and in the stash.
